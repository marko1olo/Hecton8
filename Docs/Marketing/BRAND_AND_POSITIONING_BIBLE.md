## Boundary

This is a marketing control document, not proof that the game already has a public page, trailer, demo, stable frame rate, final art direction, press kit, or outreach permission.

Before public use, every claim must be backed by one of:

- real in-game screenshot;
- real gameplay clip;
- current Steam page;
- current demo build;
- profiler/performance artifact;
- current platform rule;
- creator-specific public contact route.

## The Core Position

HECTON-8 is a single-player-first NASA-punk / deep-sea noir survival game about pressure, salvage, machinery, and staying alive below the light.

The enemy is not "Subnautica 2". The enemy is the ocean, pressure, failing machines, bad visibility, resource tension, and the feeling that the player is operating a fragile industrial system in a place humans should not be.

## One-Line Public Pitch

HECTON-8 is a single-player-first underwater survival game where pressure, machinery, salvage, and black-water exploration decide whether you make it back alive.

## Short Public Pitch

HECTON-8 is a NASA-punk / deep-sea noir survival game about building, repairing, and operating fragile systems under hostile depth. It is built around pressure, oxygen, power, salvage, heavy machinery, and the mystery of a Seed Ship anomaly beneath the light.

## Long Public Pitch

HECTON-8 is a single-player-first underwater survival game set in a hostile industrial abyss. The player survives through salvage, pressure-rated equipment, machinery, base systems, careful route planning, and hard choices under limited visibility.

The visual identity is not bright alien wonder. It is salt, corrosion, floodlights, scratched glass, crushed metal, emergency lamps, black water, and machines that sound like they are one fault away from failing. The long-term hook is the Seed Ship: a deep anomaly that changes instruments, habitats, routes, fauna, and the player's sense of safety.

## Positioning Pillars

| Pillar | Meaning | Public proof required | Forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure is the antagonist | Depth should feel like a constant system cost. | UI warning clip, base leak clip, vehicle strain clip. | Saying "realistic pressure sim" without proof. |
| Machines are verbs | Pumps, doors, tools, vehicles, power, and floodlights should look used and necessary. | Clip where a machine changes the outcome. | Decorative sci-fi panels that do nothing. |
| The base is a pressure vessel | A habitat is survival infrastructure, not a cozy house. | Screenshot/clip showing seals, power, damage, oxygen, flooding, or repair. | Cozy-base marketing before mechanics exist. |
| The ocean hides structure | Black water should contain silhouettes, wrecks, cables, routes, and implied infrastructure. | Screenshot with readable foreground/midground/background. | Empty fog with no navigational read. |
| Horror is systemic | Fear comes from being under-equipped, not from random jump scares. | Clip where a warning, sonar, or machine noise creates dread. | Monster thumbnail spam. |
| Salvage has weight | Every expedition should imply cost, route risk, and recovery. | Clip showing planning, extraction, or return. | Generic resource sparkle pickups. |
| Seed Ship is the myth engine | The anomaly should make the world feel corrupted and instrumented. | Visual/audio/UI corruption clip. | Lore wall with no gameplay expression. |

## Differentiation Against The Market

Do not attack other games publicly. Use contrast in internal planning only.

| Market expectation | HECTON-8 answer |
|---|---|
| Bright alien ocean wonder | Industrial black-water pressure and deep-sea noir. |
| Cozy base-as-home | Base-as-machine that keeps the ocean out. |
| Cute scanning loop | Salvage, route risk, instruments, and machinery. |
| Multiplayer promise | Single-player-first isolation and authored tension. |
| Creature zoo | Threat ecology plus environmental risk. |
| Crafting treadmill | Scarcity/bounty curve, route fatigue control, recovery windows. |
| Clean sci-fi plastic | Salt, rust, scratched glass, old warning labels, oil, grime, hard edges. |
| Generic survival horror | Predictable systems that fail loudly before they kill the player. |

## Voice Rules

Use:

- direct, physical language;
- nouns players can picture: pressure, hull, seal, pump, oxygen, floodlight, silt, salvage, sonar;
- short sentences for clips;
- precise caveats when proof is not final.

Avoid:

- "Subnautica killer";
- "better than Subnautica";
- "revolutionary";
- "realistic full ocean simulation";
- "AI-generated world";
- "co-op planned";
- "play with friends";
- "zero-stutter" until performance receipts exist;
- "AAA quality" unless the asset stack can survive comparison.

## Public Copy Patterns

Good:

- "A base is not a home at this depth. It is a machine that keeps saying no to the ocean."
- "Pressure is not a status effect. It is the cost of being here."
- "HECTON-8 is single-player-first. The focus is isolation, machinery, salvage, and hostile depth."
- "The safest room in HECTON-8 is still underwater."

Weak:

- "Explore a beautiful underwater world full of mystery." Generic and too close to existing underwater fantasy.
- "Build the ultimate underwater base." Implies sandbox comfort before proving base survival systems.
- "A Subnautica-inspired survival adventure." Useful only for hidden targeting, not public brand voice.

Dangerous:

- "Zero stutter survival." Performance claim requires hardware, build, profiler, and capture proof.
- "Co-op may come later." Roadmap trap. User has corrected that co-op is not planned.
- "100km multiplayer ocean." Agent hallucination, banned from public marketing.

## Steam Tag Direction

Primary tag candidates, only when supported by store proof:

- Survival;
- Open World Survival Craft;
- Underwater;
- Exploration;
- Atmospheric;
- Singleplayer;
- Base Building;
- Crafting;
- Sci-fi;
- Horror or Survival Horror only if the footage truly sells dread.

Do not over-index on "Horror" if the first screenshots look like exploration. Mis-tagging produces wrong expectations and bad conversion.

## Screenshot Identity Rules

Every first-pack screenshot must answer at least one of these:

- Where am I?
- What keeps me alive?
- What can fail?
- What am I going toward?
- Why is this not a clean sci-fi ocean?
- What is the player doing besides looking?

Reject screenshots that show:

- empty blue/black water with no read;
- clean lab plastic with no pressure/grime;
- UI-only systems with no environmental consequence;
- creature glamour shots with no player stakes;
- base interiors that look cozy before they look functional.

## Clip Identity Rules

A 15-20 second clip must communicate without caption dependency.

Best structures:

1. machine action -> environmental response -> danger reveal;
2. sonar/instrument warning -> player choice -> visible consequence;
3. salvage attempt -> pressure/flood/power cost -> partial recovery;
4. calm descent -> one wrong reading -> retreat.

Bad structures:

- walking/swimming in a pretty hallway for 20 seconds;
- UI menu scrolling;
- lore text without interaction;
- monster scream without system context;
- cinematic camera with no game verb.

## Seed Ship Narrative Hook

Public language should frame Seed Ship as the mystery engine, not as a boss.

Usable:

- "Something below the light is changing instruments, routes, and the rules of survival."
- "The Seed Ship is not just a location. It is the reason the ocean stops behaving like an environment and starts behaving like a system with an agenda."

Avoid:

- "Final boss."
- "Ancient alien evil."
- "Lovecraftian" unless the writing, visuals, and audio truly support it.

## Competitor Safe Language

If asked directly about Subnautica:

"We respect the genre. HECTON-8 is aimed at a different feeling: less colorful wonder, more industrial pressure, machinery, salvage, and single-player isolation."

Do not:

- dunk on Subnautica or Subnautica 2;
- repeat player hate as marketing copy;
- imply legal comparison;
- use their screenshots or assets;
- name them in ads unless doing platform-keyword analysis internally.

## Budget Reality

With only a few thousand USD, the project cannot buy broad awareness. It can buy:

- a strong Steam capsule/key art pass;
- trailer editing polish;
- a small number of paid creator spots after organic proof;
- localized subtitles/copy for highest-fit regions;
- basic tracking tools;
- Steam page visual QA.

The real advantage is agent labor:

- lead mining;
- contact verification;
- pitch personalization;
- community monitoring;
- screenshot scoring;
- caption/thumbnail A/B drafts;
- feedback summarization;
- weekly funnel dashboards.

## Kill Criteria

Stop a marketing direction if:

- three creator batches produce no replies and no constructive feedback;
- screenshots cannot be understood by cold viewers;
- store page click-to-wishlist conversion is weak after capsule/copy revisions;
- posts get comments that the game looks generic or derivative;
- the strongest hook requires explaining planned features that are not visible.
