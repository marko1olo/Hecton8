31|## First Experience Locks
32|
33|- Start: semi-open.
34|- First exit: bright, beautiful, readable, and alien, with something uneasy or threatening in the composition.
35|- First 20 minutes: more spectacular than a simple resource proof route.
36|- The player may die immediately if they ignore oxygen, go too far, or approach an aggressive creature.
37|- The game may give the player real calm time to look around when oxygen and local safety allow it.
38|- Scenic rest is allowed. It is not filler if it helps the player love the world before the descent punishes them.

40|## Visual Locks
41|
42|- Subnautica-level visual and content density is the floor for surface, photic shallows, and medium-depth hero routes.
43|- The target is more detailed, more beautiful, and more premium than that floor.
44|- `GlobalQualityWeight = 0.0` is not an ugly mode.
45|- Surface, coast, exposed rock, ocean skin, sky, Aegir, moons, and photic shallows must remain readable and attractive on every hardware lane.
46|- Cinematic realism is the main visual mode: believable PBR/material truth plus controlled cinematic color, composition, contrast, and atmosphere.
47|- Correct references include Subnautica for water/beauty floor plus SOMA, Alien Isolation, The Expanse, Pacific Drive, and similar references for pressure, machinery, hard-sci-fi texture, and vehicle/shelter vulnerabilit

49|## GlobalQualityWeight Planning Labels
50|
51|Low/Middle/High/Ultra are continuous planning labels on the same `GlobalQualityWeight` curve, not binary switches:
52|
53|- Low: minimum-survival hardware still gets premium composition, readable water/sky/route cues, stable gameplay truth, and no ugly mode.
54|- Middle: expected player lane; must look genuinely good with rich material identity, route density, stable controls, and first-20 spectacle.
55|- High: saved CPU/GPU budget buys richer shaders, lighting, scatter, VFX, animation, haptics, and UI polish around unchanged authority routes.
56|- Ultra: visual overkill, dense presentation, deeper diagnostics, and capture-grade staging without changing gameplay truth, save identity, DTO layout, or hot-path ownership.
57|
58|## Depth And Light Locks
59|
60|- 0-100 m: mostly bright, beautiful, colorful, and readable.
61|- Deep caves may be dim or dark even inside the shallow band.
62|- 200-400 m: progressively more subdued and twilight-like.
63|- 400-500 m and below: true darkness/murk becomes normal, but it must still preserve route structure, silhouettes, evidence, and instruments.
64|- Darkness is not allowed as a method to hide bad terrain, weak textures, empty water, or unfinished celestial art.
65|
66|## Surface And Shallows Content Locks
67|
68|- Shallows are colorful and alien.
69|- Coral and unusual biota are welcome.
70|- Industrial/technogenic remnants should be present in places: old colony traces, wreck fragments, route hardware, pipes, stations, cables, salvage cuts, or other signs that people and machines were here.
71|- Aegir's current texture/color direction is acceptable. Its blue/purple/methane-rich look is not automatically wrong if the texture quality is premium and believable.
72|- Storm behavior remains open for design work. Storms may create danger and temporary darkness, but must not turn normal surface art into permanent mud.
73|
74|## Bioluminescence Lock
75|
76|Bioluminescence can be both beauty and system information.
77|
78|It may:
79|- help navigation;
80|- indicate ecology, contamination, danger, route, or evidence;
81|- attract or repel creatures;
82|- exist as visual wonder in selected safe views.
83|
84|It must not become random glowing noise everywhere.
85|
86|## Base And Safe-Room Locks
87|
88|Bases and safe rooms may be cozy.
89|
90|The core identity remains industrial, pressure-rated, and maintained, but the player is allowed to build or find spaces that feel safe, warm, beautiful, and worth protecting.
91|
92|Subnautica-like base decoration and beauty are allowed, especially after survival infrastructure is solved. Industrial logic stays underneath the comfort.
93|
94|## Survival And Death Locks
95|
96|- Oxygen behaves broadly like Subnautica: the player has a reserve, upgrades it, plans trips around it, and can still die quickly from neglect.
97|- Longer expeditions may use upgraded tanks, hoses/tethers from a ship or base, and other physical oxygen-extension routes.
98|- Early survival focuses on oxygen and pressure first; thermal, gas, contamination, radiation, and complex channel pressure can arrive later.
99|- Death should respawn the player at a base or safe anchor.
100|- Dropped resources are acceptable, similar to Minecraft/Subnautica-style loss.
101|- Core tools should remain with the player after death unless a special scripted event says otherwise.
102|
103|## Inventory And Resource Locks
104|
105|- Inventory should be cell/grid-limited. Existing inventory work should be inspected before redesign.
106|- Resource balance is a design task, not a fixed number here.
107|- Resources must remain physically credible and not become abstract colored currency.
108|
109|## Combat And Creature Locks
110|
111|- Small creatures can be fought and killed with appropriate weapons/tools.
112|- Larger creatures are not simple combat targets. Escape, distraction, avoidance, baiting, route choice, and system knowledge should often be better than fighting.
113|- Killing is allowed, but the game should not become a generic hunting/action game.
114|- Jump scares are allowed when built correctly, with evidence, sound, staging, and systemic follow-through. Random cheap scares remain rejected.
115|
116|## Map, Sonar, And Navigation Locks
117|
118|Map and sonar usability may be closer to Subnautica than to a pure hostile-instrument simulation.
119|
120|They should be useful enough that the player can play, plan, and love exploration. They still should not become an omniscient debug map with perfect hidden truth, creature state, or free objective certainty.
121|
122|## Vehicles And Drones Locks
123|
124|- Submarines and vehicles may feel safer near the surface and early depths.
125|- The deeper the submarine goes, the more unsafe, costly, noisy, fragile, and pressure-exposed it becomes.
126|- Friendly helper drones are not a current design direction.
127|- Underwater drones from the source/ship/old systems can be neutral or hostile.
128|- Deeper drone encounters should trend more hostile or more dangerous.
129|
130|## Narrative Locks
131|
132|- The protagonist may be former Deep Reach and current Marauder.
133|- The opening motive is Marauder work: contract, salvage, debt, payout, dead claim, professional procedure. No family-revenge or missing-relative hook.
134|- Personal weight may emerge through professional recognition, recovered workers, procedures, guilt, and Deep Reach language, not through family melodrama.
135|- Deep Reach stays clean, defensible, legalistic, and lethal through procedure. No cartoon villainy.
136|- Atlas-6 may feel intimate and frightening through misclassification, but it remains damaged repair/classification logic, not a melodramatic villain.
137|- Diaries and logs should vary: dry procedure, human fear, practical notes, corporate language, and emotional fragments all have a place when the source supports them.
138|- False endings remain a design space. They should be real outcomes, not obvious fake fail screens. No single clean moral ending is currently locked.
139|- Replay may preserve knowledge, endings, rumors, contract context, and suspicion. It must not become power-roguelite inheritance.
140|

144|- Translation status must be honest: native-final cannot be claimed without proof.
145|- Public website/wiki content is required and should be spoiler-safe by default, with spoiler/archive lanes where needed.
