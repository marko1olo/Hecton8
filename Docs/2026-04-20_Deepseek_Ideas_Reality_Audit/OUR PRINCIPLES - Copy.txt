## CROSS-CUTTING PATTERNS: THE COMMON THREADS

---

### 1. The Religion of AUP (Absolute Universe Position)

This is the primary "through-line" of the entire project. It appears in the physics, voxel, AI, and scatter mandates throughout.

Common Pattern: We never trust transform.position. It is merely a temporary representation of data for Unity.

Essence: Every coordinate in the game is int64x3 (sector) + float3 (offset).

Why: This allows you alone to build a world thousands of kilometers in size. If an agent writes Vector3 pos without binding it to OriginShift, they have committed a sin. All systems "communicate" in the language of AUP.

---

### 2. The "Double-Buffer & Fence" Pattern

This principle of double data buffering is prescribed in almost every mandate (from AI to Rendering).

Common Pattern: bufferA (Read-only for all systems) and bufferB (Write-only for the current job). At the end of the frame, they swap.

Essence: This eliminates race conditions. The AI makes decisions looking at "yesterday's" (previous frame) physics positions while physics calculates "tomorrow's."

Why: This is the foundation for Burst and Job System. Without this, the game on an i3 will turn into a mess of memory access errors.

---

### 3. Bit Dictatorship (Bitmask over Objects)

The logistics (power grid), AI, and inventory mandates forbid complex state objects.

Common Pattern: Using uint or byte flags for storing states.

Essence:
- In the power grid: bit 0 = active, bit 1 = ruptured.
- In AI: bit 0 = hunting, bit 1 = fleeing.

Why: This allows the processor to handle thousands of entities in a single pass (SIMD). Agents constantly try to slip in Enums or, god forbid, Booleans — punish them for it. We need data density.

---

### 4. Visual Dithering and "Noir Stabilization"

This is a common feature of all graphics mandates (REND-) and interfaces (UI-).

Common Pattern: The prohibition of transparency through Alpha-Blending. Only Dithered Transparency using Blue Noise.

Essence: Transparency kills the MX350. We use dithering (a checkerboard of pixels) which is then "blurred" by fog and TAA.

Why: This creates that very Deep Sea Noir. The picture appears dense and volumetric, while in reality we are saving 70% of GPU resources.

---

### 5. The Principle of "Sensory Echolocation"

Physics and Sound in our mandates are nearly the same thing.

Common Pattern: Every physical event must generate a signal in PhysicsEventBus.

Essence: The sound engine does not "guess" when to play a sound. It subscribes to tensorial changes in the submarine. AI predators do not "see" through walls — they execute RaycastCommand.ScheduleBatch, using the same occlusion layer as the renderer.

Why: This creates Realism-by-Data. The player feels that the world is honest. If they hear a screech — it means the physics engine genuinely registered a bend in the mesh under pressure.

## DEEP PATTERNS: ARCHITECTURAL PILLARS

---

### 1. The "Snapshot & Resolve" Concept (Double Data Buffering)

This is the red thread running through all mandates (AI, Physics, Logistics).

Common Thing: A system never works with "live" data in real time. First, a Snapshot is taken (a state snapshot into a NativeArray), then a Burst job performs the Resolve (computation), and only then is the result written to the Back Buffer.

Why this matters to you: You can modify anything in any system without fear of race conditions. While physics calculates the new position of the submarine, the AI is looking at a stable snapshot from the previous frame. This is the key to determinism.

---

### 2. The Dictatorship of Bitmask (Bit-Packing of States)

Forget bool isBroken, bool isActive. The Logistics, AI, and Scatter mandates require packing into uint or byte.

Common Thing: The state of an object is a set of bits.
bit 0 — alive, bit 1 — in visible range, bit 2 — damaged.

Why this matters to you: This enables instant filtering through bitmasks ((flags & MASK) != 0). This saves the i3's cache and allows you to simulate 10,000 fish where a typical game would stutter at 100.

---

### 3. The Law of "Mathematical Conservation"

This is what makes HECTON-8 realistic. The mandates for Power Grids (LOGI) and Fluids (PHYS-Fluid) require adherence to physical laws in code.

Common Thing: No "magic" numbers. If water flows into a compartment, it is drawn from the external environment. If energy flows through cables, it dissipates according to Ohm's Law.

Why this matters to you: You do not need to manually tune the balance. If you correctly implemented Jacobi Relaxation for network nodes, balance will "equalize" itself by the laws of physics. Mathematics works for you.

---

### 4. The "ALU over Bandwidth" Principle (Computation over Memory)

This is critical for the MX350. The GPU reads textures slowly but computes numbers quickly.

Common Thing: The REND-Noir and REND-URP mandates prescribe: use procedural noise (ALU) rather than pulling textures. Dithering (Blue Noise), caustics, distortions — all of these are formulas, not images.

Why this matters to you: The build will weigh less and the picture will be sharper. We do not fill 2GB of VRAM with garbage — we make shaders "paint" with mathematics.

---

### 5. The Atomicity of "Floating Origin" (AUP-Centricity)

The entire project stands on Absolute Universe Position.

Common Thing: Not a single script has the right to store a Vector3 position for more than one frame without accounting for GlobalFloatingOrigin.Offset.

Why this matters to you: This eliminates the most terrifying bug of large worlds — the "jitter" (trembling) of models at a distance of 5 km from the center. Your world can be infinite, and it will stand absolutely still.

---

### 6. Interface Isolation (SRP — Single Responsibility)

Systems have no direct knowledge of each other.

Common Thing: Communication goes through EventBus or GlobalRegistry. Physics does not invoke sound. Physics throws an ImpactEvent, and the audio system "hears" it.

Why this matters to you: Since you are alone, you will frequently replace systems. Thanks to this isolation, you can completely discard the old sound system and insert a new one without changing a single line in the physics code.

## FOUNDATIONAL PILLARS: THE ARCHITECTURAL BEDROCK

---

### 1. The Death of the "Actor," The Power of the "Buffer" (DOD Sovereignty)

In standard Unity, the "Actor" (GameObject) is the center of the world. In HECTON-8, the "Actor" is garbage.

Common Thing: Everything that has state lives in NativeArray or NativeStream.

Essence: Positions, predator hunger, battery charge, current flow in cables — these are simply indices in data arrays.

Why this matters to you: When you are alone, you cannot debug 1,000 different objects. It is easier for you to debug one array. If the data in the buffer is clean — the entire game works. This allows the Burst compiler to squeeze server-grade performance from an i3, because the data lies flat rather than scattered across the heap.

---

### 2. PCIe Discipline (Hardware over Software)

We do not optimize "code" — we optimize the data bus between CPU and GPU. This is critical for the MX350.

Common Thing: Broad transition from ComputeBuffer.SetData to GraphicsBuffer.LockBufferForWrite.

Essence: We do not ask the GPU to "receive data" when convenient for us. We write directly into the memory region that the GPU sees.

Why this matters to you: This eliminates micro-stutters. Most Unity games stutter not because of graphics, but because the CPU waits for the GPU to confirm receipt of data. HECTON-8 simply streams data into memory continuously.

---

### 3. The Mathematics of "Absolute Zero" (Deterministic Precision)

In the ocean, there is no "approximately." If the mathematics trembles — immersion dies.

Common Thing: Using AUP (Absolute Universe Position) and the 3D Triple Product.

Essence: We emulate 64-bit precision where the engine provides only 32-bit. A Floating Origin shift is not simply a "player teleport" — it is an atomic reassembly of all matrices.

Why this matters to you: Without this, at a distance of 10 km from the spawn point, your voxels will begin to "dance" and the submarine will fall through the ocean floor. These mandates guarantee that the world stands absolutely still, even if you sail to the edge of the world.

---

### 4. The "Double Barrier" Pattern (Double-Buffer & Fence)

This is the only way to make the Job System work without crashes.

Common Thing: FrontBuffer for reading, BackBuffer for writing, plus JobHandle dependencies.

Essence: Every system (AI, Physics, Render) consumes data from the "past frame" and prepares data for the "future." They never intersect at the same moment in time.

Why this matters to you: This gives you free multithreading. While the renderer draws a frame, physics is already computing the next one, and they do not interfere with each other. For a solo developer, this means you can increase the complexity of systems without fear of them "consuming" each other.

---

### 5. Temporal Segregation (The Cadence of Reality)

Not everything in the world must happen simultaneously.

Common Thing: Division into FastTick (60Hz), SlowTick (10Hz), ColdTick (1Hz), and FrostTick (once every few seconds).

Essence: Chunk streaming — ColdTick. AI pathfinding — SlowTick. Player locomotion — FastTick.

Why this matters to you: This is your time budget. If you push everything into Update, the MX350 will burn. By distributing tasks across different "pulses," we create the illusion of a living, complex world at minimum cost.

## MASTER PATTERNS: THE OPERATIONAL SYNTHESIS

---

### 1. The Death of "Smart Objects" (DOD Sovereignty)

All mandates (from AI to Power Grids) prescribe: no logic inside objects.

Pattern: An object (GameObject) is an empty visual shell. All data lives in flat NativeArrays.

For you: This means you can change the behavioral logic of all 5,000 fish in one place (in one Job), without re-clicking 5,000 prefabs. If an agent tries to put state into a MonoBehaviour, they are trying to make you a slave to manual labor. Drive them out.

---

### 2. The Mathematics of "Raw Memory" (Pointer over Property)

The save, voxel, and rendering mandates require direct memory access.

Pattern: We move away from Vector3, Transform.position, and byte[]. We move toward (byte*)ptr + offset and UnsafeUtility.MemCpy.

Why: On the MX350 and a weak i3, data marshaling (copying from C# to C++) is the primary FPS killer. We work in "shared memory," eliminating all intermediaries. This delivers AAA-level speed on office-grade hardware.

---

### 3. Temporal Hierarchy (The Pulse of Reality)

The project implements a strict frequency segregation.

Pattern: FastTick (60Hz — locomotion), SlowTick (10Hz — sensory systems), ColdTick (1Hz — AI director/spawning), FrostTick (once every 5 seconds — streaming/memory).

For you: This is your budget. If the game lags, you simply move a system from Fast to Slow. This is a lever that allows you, alone, to rebalance the load across the entire project in 5 minutes.

---

### 4. The Religion of "Bit-Packing & Masking"

The logistics, AI, and inventory mandates require using bits instead of classes.

Pattern: Instead of bool isBreached, bool isPowered, bool isUnderRepair — one variable: uint32 flags.

Essence: For the processor, checking the state of an entire power grid becomes a single AND operation. This is what allows us to implement "deep" simulation (Kirchhoff's Laws, Fluid Incursion) without performance drops.
