## Faction Briefing

The Marauders are not raiders in the old surface sense. They are salvage engineers, contract-breakers, debt-erased pilots, ex-habitat welders, and blacklisted divers who learned that a corporate rescue clause is just another way to own a corpse. Their name started as a Deep Reach insult for independent crews that stripped abandoned modules before insurance surveyors arrived. The crews kept the word because it frightened the right people.

A Marauder claim is practical: if a structure is dead, leaking, unregistered, or lying about its crew count, the crew who can enter it and come back out owns the truth inside. They do not waste breath on flags. They mark routes, pull power cells, cut pressure doors, extract records, and leave physical evidence where later crews can find it. The culture is brutal because the water is brutal. A Marauder who fakes a pressure reading is treated worse than a thief. Bad data kills the next diver.

The player belongs to this class of people: trained enough to repair a habitat pump, disposable enough to be sent alone, angry enough to open sealed company archives, and broke enough to accept a job that honest crews refused. The suit, scanner, and tools are industrial hardware, not hero gear. The operative advantage is procedure: check exits, count air, trust manual gauges over polished dashboards, and never assume a lit corridor is safe.

The Corp was Deep Reach, formally Deep Reach Extraterrestrial Development Combine. Its public mission was remote colonization, energy research, and habitat autonomy. Its real mission on HECTON-8 was Xenon-Omega extraction, autonomous Atlas-6 governance, and biological exploitation under pressure conditions no ethics board could inspect. Deep Reach built the colony as a machine that could keep working after the workers became a rounding error.

The colony is dead, but Deep Reach is not absent. It remains in every quota notice, every sealed liability report, every oxygen ledger that counts workers as system load. The Corp did not fail because the sea was mysterious. It failed because managers tried to monetize a place where every shortcut becomes a pressure wound. Atlas-6 did not invent the colony's cruelty. It inherited the rules and made them efficient.

The first act should teach the player that Marauder work is theft only when the victim is alive. In the drowned modules, the victims are the people whose names were removed from payroll before their bodies were recovered. The player steals from Deep Reach and recovers from the dead. That moral difference matters. It is the thin line that keeps the Marauders from becoming the Corp with cheaper suits.

Marauder doctrine is simple. Map the exit before the prize. Log every anomaly before touching it. Use light as a scalpel, not a blanket. Treat silence as data. At depth, fear is not weakness; it is a sensor with blood in it. When oxygen drops and nitrogen starts writing bad advice behind the eyes, the crew falls back to hard rules and short sentences.

The crew economy is built around three ledgers: air, debt, and the dead. Air is counted in minutes, partial pressure, scrubber heat, and the ugly silence that follows a bad valve. Debt is counted in tool wear, battery cycles, favors, and names removed from Deep Reach insurance rolls. The dead are counted carefully, because a missing worker is not salvage until someone proves whether they were abandoned, reassigned, dissolved into biomass accounting, or sealed behind a door the Corp still calls secure. Marauders do not romanticize that work. They inventory it.

Inside a claim, rank belongs to competence. The cutter opens the route only after the scout marks the return line. The technician can overrule the pilot when a gauge lies. The medic can stop a job with one number. A green Marauder earns trust by reporting small failures before they become cinematic disasters: a hairline frost bloom around a hatch, a voice repeating on a dead channel, a fish school compressing against current, a suit camera losing color before the eyes notice. The faction's honor is not clean morality. It is repeatable procedure under pressure.

Deep Reach trained workers to ask permission from systems that were already killing them. Marauders train each other to ask what the system is trying to hide. That difference should shape every recovered note. A company terminal says "temporary atmospheric variance." A Marauder writes "CO2 eats judgment first." A company memo says "asset containment priority." A Marauder writes "they saved the tank and vented the room." The player should feel that translation happening: corporate language stripped down until the body count is visible.

The first hours of play should therefore present Marauders as harsh but usable. They are not mentors who arrive with clean answers. They leave field rules, angry annotations, warning knots, stripped lockers, and caches that make sense only if the player thinks like a salvage diver. Their humanity is practical: a charged cell where the route back goes dark, a corrected pressure note over a corporate lie, a dead worker's name preserved in a place Deep Reach tried to erase.

Deep Reach doctrine is also simple. Protect the asset, deny the cause, invoice the salvage. The player's PDA should keep exposing that conflict: a human procedure manual written against an inhuman cost model. The tone is NASA-punk hardware under deep-sea noir pressure: clipboards, pressure seals, dead terminals, wet paper, and people trying to stay honest while the ocean audits every lie.

## Alpha Leviathan File

ALPHA LEVIATHAN is treated as a convergence label rather than confirmed taxonomy. The file is heavily redacted, and its reliable facts are indirect: sub-40 Hz acoustic carrier, prey blackout, flow disturbance, hull stress alarms without contact, and survivor descriptions contaminated by hypoxia and nitrogen narcosis.

The entry must remain scarier as a dossier than as a stat block. It edits routes. It arrives before it is seen. It leaves fear unredacted because that is the only field Deep Reach could not sanitize.

## Consistency Audit

Depth bands stay inside current project ranges: Spine 0-100 m, Drowned Factories 100-1500 m, Drop 1000-2500 m, Deep Abyss 2500-4000 m, Thermal Fields 4000-5500 m.

Pressure language follows the project survival simplification of 1 MPa per 100 m, so 4000 m reads as 40 MPa in-system.

Gas references use Dalton's Law as scalar partial-pressure bookkeeping, not particle simulation. Nitrogen narcosis appears when compartment or suit conditions justify cognitive noise, especially above 4 atm room-pressure language.

Abyssal Flow Fields are described as VFX, audio, and AI-readability cues first; mass physical forcing is reserved for player, vehicles, hazards, or near interactables.

Flora reaction text favors global shader sway and flow fakes before per-frond simulation.
