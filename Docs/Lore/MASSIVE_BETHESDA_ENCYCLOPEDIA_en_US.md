## CHAPTER 1: WATER (THE ABYSS AND ITS INHABITANTS)

### [Scientific Article] The Brine Lakes of Sector 4
*Author: Dr. Elias Vane, Aegir Institute of Oceanography*
The deep-sea brine lakes of Sector 4 represent one of the most hostile environments on the planet. The salt concentration in these underwater pools exceeds standard ocean salinity by a factor of seven, making the water so dense that submersibles can literally "rest" on its surface without sinking. However, the true danger lies in the chemical composition: the brine is saturated with toxic hydrogen sulfide and methane. Any breach in a hydro-suit's filters in this zone results in fatal poisoning within 14 seconds. Life here isn't just impossible—it is forbidden by physics. Yet, it is precisely in these lakes that the Xenon-Omega substrate aggregates.

## CHAPTER 2: SPACE (ORBITAL LOGISTICS AND CORPORATIONS)

### [Encyclopedia Entry] The Centauri Compact of 2120
*Excerpt from "A History of Corporate Wars"*
The Centauri Compact, signed on the orbital station "Lagrange-Prime," ended the first era of uncontrolled resource extraction. The treaty rigidly demarcated zones of influence: deep space and the asteroid belt fell under the jurisdiction of the United Fleet, while deep-sea ocean worlds like HECTON-8 were leased out to private megacorporations like "Aegir Holdings." This led to a paradox: the orbit above the planet is regulated by the strictest human rights laws, but the moment a drop-pod crosses the terminator line and plunges into the ocean, corporate law supersedes state law. There are no human rights in the water. Only extraction rights.

### [Corporate Memo] Orbital Drop Procedures
*Internal Document, Aegir Holdings Logistics Division*
To all orbital elevator operators:
A reminder that "Styx" class drop-pods are intended strictly for the one-way deployment of expendable supplies and low-tier personnel. When calculating atmospheric entry trajectories, a splashdown deviation of up to 45 nautical miles is permissible. Given the Class 4 storm activity on the surface of HECTON-8, rescue operations for off-course pods are economically unviable. Crews are instructed to deploy emergency buoys and wait for passing submersibles. Using thruster fuel to correct orbit merely to preserve the crew will be deducted from your quarterly bonus.

## CHAPTER 4: SURVIVAL (MARAUDERS, OXYGEN, TOXICITY)

### [Medical Log] Nitrogen Narcosis and Xenon-Omega Toxicity
*Chief Medical Officer, Station "Atlas-Prime"*
The biggest problem with deep-sea dives isn't the pressure; it's the chemistry. Under high pressure, nitrogen becomes a narcotic. We call it "abyssal euphoria." Workers start taking off their helmets, claiming they can breathe water. But Xenon-Omega adds a new layer of hell. Exposure to the unrefined isotope causes microscopic crystallization in the lung tissue. The patient starts coughing up tiny shards of blue glass. It is painful, incurable, and mandates immediate euthanasia under Protocol 11-B.

### [Marauder Guide] How to loot a crushed pod
*Author unknown, found on a corpse*
Rule one: never open the inner hatch immediately. If the pod is crushed, the water inside is at the same pressure as outside. If you loosen the lock, the hatch will shoot out like a cannonball and cut you in half.
Rule two: look for filters. Dead men don't need oxygen. Take the batteries, take the food, but filters come first.
Rule three: if the pod looks intact but it's quiet inside—don't go in. It means they slit each other's throats when the air started running out. The smell inside will drive you insane.
