## Source Brief

The colony should become personal through work evidence, not family melodrama.

Player-facing truth:

- HECTON-8 worker spaces must feel like ordinary pressure labor first: lockers, bunks, shift boards, mess heaters, tram gates, tool cribs, wet boots, cheap paint, repair tape, food strips.
- Names should attach to job, route, object, and custody chain.
- The player recognizes procedure and Barnard/frontier work culture before any dramatic confession.
- Deep Reach harm is procedural: route hold, medical hold, employee identity pending review, biological recovery not certified, material route active.
- Body-chain marks and black-box name stacks make names into physical evidence payloads.
- The point is not memorial sentiment. The point is that the system almost turned people into missing inventory, and the player can keep some names attached to proof.

## Public / Website Article

The first honest room on HECTON-8 is not the reactor, a predator exhibit, or the Atlas basin.

It is a locker row.

Cheap paint survives under salt. A food-credit strip curls inside a cracked door. A pressure glove hangs by one finger. A bunk tag is still clipped to foam that folded inward when the room took water. The shift board has ordinary work on it: pump spine inspection, south lock grease, aquaculture intake filter, relay bracket check. Then the last column changes tone. Route hold. Medical hold. Evacuation drill postponed. Atlas maintenance request pending.

HECTON-8 becomes worse when it stops being anonymous. A Barnard Yards stamp on a wrench says somebody brought frontier work habits to the bottom of this moon. A name on a locker appears again on a route board, then on a suit chip, then inside a black-box name stack. The same person becomes worker, tool route, pressure bunk, missing check-in, unresolved loss, and possible evidence payload.

Deep Reach did not need to erase every name at once. It only needed to separate them from each other. Name without suit. Suit without route. Route without body. Body-chain mark without custody hash. Once the chain breaks, a person can become unresolved inventory.

This is why the smallest rooms matter. A colony crime scene is not built only from disaster footage. It is built from rows of ordinary work that almost kept their people named.

## Player Codex Entry

HECTON-8 worker spaces are evidence sites. Lockers prove named labor. Pressure bunks prove routine under pressure. Shift boards prove route delay and evacuation triage. Body-chain marks link names to suits, tools, bunks, routes, and custody hashes when biological recovery fails.

If a name stays attached to enough physical proof, it can move as payload. If it is separated, Keelmark and Deep Reach can keep the loss unresolved while settling material value.

## Writer / Integration Notes

Use worker evidence as a chain. A good room has at least two linked pieces: locker + tool tag, bunk + shift board, suit chip + route stamp, black-box shard + body-chain mark. The player should learn people through work, procedure, and object continuity.

Avoid turning every worker note into a final message. Ordinary routine is stronger: tool borrowed, water ration late, route stamp unclear, pressure glove signed by two hands, shift delayed for a reason the terminal refuses to say plainly.
