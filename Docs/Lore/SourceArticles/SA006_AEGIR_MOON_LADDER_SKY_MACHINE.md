## Source Brief

The Aegir sky is a machine the player lives under. It is not decorative astronomy and not permanent darkness.

Working player-facing moon ladder:

- H1 Skarn: inner charged-particle and radiation shear.
- H2 Vela: relay masking and ice/dust scatter.
- H3 Claw: dirty ice scatter and salvage rumor lanes.
- H4 Lumen: old public route charts and cleaner archive timing.
- H5 Thorne: failed brine claim and quarantine rumors.
- H6 Anvil: industrial salvage and metal claim logic.
- H7 Kestrel: relay shadow, eclipse timing, carrier-window teeth.
- H8 HECTON: the ocean moon, Deep Reach claim wound, Atlas-6 site.
- H9 Mute: outer dead beacon, cold custody ghost, stale route solver.

The point is not to pull the game away from HECTON-8. The point is to make the sky operational: relay windows, tide timing, eclipse dimming, acoustic packet queues, storm coupling, carrier mass windows, and the feeling that the player is one body inside a much larger system of moving obligations.

## Public / Website Article

On HECTON-8, the sky is not empty. It is scheduled.

Aegir hangs behind the moon system as gravity, radiation, magnetic noise, reflected light, and legal geometry. Its moons are not a postcard lineup. They are moving reasons why a signal breaks, a tide climbs, a carrier waits, or a route clerk refuses to certify a pickup.

The public ladder names nine bodies worth timing: Skarn, Vela, Claw, Lumen, Thorne, Anvil, Kestrel, HECTON, and Mute. Some are hard hazards. Some are old infrastructure. Some are useful because their stale tables still sit inside route solvers. Kestrel matters most to the first survival read: when it crosses the useful Aegir lane, the relay does not simply fade. It breaks into teeth: bright edges, noisy slots, stale returns, and short bursts with enough checksum to prove someone listened but not enough continuity to approve a lift.

HECTON itself is H8 in that ladder, not the whole system. Its ocean takes Aegir's pull as tide, crust flex, vent rhythm, silt movement, and pressure weather. The surface can be bright. The shallows can be beautiful. The danger is that beauty belongs to a clock the player did not build.

This is why route notes talk like weather and debt at once. `Kestrel shadow closing`. `Vela scatter high`. `Mute beacon stale`. These are not flavor lines. They are the local sky deciding whether a packet, a body, or a sealed box can move.
