## Public

The player is not from Earth. Their working origin is Barnard Yards or a connected frontier salvage belt: a place where old ships are stripped, pressure hardware is rebuilt, claim contracts are traded, and dead infrastructure is treated as a job category.

This makes the HECTON-8 contract dangerous, but not incomprehensible. The player knows what a dead claim is. They know why a company pays someone else to enter one.

## Marauder Field Note

Barnard teaches the first rule early: a machine can be dead and still owe money.

Every hull has a creditor. Every pressure seal has a history. Every clean contract has a missing page. You learn to read tool wear, not promises.

## Writer Notes

This origin avoids the Earth-tourist problem.

The player should not arrive as a naive outsider who needs the frontier explained. They understand salvage, debt, hulls, bad carriers, and pressure gear. What they do not understand is HECTON-8's specific crime.

Good implications:

- player competence feels earned;
- Marauder culture is normal to the player;
- Deep Reach contracts feel familiar but worse;
- the professional motive can become personal without needing a melodramatic pre-existing family tie.
