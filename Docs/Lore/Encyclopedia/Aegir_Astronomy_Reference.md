## Real Anchor

Working real anchor: Epsilon Eridani / Ran and Epsilon Eridani b / AEgir.

Source facts used as external anchor:

- NASA lists Epsilon Eridani b as a gas giant orbiting a K-type star, with mass 0.66 Jupiter, estimated radius 1.25 Jupiter, orbital radius 3.53 AU, orbital period 7.3 years, eccentricity 0.07, discovery by radial velocity in 2000.
- NASA SOFIA material describes Epsilon Eridani as about 10.5 light-years away and a nearby planetary system with debris architecture useful as a young-solar-system analogue.
- IAU official naming uses `AEgir` for Epsilon Eridani b and notes the typographic distinction from `Aegir`.

## Project Translation

The project may write `Aegir` in fiction for readability. Internally, remember that the real IAU spelling is `AEgir`.

HECTON-8 remains fictional. The real system does not provide a known ocean moon. The moon system is invented inside a physically plausible gas-giant satellite architecture.

## Derived Production Logic

A K-star giant at 3.53 AU gets dim but usable stellar light. The player-facing darkness should still come from ocean depth, silt, storms, eclipses, and industrial failure, not from a starless or brown-dwarf system.

A 0.66 Jupiter-mass gas giant can plausibly host a large satellite system. A conservative stable prograde moon region sits well inside the planet's Hill sphere. For production, place major moons within a few million kilometers and keep the outer captured bodies as navigation/debris/smuggler context.

Do not simulate this live. Use authored orbit/window tables and staged celestial presentation.
