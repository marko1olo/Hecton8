## Starting Motive

The player starts with professional interest:

- contract;
- salvage;
- debt;
- rare claim;
- payout;
- reputation.

They are not a tourist, chosen-one hero, clean corporate employee, or noble rescuer.

## Personalization Path

The motive becomes personal through evidence, not opening monologue.

Stage 1 - Work:
The player arrives to do a bad job well.

Stage 2 - Names:
Recovered worker IDs, payroll gaps, body ledgers, and suit records show that Deep Reach erased people procedurally.

Stage 3 - Barnard Link:
One or more names connect to Barnard Yards or frontier salvage culture: old shipbreakers, pressure technicians, claim workers, or debt-chain families. The player is not related to everyone, but this is their world.

Stage 4 - Contract Trap:
Later evidence shows the contract was shaped around the player's background, debt status, or skill profile. They were not randomly hired. They were the right kind of disposable.

Stage 5 - Choice:
The player must decide whether to remain a contractor, become a witness, sell the crime scene, preserve Atlas, or break the recovery chain.

## Rejected Baseline

Do not make the player openly ex-Deep Reach from the start unless a later control pass reverses this.

Reason:
It shrinks the world and makes the mystery too personal too early.

## Marauder Field Note

You do not need to know the dead to owe them accuracy.

But it gets worse when one of them knew your home port.

## Writer Notes

The best arc is:

professional interest -> procedural disgust -> local human link -> manipulated contract -> final agency.

This supports tens of hours because the player earns personal stakes by exploring.
