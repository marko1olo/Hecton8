## Source Brief

A black box name stack is a pressure-rated recorder payload that keeps names together after ordinary records fail. It is not a memorial by design. It is closer to a last surviving manifest: worker identifiers, partial job cards, body-chain links, local timestamps, custody hashes, and recorder confidence states packed into a thing that can be carried.

HECTON-8 makes the stack matter because distance and law can turn people into missing inventory. A name without custody can be delayed, disputed, sold as claim noise, or buried under equipment accounting. A name inside an intact stack can travel. It can enter a Luyten packet. It can become leverage against Deep Reach. It can be paid out badly, published cleanly, hidden, damaged, or used as proof that the site was not only metal.

The stack is heavy in the wrong way. It is not a weapon. It is not a keycard. It is a piece of human consequence that the player chooses to keep carrying when salvage weight, oxygen, route risk, and corporate pressure all argue for leaving it behind.

## Public / Website Article

Remote colonies do not trust one record. They layer names.

A worker may have a bunk tag, a suit chip, a tool certification, a medical lock entry, a shift-board route, and a payroll identity. In a normal site, those records agree often enough for nobody to care. In a drowned site, agreement becomes survival. A flood strips tags from suits. A pressure door cuts a route in half. A company inventory system keeps the equipment number and loses the person attached to it.

The black box name stack exists for the point where the ordinary layers stop agreeing. It gathers identity fragments into a physical recorder bundle and marks their confidence. One name may be complete. Another may be only a family name and a tool route. Another may be a locker plate attached to a body-chain mark with no body. The stack does not pretend those are equal. It preserves the difference.

That difference is why it matters. Deep Reach can dispute a story. It can bury a damaged terminal. It can call a missing worker an unresolved local loss. A carried stack is harder. It is evidence with mass. It asks to be transported, copied, sold, protected, or destroyed. It makes the player feel the cost of names as cargo.

For public readers, the black box name stack explains why HECTON-8's endings cannot be measured only in escape distance or resource value. What leaves the ocean decides what the wider human system can be forced to know.

## Spoiler / Writer Truth

The black box name stack is the bridge from exploration evidence to ending consequence. It gives the player a non-abstract payload: names as physical burden, not quest text. It can support proof, leverage, memorial, payout modification, or public release without promising justice.

Use it when the player has already learned enough body-chain logic to understand why partial names matter. Keep the stack physical. It should be found in places where records had to survive pressure, not in clean exposition terminals. The best version feels like a manifest that resisted being converted into salvage.
