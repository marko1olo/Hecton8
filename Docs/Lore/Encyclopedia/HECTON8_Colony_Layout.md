## Public

HECTON-8 was not only a resource site. It was a pressure colony built from ordinary work spaces: locker corridors, bunk rows, shift boards, mess heaters, tram gates, tool cribs, hydroponic break rooms, medlocks and archive stacks.

The layout is ugly because it is practical. Modules were built around pressure seals, thermal mass, oxygen routing, tram spines, wet storage and emergency holds. A player should read the place as a workplace first and a crime scene second.

## Marauder Field Note

The first honest thing down there is a locker.

Not a reactor. Not Atlas. Not Deep Reach legal poison.

A locker: scratched nameplate, food credit strip, cheap paint, tool mark from a yard you recognize. That is when the claim stops being anonymous salvage.

## Deep Reach Internal

Colony-space records should be classified by function, not by casualty association.

Recommended categories:

- recoverable equipment;
- contaminated living infrastructure;
- medical/quarantine liability;
- route-hold artifacts;
- employee identity data pending review;
- Atlas-adjacent process evidence.

Do not publish raw names unless compelled by settlement authority.

## Writer Notes

Use colony layout as the player's human bridge:

- lockers prove named labor;
- pressure bunks prove routine under pressure;
- shift boards prove procedural evacuation delay;
- medical locks prove supplies existed while access failed;
- black-box name stacks make people into physical ending payload.

The colony should not feel like a museum of corpses. It should feel like a worksite where the systems still know what to do, but no longer know who they are doing it for.
