# Interstellar Travel

Status: draft encyclopedia entry.

## Public

Interstellar travel in 2190 is possible, slow, expensive, and infrastructure-bound. It is no longer experimental mythology, but it is still not casual. There is no FTL. There is no instant command link to Sol. Ships cross between stars through launch infrastructure, radiation shielding, long flight plans, braking systems, and legal ownership that often outlives the crew.

Fast probes can arrive ahead of people. Heavy ships take longer, cost more, and must solve the harder problem: stopping at the destination. By 2190, earlier domains and relays already exist, which is why Aegir can be a corporate claim rather than a one-off national miracle.

## Deep Reach Internal

Strategic value belongs to the entity that owns the route, not merely the ship. Beam stations, depots, old survey data, automated carriers, and claim law are all part of the same machine.

## Writer Notes

Use Go2Starss-style propulsion references for flavor:

- microwave / beam-sail probes for early surveys;
- large beam infrastructure for strategic launches;
- braking as a major constraint;
- radiation mass and shielding as ugly practical facts;
- slow domain growth through relay stations and claim systems.

For Aegir at an Epsilon Eridani-like distance, useful simple transit baselines:

- 0.05c: about 210 years per 10.5 ly;
- 0.10c: about 105 years;
- 0.15c: about 70 years;
- 0.20c: about 52.5 years.

These are pre-overhead approximations. Acceleration, braking, staging, launch windows, and politics make the real route uglier.
