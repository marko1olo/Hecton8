## Source Brief

Body-chain marks are not memorial plaques. They are small, ugly evidence objects built for places where people can vanish faster than law can travel.

On HECTON-8 they appear as suit buckle chips, locker plates, bunk identifiers, recovery tabs, cargo-bracket tags, and black-box name fragments. Each mark tries to keep one human fact attached to one physical trail: who this was, what job they were certified to do, where they were last counted, what system handled the record, and whether the body, suit, or name was recovered.

Under Aegir descent-loss priority, body-chain marking becomes one of the few certified actions left. The route may refuse biological recovery, but it still wants countable evidence. Deep Reach can live with that if names remain damaged, separated, or sellable as inventory. The player changes the shape of the record by carrying names intact.

## Public / Website Article

The first time most Marauders see a body-chain mark, it is not on a body. It is on a locker that will not open, a suit buckle under silt, a pressure bunk with the foam collapsed inward, or a cargo bracket where a recovery tab has fused to salt and paint.

That is deliberate. Body-chain systems were designed for remote work sites where clean recovery could fail. A person might leave a bunk, enter a maintenance crawl, vanish in a flood, and never become a recoverable body. The mark keeps the person connected to the chain anyway. It is not kind. It is better than being converted entirely into missing equipment.

A standard mark can carry:

- Worker name or partial name.
- Role, tool certification, or last assigned job.
- Local compartment, route, locker, bunk, suit, or tag origin.
- Recovery state: body, suit, tag, name, or record only.
- Custody hash for Luyten or local claim transfer.
- Conflict state when corporate inventory and human identity disagree.

The marks matter because Deep Reach accounting prefers ambiguity. A missing worker without a clean chain can become unresolved loss, damaged inventory, delayed liability, or a debt adjustment. A recovered name attached to a physical mark is harder to flatten. It can move into a Luyten packet, into a black-box name stack, into a hearing, into an ending payload.

HECTON-8 makes this intimate. The player is not reading a monument. The player is kneeling in a drowned room, deciding whether a strip of corroded ceramic is cargo trash or the last surviving proof that someone had a name before the system priced them.

## Player Codex Entry

This object is a body-chain mark. It links a worker identity or partial identity to a physical trail: suit, bunk, locker, tool route, recovery tab, or terminal record.

Under descent-loss priority, body-chain marking is certified even when biological recovery is not. The system may refuse to promise a rescue, but it still records countable identity evidence. If the mark remains linked to a custody hash, the name can be carried into later evidence packets.

Recovered names are not flavor text. They are leverage against the process that turns a person into missing inventory.

## Spoiler / Writer Truth

Body-chain marks give the player human-scale evidence without forcing cutscenes or exposition dumps. They convert the colony from abstract tragedy into recoverable names, jobs, rooms, routes, and last tasks. They also explain why endings can care about names: a name with custody can become proof, leverage, memorial, payout modifier, or public release payload.

Use these marks to show ordinary labor: a pressure-bunk routine, a tool certification, a worker locker, a medical hold, a route denial. Keep the text specific and physical. Avoid heroic speeches. A good body-chain mark should feel like the system almost erased someone and failed because the player picked up the right piece.
