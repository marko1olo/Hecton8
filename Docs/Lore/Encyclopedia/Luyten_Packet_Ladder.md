## Source Brief

The Luyten Packet Ladder is the part of human civilization that still arrives when no ship can. It is a chain of relays, buffer depots, notary kernels, bonded receiver crews, laser windows, and route ledgers around Luyten Junction. Its job is not rescue. Its job is custody.

A packet enters the ladder with a signed origin, a timestamp, a witness hash, and a route promise. The ladder buffers it, repeats it, compares it against adjacent ledgers, and hands it onward when light geometry and paid priority allow. The packet may be a claim notice, a debt update, a distress digest, a salvage lien, a legal denial, or the kind of evidence no corporation wants to see in a public ledger.

On HECTON-8, this makes isolation uglier. Civilization has not vanished. It is present as stamps, queue positions, fee tables, receipts, silence windows, and proof that a message was received somewhere by someone who still will not come. A Luyten mark can make a death admissible. It cannot warm a suit, lift a hull, or push a carrier through a bad window.

## Public / Website Article

Luyten Junction is often described as a depot, but the name undersells the thing that matters. Ships use Luyten. Corporations rent Luyten. Claims pass through Luyten. Families wait on Luyten receipts until a clerk or machine confirms that a person has become a case number.

The Packet Ladder is the reason.

By 2190, the mature human sphere is still slow. A laser can cross interstellar distance at light speed; a rescue hull cannot. A carrier burn is mass, propellant, schedule, crew risk, port priority, claim law, and debt. A packet is lighter. A packet can be repeated until a receiver catches it. A packet can be notarized by machines nobody loves but everybody has agreed to trust when the alternative is corporate hearsay.

The ladder grew out of Luyten Junction's old function as a custody midpoint between the inner domains and sparse outer claims. It became the place where route ledgers were compared, where salvage rights were marked, where deaths in distant systems could be proved without sending a judge through vacuum. Its relays do not speak with one voice. They are an argument in hardware: Barnard maintenance standards bolted to Tau Ceti accounting, Sol legal syntax, Centauri arbitration kernels, and thousands of smaller claim customs stripped down until they fit in a packet header.

A standard Luyten packet carries:

- Origin authority and local timebase.
- Chain-of-custody witness hash.
- Route class and priority bond.
- Receiver obligation or refusal code.
- Delay disclosure measured against known light geometry.
- Tamper state for every buffer that handled it.

That list is dry until it touches a drowned colony.

A HECTON-8 distress digest can leave a relay housing as evidence before a Marauder can leave the water. Deep Reach can receive a liability notice before it sends hardware. A salvage carrier can inherit a debt marker before its crew has seen the wreck that created it. The player can find a Luyten plate on a cargo bracket and know, with bureaucratic cruelty, that the system did hear something.

The Packet Ladder does not make humanity kinder. It makes lying expensive.

That is why corporations fear intact receipts. A broken beacon is a tragedy. A preserved Luyten hash is a witness.

## Player Codex Entry

The recovered plate is a Luyten packet-custody receipt, not a distress beacon. Its ceramic core held a witness hash, route class, buffer stamp, and receiver obligation code. The corrosion pattern suggests it was mounted to cargo or evidence stock, not personal luggage.

Luyten receipts matter because they survive the gap between signal and ship. A local disaster can become a remote legal object before any rescue mass moves. Deep Reach procedure treats that gap as a business surface: contain the site, control the evidence object, buy time against public custody.

If the hash remains intact, this receipt can prove that a packet left HECTON-8's local chain in a specific state. It does not prove that help was coming. It proves that someone could be forced to admit what they knew.

## Spoiler / Writer Truth

The Packet Ladder is a story tool for delayed civilization. It lets HECTON-8 stay isolated without making the setting empty. Law, debt, corporate pressure, and evidence can move at light speed through known windows; bodies, fuel, repair mass, and rescue authority cannot. This supports the game's central pressure: the player is alone in the ocean, but not outside the human system. The human system can see enough to price the player and still fail to save them.

Use Luyten receipts when the player needs proof that information escaped, when Deep Reach wants custody before truth becomes public, or when a false-success ending pays material value while ignoring people. Do not use the ladder as live chat, instant command, remote rescue authorization, or a miracle route around the carrier window.
