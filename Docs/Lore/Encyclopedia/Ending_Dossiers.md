## Source Brief

An ending dossier is the record the game leaves in the player's hands after the last route closes. It should never be a moral title card with a hidden score underneath. It is a custody document.

The dossier asks hard physical questions. What left the ocean? In whose hold? Under which receipt? Did the names leave with their body-chain marks, or did metal outrun the dead? Did a Luyten packet preserve external custody, or did the carrier keep the evidence inside a negotiable route? Did Keelmark settle material first and defer names? Did Deep Reach receive process knowledge, living sample, blue debt, Atlas signal, or only a warning?

This lets endings be bitter without lying to the player. A profitable exit can be real. A rescue can be incomplete. A public release can be powerful and still leave people unburied. A burned route can prevent exploitation and still trap truth under kilometers of water. The dossier does not tell the player what to feel. It shows what survived paperwork.

## Public / Website Article

HECTON-8 endings are written as dossiers because escape is too small a question.

The player can leave the ocean alive and still fail the dead. The player can carry valuable material and leave proof behind. The player can burn a route, preserve a name stack, sell a sample, publish a custody packet, or enter recovery under a carrier that was never neutral. None of those outcomes fits cleanly into good, bad, or secret.

An ending dossier records the argument in physical terms: payload mass, custody route, recovered names, evidence integrity, material settlement, public receiver, carrier compromise, Atlas signal state, living sample containment, and unresolved loss. The page reads cold because the systems around HECTON-8 read cold. That coldness is the point.

The strongest dossier is not the one with the most cargo. It is the one where the receipt cannot cheaply separate a name from a place, a body-chain mark from a route, or a recorder from the pressure state that proves where it was found.

When the final window opens, the question is not only whether the player escapes. The question is what the human system is forced to acknowledge after the player is gone.

## Player Codex Entry

Ending dossiers are assembled from payload state, custody route, names recovered, evidence integrity, carrier control, and unresolved loss.

The dossier is not a judgment screen. It is a receipt. It records what left HECTON-8, who acknowledged it, what was paid for, what was disputed, what was deferred, and which names were still separated from proof.

A clean launch can still produce a dirty dossier. A poor launch can still carry the one page Deep Reach wanted buried.

## Spoiler / Writer Truth

Ending dossiers are the narrative surface for final payload combinations. They should be assembled from concrete states: self exit, material payload, evidence payload, names payload, Atlas signal, living sample, continuity substrate, burned route, carrier compromise, Deep Reach capture risk, Luyten custody, Keelmark class, and unresolved worker loss.

Do not write endings as a scolding narrator. Write them as documents that expose consequence through custody. The player should be able to see why a route that pays well can be morally filthy, why a route that saves names may be politically weak, and why a route that denies Deep Reach a payload can still be lonely, expensive, and incomplete.

The best dossier has at least one human line and one systems line: a name preserved or lost, and the mechanism that made that outcome possible.
