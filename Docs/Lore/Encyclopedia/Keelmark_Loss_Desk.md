## Source Brief

Keelmark Mutual is not the owner of HECTON-8 and not a rescue authority. Its Loss Desk is worse in a quieter way: it is the place where missing workers, damaged payload, carrier exposure, lien reduction, and public-evidence risk are converted into categories.

The desk decides whether a loss becomes payable, deferred, denied, unresolved, contaminated, locally governed, or routed for outside custody. Those words decide which queue moves. A rescue request can become an accounting entry without anyone saying the word "abandon." A name stack can raise the cost of that conversion. A missing name makes the conversion easier.

Keelmark is useful to Deep Reach because it turns violence into procedure. It is useful to Black Keel because it can reduce or preserve debt. It is dangerous to the player because it can buy the evidence and still bury the consequence.

## Public / Website Article

Every frontier needs somebody to price failure. On paper, Keelmark Mutual prices it after the fact. In practice, its Loss Desk can shape the fact before the wider human system sees it.

Keelmark sits between claim holders, carriers, salvage crews, underwriters, and public receivers. It does not need to own the wreck. It needs to classify the wreck. Missing worker, damaged payload, unbonded burn, contaminated evidence, disputed custody, lien offset, public exposure: each category pushes a different door open and closes another.

That is why the desk matters on HECTON-8. Deep Reach can route a loss through Keelmark before a public ledger receives the same evidence. Black Keel can receive a debt adjustment that makes carrying ugly proof more expensive. A Marauder can be offered a settlement that pays for metal and leaves names unresolved. None of this requires theatrical malice. The machine works because every category sounds reasonable when read alone.

Keelmark's strongest move is separation. Separate a name from a body-chain mark. Separate a custody hash from a location. Separate a recorder stack from the pressure state that proves where it was found. Once the pieces are separate, the desk can price them separately. The human part becomes unresolved. The material part becomes payable.

The countermeasure is dull and heavy: keep the packet whole. Name, place, route, pressure state, custody hash, and physical object together. A complete stack does not guarantee justice. It makes the cheaper lie harder to file.

## Player Codex Entry

This notice is a Keelmark Loss Desk trace. It does not authorize rescue. It classifies loss exposure: missing workers, damaged payload, carrier risk, lien state, custody value, and public-release hazard.

If worker names are damaged or separated from body-chain marks, Keelmark can keep them in unresolved accounting while settling equipment or salvage value. If names remain attached to custody hashes and physical evidence, the category becomes harder to downgrade.

Keelmark is not the hand that drowned the site. It is the hand that writes down what the drowning is allowed to cost.
