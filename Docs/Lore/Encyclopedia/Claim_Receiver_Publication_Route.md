## Source Brief

A claim receiver is the part of human bureaucracy that can make a buried corporate wound harder to keep private. It is not a rescue service. It is not a court. It is not a heroic newsfeed. It is a bonded archive route that can say: this packet arrived, this custody chain was intact, this sender had standing, this claim holder must answer, this casualty appendix cannot be priced as loose salvage without a dispute trail.

That distinction matters for HECTON-8. If the player sends only outrage, the route eats it. If the player sends only metal, the route prices it. If the player sends names without custody, the route may acknowledge grief and file no pressure. If the player sends names, body-chain marks, pressure state, Luyten custody, Keelmark category, and Deep Reach process records together, the receiver has less room to pretend the packet is only noise.

Public acknowledgement is slow pressure. It can create a record Deep Reach cannot quietly erase. It can force delay, embargo, response, redaction, denial, counterclaim, and outside inspection. It cannot make ships burn faster than physics. It cannot make a dead colony whole.

## Public / Website Article

The claim receiver route is why HECTON-8 evidence can matter before a rescue hull ever turns toward Aegir.

Human space in 2190 is fast enough to send packets and slow enough to let people die between acknowledgements. A public receiver is built for that ugly middle ground. It accepts custody-bearing evidence, checks whether the route is bonded, records who became obligated to answer, and decides whether a claim dispute can enter wider records.

Publication does not mean immediate justice. A receiver can acknowledge, embargo, redact, refer, delay, or demand a counterstatement. Deep Reach can still argue contamination, local jurisdiction, salvage trespass, receiver standing, or incomplete custody. Keelmark can still try to settle material first. Black Keel can still make the route expensive.

The player's leverage is not anger. It is completeness. A name attached to a body-chain mark, a pressure state, a recorder stack, a Luyten hash, and a final dossier line is harder to classify as rumor. The route does not save the dead. It makes lying about them cost more desks, more signatures, and more time.

## Player Codex Entry

Public receiver acknowledgement is not rescue authority. It is a custody route.

If the final packet keeps names, body-chain marks, evidence hashes, location, pressure state, and claim context together, the receiver can push the record into a wider dispute archive. If the packet arrives broken, the same receiver may acknowledge receipt while leaving worker loss unresolved.

Public does not mean safe. It means more than one desk has to lie.

## Spoiler / Writer Truth

Claim receiver publication exists to make evidence endings believable without turning them into instant victory. It gives the player's complete dossier a place to land, but it preserves delay, ambiguity, and corporate counterpressure.

Use this route when the player has enough custody to force a response but not enough power to control the response. A receiver can make Deep Reach answer. It can make Keelmark pause a material settlement. It can turn worker names from private grief into claim-bearing public record. It cannot guarantee court, rescue, punishment, survivor recovery, or moral closure.

Keep the prose procedural and tense. The emotional force comes from watching a name survive classification.
