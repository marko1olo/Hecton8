## Surface Texts

**Website article seed:**

A claimant-safe summary is not a neutral short version. It is a protected rewrite. It may remove names, collapse coordinates into a broad zone, replace injury detail with a category, turn a route action into a filing phrase, or replace a receiving office with an office allowed to answer public mail. Those edits can be necessary. They can also be exploited.

HECTON-8 records make the difference visible because raw objects survive beside summary language. A scanner mark may show the real route while the summary says "transferred." A terminal may carry a pressure warning while the summary says "handled." A casualty detail may become "claimant risk." The conflict is not a lore error. It is a custody signal. The question is which omission protected a person and which omission protected an institution.

**Wiki article seed:**

Claimant-safe summary conflict: a mismatch between protected public wording and object-level evidence. Common redactions include name removal, coordinate rounding, injury category replacement, office substitution, route softening, and legal status neutralization. The wiki may describe the conflict type, but protected details remain held until proof gates and spoiler policy permit them.

**PDA / codex entry:**

Codex warning: claimant-safe summary found. Do not assume the summary is false because it omits detail. Do not assume it is honest because it claims safety. Compare what it removes against the object: names, coordinates, injury specifics, receiver labels, route verbs, and payment lines.

**Scanner entry:**

Claimant-safe summary conflict. Public wording does not match object route. Confidence: partial. Omitted fields: identity, coordinate, injury, receiver, or action verb. Required next proof: raw strip, witness hash, terminal stamp, or custody transfer.

**Terminal note:**

CLAIMANT-SAFE SUMMARY CONFLICT

Summary text is safe for public handling.

Raw object may not be safe.

Do not restore protected names or coordinates from guesswork.

Action: preserve omission list, route proof, and claimant risk marker.

**Evidence caption:**

Claimant-safe summary strip. The omission itself is evidence. It may protect a claimant, protect an office, or protect a payment route. It does not resolve final truth.

**Spoiler policy:**
