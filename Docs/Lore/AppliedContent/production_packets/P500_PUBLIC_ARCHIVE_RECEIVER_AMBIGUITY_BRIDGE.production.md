## Surface Texts

**Website article seed:**

Two receiver labels on one HECTON-8 packet do not automatically prove a forged file. Public archives split the receiver problem into several lanes: the office named on the original address band, the relay node that first accepted the packet, the quarantine office that held it, the claimant desk that summarized it, and the legal archive that later filed it under a safer title. Those lanes can disagree without being harmless.

The dangerous mistake is treating the cleanest label as the real one. Deep Reach paperwork often keeps the public-facing receiver narrow while moving the useful packet through a different custody path. Atlas recovery fragments show the same pattern in reverse: a relay receipt may look like ownership even when it only proves transit. The public archive therefore marks receiver conflict as ambiguity, not acquittal and not conviction.

**Wiki article seed:**

Receiver ambiguity is a custody condition where a packet family contains more than one plausible receiving party. Common causes include relay lag, quarantine hold, claimant-safe rewriting, legal archive recategorization, and office-level shielding. The wiki may list the visible labels, but final receiver identity remains held until packet custody, witness hash, scanner gate, and terminal route evidence converge.

**PDA / codex entry:**

Archive note: receiver conflict found. Treat address band, relay receipt, quarantine hold, and claimant summary as separate facts. If the packet names a public office but the custody route points elsewhere, follow the physical route first. A clean receiver field can be a filing convenience. A dirty receiver field can be a delayed relay. Neither is enough alone.

**Scanner entry:**
