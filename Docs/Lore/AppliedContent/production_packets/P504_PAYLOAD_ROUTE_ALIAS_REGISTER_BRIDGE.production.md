## Surface Texts

**Website article seed:**

Payload route aliases appear when the public name of a route cannot carry the raw route without damage. The alias may protect coordinates, living claimants, salvage crews, or a sealed receiver. It may also protect the office that moved the payload. A useful archive keeps both names: the old route mark that touched the object and the safe route label that readers are allowed to search.

Alias registers are not evidence of fiction. They are evidence that a route became dangerous to name. The next question is who benefits from the alias. If the alias hides coordinates, the protection may be legitimate. If it hides a receiver, a payment lane, or a cleanup bid, the alias becomes part of the pressure chain.

**Wiki article seed:**

Payload route alias: a safe or substitute route label used when raw route language exposes coordinates, protected parties, or liability. Alias entries should list old mark, safe label, payload class, redaction reason, and proof required for route restoration. Alias presence does not prove route falsification.
