## Surface Texts

**Website article seed:**

A payload route alias is assigned when the raw route name would expose something the public archive cannot safely publish: coordinates, a living claimant, a sealed receiver, a salvage crew, or a liability lane. The alias is the public handle. The old mark is the line that touched the object. A usable register keeps both, because either one alone can lie by omission.

Do not read an alias as proof that the old route was false. Read it as proof that the route became dangerous to name. If the alias hides coordinates, it may be a real safety measure. If it hides a receiver, payment lane, cleanup bid, or Deep Reach office, the alias belongs to the pressure chain.

**Wiki article seed:**

Payload route alias: a public substitute label for a raw cargo route mark. A complete register keeps the old mark, safe label, payload class, redaction reason, witness hash, and proof required to restore the route name. Alias presence proves a naming conflict, not route falsification.

**PDA / codex entry:**

Alias register found. Do not overwrite the old route mark. Keep the public label and raw mark together until scanner evidence, witness hash, and terminal route note agree on which label belongs to the object.

**Scanner entry:**

PAYLOAD ROUTE ALIAS // Old route mark and safe route label differ. Confidence: partial. Required next proof: witness hash, payload class, terminal note, quarantine stamp, or custody transfer.

**Terminal note:**

Old route mark: retained.
Safe route label: assigned.
Reason: coordinate protection / claimant protection / liability hold / office shielding.
Action: preserve both names until route proof resolves alias.

**Evidence caption:**

Alias register strip. The route was renamed for public handling. This proves a naming conflict; final receiver and route outcome remain unset.
