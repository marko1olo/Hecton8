## English Authority Surface Texts

### External Site / Wiki Article

Title: PressureSeal First Repair

The first useful repair at Shallow Annex P-63 starts in daylight. The shelf outside is bright enough to see FiberKelp moving in the warm current, and the leak inside the annex is ugly enough to prove the module was never as clean as the Deep Reach plate claims.

FiberKelp is not rare salvage. It is a shallow biofiber that grows where light, current and old industrial heat overlap. Cut strands can be washed, combed and pressed into FiberMesh. A P-63 field fabricator can use that mesh as the carrier for a PressureSeal: a soft, temporary pressure boundary for hatch rings, pump collars and service sockets.

The part matters because it is limited. A PressureSeal can slow a return-pocket leak and make a short repair loop survivable. It cannot certify the bathy-drop for ascent, replace a Deep Reach clamp ring, or make Black Keel recovery immediate. It buys time and route safety, not escape.

P-63's contradiction is visible before any archive explains it. The terminal language says "pressure variance within service tolerance." The collar has warped bolts, salt bloom under the inspection strip and fibers trapped in a crack that should have been clean metal. Someone patched around a damaged seal and wrote the room as acceptable.

For the returning Marauder, that is the first familiar lie: a safe word attached to an unsafe object.

### In-Game Codex

Recovered P-63 repair note. FiberKelp from the photic shelf can be worked into FiberMesh and used as the carrier layer for a PressureSeal. The seal is useful for a leaking P-63 collar or damaged bathy-drop boundary, but it is not ascent-rated. Deep Reach marked the collar as nominal; the deformed ring and waterline prove a manual override was ignored.
