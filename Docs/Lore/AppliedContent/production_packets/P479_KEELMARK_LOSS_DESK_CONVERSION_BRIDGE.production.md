## Surface Texts

### External Site / Wiki

Keelmark Loss Desk was the Keelmark Mutual interface that made HECTON-8's missing people fit inside Aegir claim paperwork. It did not decide whether a worker was alive. It decided what the unresolved worker cost the recovery window.

The desk sat between Deep Reach's Aegir shell, Black Keel custody language, and the Aegir Reclamation Pool. A badge without a confirmed body could be held as "pending recovery," "delayed certification," "welfare addendum," "contamination exposure," or "load variance." Each label moved mass, liability, and priority. A pressure-rated sample could gain a custody line before a person behind the same door gained a rescue line.

That is the cruelty of the desk. It does not need a clean murder order. It needs a seal, a window, a tonne entry, and a reason why the body count is not yet a body count.

### In-Game Codex

Recovered Keelmark records show that missing workers were not first handled as rescue tasks. They were routed through a loss-conversion table tied to Black Keel recovery windows. The table weighed unresolved identity, delayed recovery, welfare addenda, sample mass, evidence payload, contamination notes, and available tonne-window.

The starting lien language on Black Keel makes more sense after this file. A 4.8 tonne-window is not only debt. It is an accounting slot. If the slot fills with certified sample mass or evidence payload, a worker record can remain pending until someone pays, proves custody, or forces the packet out of Keelmark control.
