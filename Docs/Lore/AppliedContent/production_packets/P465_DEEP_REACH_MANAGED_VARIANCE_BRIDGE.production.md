### External Site / Wiki Article

Title: Deep Reach Managed Variance

Deep Reach documents rarely say broken.

They say managed variance, nominal tolerance, service tolerance, deferred replacement, continuity hold. The language is clean because it was built to survive audit, relay delay, insurance review, and claimant challenge. A wet room is not written as a wet room. It becomes a local condition inside service threshold. A torn collar is not written as a torn collar. It becomes replacement deferred pending route certification.

The rule on HECTON-8 is simple: read the document, then read the object.

At P-63, the phrase pressure variance can sit beside warped bolts, salt bloom, and a waterline climbing past the inspection strip. On a route board, nominal can sit beside a missing worker stamp. In a Black Keel custody packet, accepted cargo can sit beside contested proof. Aegir delay makes every word easier to file and every physical mark harder to move.

Managed variance is not a confession. It is a warning label for the player. Deep Reach may be telling the truth about a measured value while hiding the cost of keeping that value inside the report. The stronger truth lives in route stamps, tool scars, Black Keel receipt states, black-box fragments, and the places where Deep Reach wrote nominal while the room was already losing the argument.

Atlas-6 and Xenon-Omega remain behind stronger evidence gates. This page names the reading method, not the final cause. Clean language is context. Physical contradiction is proof.

### In-Game Codex

Recovered Deep Reach terminology note. Treat managed variance, nominal, service tolerance, and deferred replacement as evidence leads, not conclusions. Compare each term against the object: waterline, route stamp, collar deformation, worker mark, custody state, and black-box fragment. If the document is clean and the room is wet, preserve both.
