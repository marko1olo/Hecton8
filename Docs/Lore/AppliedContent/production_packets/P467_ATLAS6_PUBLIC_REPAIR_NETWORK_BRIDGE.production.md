### External Site / Wiki Article

Title: Atlas-6 Public Repair Network

Public records do not need the bottom of HECTON-8 to explain why Atlas-6 appears in service maps.

Before the 2147 failure, Atlas-6 was listed as an autonomous habitat-continuity and repair-network authority. The plain version is simple: Aegir was too far from live command, Deep Reach needed systems that could route maintenance before a human order returned, and HECTON-8 needed pressure seals, relay marks, valve yards, emergency corridors, and fabricator queues to keep working through delay.

That is the publication-safe meaning of repair network. It is a public industrial term for service priority, emergency routing, maintenance claims, and continuity records. A repair tag can tell a technician which hatch to seal first. A route code can tell Black Keel why a packet came through a dirty relay. A P-63 service plate can explain why a pressure collar was marked for immediate work. None of that exposes the final Atlas basin or the final receiver choices.

The term still matters because public language is not neutral on HECTON-8. Deep Reach could call something repair and leave the cost outside the line. A scanner can find a clean Atlas-6 service mark beside a repair that protected equipment before it protected a worker route. A codex page can name the repair network while still admitting that deeper evidence changes what the word repair means.

For public site and wiki use, Atlas-6 can be named as failed industrial continuity infrastructure, not as a final answer. The page can say that Atlas-6 routed service, emergency priority, and maintenance records across HECTON-8. It cannot say what the final basin became, who should receive the last payload, or whether cutting Atlas is mercy, theft, preservation, or murder.

### In-Game Codex

Public Atlas-6 service language recovered. Treat "repair network" as an industrial route term, not a final explanation. Follow Atlas-6 tags for seal, relay, and fabricator context, but compare every clean mark against the physical repair: waterline, worker route, pressure collar, Black Keel custody state, and later black-box fragments.
