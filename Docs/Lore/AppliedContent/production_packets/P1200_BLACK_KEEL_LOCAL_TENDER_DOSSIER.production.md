### External Site / Wiki Article

Title: Black Keel Is Not A Home

Black Keel does not wait above HECTON-8 like a rescue ship. Most days it is not above HECTON-8 at all.

It is an Aegir-system claim-tender: a machine for receiving salvage rights, moving certified mass, enforcing custody, and closing contracts when the route geometry allows it. The public registry points to the Aegir Reclamation Pool. The insurance shell points to Keelmark Mutual. The useful truth sits between those names. Black Keel belongs to a process before it belongs to a person.

The player enters HECTON-8 through that process. A bathydrop package gets cleared, billed, fired, and logged. Then the ocean damages the one chain that matters: crush-frame alignment, ascent avionics, high-gain antenna, flotation ring, quarantine state, payload manifest. A carrier can confirm a signal while every physical condition needed for lift is still broken. That contradiction is not a plot trick. It is the shape of the job.

Black Keel can hear you before it can take you.

Its voice is dry because the tender does not need to hate you. It prices the operator signal, opens a claim queue, asks for payload state, and waits for the next legal and orbital condition to line up. Deep Reach pressure can ride inside that language without declaring itself. A sample can become higher priority than a body if the clause is valid. A sealed packet can move first because it weighs less than a person. A quarantine hold can be called safety while it preserves custody for whoever owns the next signature.

That is why Black Keel should never feel like a home base. It is closer to a locked loading dock in orbit, heard through bad weather and old contracts. It keeps the oxygen math honest. It makes the player ask what recovery actually means. A rescue promise would soften the ocean. A claim-tender makes the ocean worse because it proves civilization is still there and still counting.

The clean public version says: receive, review, recover. The HECTON-8 version arrives in colder order: received, billable, ascent absent, quarantine unresolved, tonne-window contested, lift pending.

When the carrier answers, the player has not been saved. The player has been entered into a ledger that now knows they are alive.

### In-Game Codex

Black Keel is a local Aegir claim-tender, not the player's ship and not a core-world rescue craft. It can receive a signal, price a packet, and queue a recovery before it can move a body. Extraction still requires an ascent-capable package, clean relay/carrier geometry, quarantine handshake, accepted payload state, and tonne-window allocation.

Carrier acknowledgement is evidence, not comfort. Preserve timestamp strips, custody notices, and bathydrop status records. They prove who knew the operator was alive, which parts of the return chain failed, and who tried to convert delay into billable custody.
