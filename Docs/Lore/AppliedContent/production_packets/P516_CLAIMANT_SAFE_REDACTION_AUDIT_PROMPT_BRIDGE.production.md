## Surface Texts

**Website article seed:**

Claimant-safe redaction is not a permission slip for silence. A public archive can hide a name and still show what kind of field was removed, whether a witness hash survived, whether the custody route changed, and whether the removal also made an office look cleaner. That is the audit line: protect the person, test the paperwork.

The audit does not reveal the claimant. It asks whether the omission changed the evidence route. If a summary removes a name but keeps the same object mark and witness hash, the redaction may be protective. If it removes a name, drops the hash, changes the receiver field, and pushes the packet into a legal hold, the archive has a contradiction to preserve.

**Wiki article seed:**

Claimant-safe redaction audit: a procedure for reviewing protected omissions without exposing the claimant. The audit can list removed field type, reason code, surviving witness hash, custody-route effect, and next-proof target. It must not reveal claimant identity or final legal result.

**PDA / codex entry:**

PDA audit prompt: do not ask for the protected name first. Ask what field was removed. Check whether witness hash, custody route, object mark, and receiver field changed with it. If protection removed a person but also cleaned an office, keep the contradiction card open.

**Scanner entry:**

Claimant-safe redaction detected. Protected name locked. Audit available. Required next proof: removed field type, witness hash state, custody route state, or receiver-field change.

**Terminal note:**

Witness hash state: required.

Custody route effect: required.

Do not expose claimant to test office liability.
