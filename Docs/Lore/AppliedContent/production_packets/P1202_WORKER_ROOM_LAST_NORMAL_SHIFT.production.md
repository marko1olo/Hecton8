### External Site / Wiki Article

Title: The Last Normal Shift

The first worker room should not read like a memorial. It should read like a place that was still trying to finish a shift.

The locker row gives the first correction. One seal failed inward from outside pressure. The contents stayed in place: a meal tin, a patched suit layer, a tool tag. Nobody emptied it. Nobody staged it for grief. The room became salvage only after it had already been someone's ordinary storage.

Across from it, the pressure bunk carries a habit cut into metal. Air cycles were tallied along the rim with the edge of a service key. The final marks stop before an alarm reset. That matters because panic marks would say one thing and a clean stop says another. The bunk did not fail first. The person using it had a routine, and the routine was interrupted by a system that kept writing procedure after the room stopped being safe.

The shift board is colder. C-12 lists pump spine, south lock, aquaculture intake, heater coil inventory. Two return stamps are missing. The evacuation drill line is not dramatic; it is clipped under a maintenance clearance note and never reissued. That is how Deep Reach makes a worker disappear on paper. Not by erasing the room, but by keeping the board procedural until a person becomes an unresolved route state.

Then the mess heater lowers the scale. A lunch token remains clipped to the rail. Salt crossed the lower seam and killed the coil after the waterline rose. It is not a confession. It is worse in a quieter way: the room was still using heat, credit, and food timing when the water arrived.

The Barnard tool mark gives the player a professional hook without blood melodrama. Cheap alloy. Old pressure-die cut. Not Deep Reach issue. Someone kept a nonstandard tool alive after official supply ran thin. The protagonist may recognize the craft before the object becomes useful. Recognition becomes responsibility because the object proves workers were solving problems with worn tools before the company started classifying them as load.

Read together, the room says this: the colony was not already a tomb when the shift began. It was work. Air was counted. Food was queued. Routes were assigned. Tools were repaired. Then the pressure arrived faster than the procedure could admit, and every normal object became evidence.
