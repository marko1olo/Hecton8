### External Site / Wiki Article

Title: Sol Core Remote Claim Authority

Sol Core can own a claim it cannot reach.

That is the ugly strength of old law. The inner domains built finance, insurance standards, claim certificates, and loss ledgers long before Aegir became a dirty frontier. A Sol Core stamp on a remote claim does not put a ship over HECTON-8. It makes the certificate survive distance, delay, shell ownership, and court review.

Keelmark Mutual uses that standard when it prices a Black Keel window. Aegir claim shells use it when they certify salvage rights before the operator has clean air. Deep Reach can hide inside it when priority language says a sample, coordinate packet, quarantine hold, or insurance rider has better standing than a body still under pressure.

None of this requires Sol Core to be cruel in real time. The cruelty is structural. A paper authority can bind debt, ownership, and witness hashes across a ten-light-year class delay because the route was built for contracts before it was built for rescue. The claim certificate can be valid while the bathy-drop is flooded. The insurance plate can be readable while the operator is cutting through P-63 service metal by hand.

That is why early HECTON-8 evidence keeps turning up as plates, stamps, docket strips, and receiver marks. They are not decoration. They are proof that the old core can still decide who owns the wreck, who pays the lift, and which packet counts as evidence after the carrier leaves.

Sol Core cannot save the player. It can still decide whether the player's proof has legal weight.

It can validate ownership, debt, and evidence custody before any rescue ship could arrive. Preserve claim plates and receiver strips. A broken certificate can turn proof back into claim material.
