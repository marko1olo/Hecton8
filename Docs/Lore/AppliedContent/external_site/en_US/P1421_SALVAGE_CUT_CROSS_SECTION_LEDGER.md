# Salvage Cut Cross-Section Ledger

In HECTON-8, the most honest doorway is often the one nobody designed. A panel cut open after collapse has no corporate promise left in it. It has heat color, torn insulation, gasket slices and the impatient geometry of people who needed something behind the wall.

Start at the outside face and work inward. Painted skin gives orientation. The dark core shows the load-bearing part of the hull. Ceramic dust tells how much heat wandered. Rubber torn into teeth means mechanical force after the cutter. Cable ends tell intent: neat cut for isolation, crushed cut for panic, stripped cut for theft, capped cut for a crew that expected to return.

A good cross-section can carry a whole missing scene. It can show that someone opened a wall after flooding, bypassed a sealed hatch, removed evidence, rescued a trapped worker, or made a passage for later looters. The edge is a witness that cannot remember names, but it remembers tools.
