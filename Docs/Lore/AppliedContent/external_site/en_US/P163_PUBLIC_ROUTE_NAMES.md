# Public Route Names

## Names That Bill

The public routes of HECTON-8 are not meant to be beautiful. They are meant to survive invoices, seals, insurance tables and salvage arguments. A route name is the part of a map that still matters when the picture is gone and only the contract remains.

The Sol-Centauri Charter Spine is the respectable line. It carries old legitimacy, compatibility language and the kind of wording Deep Reach likes to cite when it wants a frontier project to look civilized. Barnard Breaker Run is uglier and more honest: shipbreaking, pressure hardware, debt crews, stripped hulls and the practical culture that makes Marauders possible.

The Luyten Packet Ladder is not a society in the romantic sense. It is custody infrastructure. It tells the player why a message can become proof, why proof can be delayed, and why an honest packet is not the same thing as help. The Tau Public Ledger Lane is where evidence can become public pressure after the light has done its slow work.

Ran Long Claim is the name that belongs closest to HECTON-8. It is not a highway. It is a claim path across distance, law, braking rights and freight cost. When the player sees it on a plate, the correct response is not wonder. It is calculation: whose route is this, what did they move through it, and why did they leave a human body off the return manifest?
