# Paint Chip, Scratch and Abrasion Ledger

The colony still speaks through what the sea failed to erase. Paint is one of the smallest voices in that record. It was cheap compared with a pressure rib, weaker than a gasket and easier to ignore than a broken hatch. That is why it is useful. Deep Reach used paint to make service action legible under water haze and emergency light: off-white for pressure skins, dark primer under hard surfaces, graphite for rails and contact zones, orange for places where a worker should look before cutting, unlatching or cycling a system.

After the Great Tide, those colors stopped being instructions and became chronology. A chip filled with salt is old. A chip with a clean black rim is late. Pale mineral dust over a scratch means the mark was already there when the room breathed wet air. A scratch over mineral dust means a tool crossed the surface after that breathing stopped. Curved arcs around latches preserve the path of repeated access. Short parallel scuffs near a panel lip show where a tool case or cutter foot kept slipping. Wide polished edges tell you something heavy rubbed there under pressure or current, not while the module sat dry in a catalog.

This layer matters because HECTON-8 is full of official surfaces that no longer deserve trust. A terminal can lose power. A label can peel away. A corporate report can compress weeks of work, failure and abandoned repair into one clean accident sentence. Paint cannot explain the whole crime, but it can place a hand in time. It can show that a panel was opened after the supposed evacuation window, that a latch remained in use after flooding, that later salvage cut across worker repair marks, or that a room was entered from the side the map forgot.

The best reading cross-checks paint with other material witnesses. Leak rust and biofilm give wet chronology. Welded seams and rivet rows give repair order. Salvage cross-sections give tool direction and intent. Paint gives contact. It is the layer of choice: where a person reached, where a tool bit, where a crew repeated the same motion until the color went away. That makes it valuable before it becomes salvage. A plate with the right missing color can be shelter, entry, payout material or evidence. The wrong first move turns all four into anonymous scrap.
