# Vent Forge Field Process Guide

A vent forge is not a treasure chest on the seabed. It is a slow reactor built from hot seep flow, brine density, crushed mineral feed, and pressure that does not forgive haste. The useful material is often halfway between natural deposit and ruined industrial batch: ceramic grains grown in mineral throats, sealant precursors baked into cracks, charge-bearing films that only stay stable if cooling happens in the right order.

That makes field handling part of the resource. Pull a fresh lot into cold water and the shell crazes. Vent it too quickly and a clean sample becomes powder. Wait too long and the vent writes a different chemistry over the part you wanted. Salvage crews value vent forges because they make HECTON-8 feel worked by the planet itself: the ocean is not only hiding materials, it is still manufacturing them.
