# Scrubber Cartridge Serial Mismatch

The easiest part to replace is the wrapper. A clean refill sleeve looks useful in a photograph, especially after a service bay has been under brine long enough to dull everything else. The plate on the cartridge body is harder to move without leaving a mark.

Room air on HECTON-8 was administered through serials. A cartridge number tied the physical scrubber, the slot latch, the oxygen ledger, the store issue row and the claim restock note into one narrow chain. When those numbers split, the question is no longer whether the bay looked serviced. The question is which record arrived late.

Brine makes the timing visible. Salt under a latch, a mineral line behind the sleeve lip or a stained throat can sit below the newer paper trail. If the clean sleeve carries one serial and the plate carries another, the replacement may have happened after the room stopped supporting air.

This does not solve the death or name the final desk owner. It leaves a handled chain: plate, latch, sleeve, throat, ledger. Read it with the oxygen ledger cutoff and suit reserve delta before trusting any restock note.
