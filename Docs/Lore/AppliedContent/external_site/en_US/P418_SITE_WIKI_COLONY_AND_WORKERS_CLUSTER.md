# Colony and Workers

The colony and worker lane keeps HECTON-8 from becoming a pretty ruin full of nameless rooms. Deep Reach filed the catastrophe as storm, collapse, failed autonomy, Atlas rescue and lost data. Those words are useful because they compress responsibility. The surviving worker records do the opposite. They keep names attached to decisions.

A pump chief remains visible through a pressure board that did not reset when it should have. A relay hand remains visible in a tray of witness tags pulled before the line went blind. A brine crew leaves its shape in route stamps, ration ledgers and salt-burned gloves. A triage technician appears where a repair tag says who was allowed to spend oxygen on a stranger. A vent-forge operator survives in rejected parts that should have been hidden if the loss report were clean.

This is why the worker cluster rejects the cheap family hook. HECTON-8 does not need the protagonist's secret parent, lost sibling or sentimental photograph to make the colony human. A person becomes legible when a door stayed open because their name was on the pump card, when a shift swap changed who died in the corridor, when a signature moved a quarantine delay from safety into liability.

The Marauder reads people as pressure evidence. Every good dossier should answer a practical question first: what did this worker own, delay, repair, refuse, falsify or protect? Once that answer is clear, the emotion arrives without being begged for.

The colony was not empty before the ocean entered. It was full of routines, grudges, competence, shortcuts, fear and professional pride. The records that remain are not memorial plaques. They are still working documents, and some of them can still accuse.
