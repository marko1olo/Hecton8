# Aegir Sky Weather Ledger

HECTON-8 keeps no weather of its own. Aegir sets it. The gas giant's magnetosphere, its storm bands, the moon ladder crossing in front of it and the geometry of the carrier in orbit decide when a stranded worker can transmit, when a tide card can be trusted, and when an ascent stack is only expensive metal.

The readings run against each other. A calm horizon sharpens the uplink and raises the charged-particle count. A storm band shelters the body and buries the carrier in static. A moon crossing pulls the tide model off by minutes, which is enough to make a shallow hatch or a brine lip read wrong.

Station ephemeris plates do not solve live celestial mechanics. They carry window tables: when a relay lane is clean enough to send evidence, when the tide model is suspect, and when Black Keel can answer. The sky timing desk withholds an ascent-mass estimate until carrier geometry, storm band and quarantine receiver agree, and uplink confidence on the current plate reads 61 percent.
