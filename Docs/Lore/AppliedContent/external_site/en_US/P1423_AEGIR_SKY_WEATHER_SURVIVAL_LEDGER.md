# Aegir Sky Weather Ledger

HECTON-8 uses its sky as a survival system. Aegir is huge enough to give the surface drama, but the important fact is not size. The important fact is that the sky changes what is true for the player. A calm horizon can sharpen uplink and raise radiation. A storm can protect flesh and bury the carrier in static. A moon crossing can pull the tide model away from the door the player planned to use.

This keeps the fiction physical. Rescue is not blocked by a vague plot wall. It is blocked by weather, charge, relay geometry, custody routing, mass allocation, and the fact that a carrier in orbit cannot spend lift opportunity whenever a stranded person wants it. Black Keel may accept evidence during one window and still refuse body recovery until a later one. That difference is the game.

The player should learn to read Aegir the way they read oxygen. The sky tells them when to transmit, when to shelter, when to trust a current, when to mistrust a clean signal, and when an ascent plan is only a promise waiting for geometry.
