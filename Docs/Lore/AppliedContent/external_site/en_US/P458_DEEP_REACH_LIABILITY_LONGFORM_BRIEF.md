# Deep Reach Liability Chain

Deep Reach did not invent the ocean, and HECTON-8 failed under genuine hydrostatic pressure, undeniable tide forces, and catastrophic infrastructural stress. This foundational truth makes the corporation's liability difficult to prosecute. The official incident report cites a "localized geotechnical cascade," "corrupted Atlas telemetry logs," and an "incomplete evacuation due to sudden structural shear." Every phrase contains just enough verifiable truth to survive a tribunal. However, the physical remains of the facility contradict the omissions of the public narrative. Evidence recovered from the drowned shelf reveals margin approvals signed explicitly before the tide window closed. It shows evacuation routes held securely behind executive authorization protocols. Quarantine bulkheads were sealed and certified late. The Atlas continuity weighting algorithms prioritized core system preservation over biological recovery. Furthermore, Black Keel loss ledgers began categorizing personnel as acceptable write-offs while water was still actively breaching the hab-modules. The prevailing accusation is not that Deep Reach manufactured the disaster. It is that they accurately modeled the risk, deliberately delayed human exit to secure assets, sanitized the logs, and entrusted recovery operations to contractors tasked with converting a profound human tragedy into manageable legal custody. Within the sunken corridors of HECTON-8, the deformed metal and locked doors testify far more precisely than any prepared statement.
