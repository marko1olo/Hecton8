# Wet Service Deck Anti-Slip Ledger

A drowned service deck shows the difference between design and use: the intended grid of raised tread, the practical stripe of yellow paint, the human arc where a boot kept turning, and the slow edits the water adds in salt and rust.

Anti-slip plate keeps time through contact. Ridge tops go pale where boots and dragged crates polished them. Troughs darken as oil, coolant and brine settle. Safety paint disappears first on the routes that carried traffic. Rust grows only where hands, boots, carts and water no longer disturb it.

Those marks resolve into decisions in the field: trust the worn line, avoid the glossy trough, preserve a late scuff as proof, or follow yellow paint into a maintenance space that never appeared on a public plan. On deck card SD-6 the seam rust is broken on the airlock side, which records a lock cycle after the 2147 evacuation entry and gives the deck a later date than the room log.
