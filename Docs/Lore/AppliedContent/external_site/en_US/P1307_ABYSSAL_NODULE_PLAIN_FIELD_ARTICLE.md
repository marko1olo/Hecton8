# Abyssal Nodule Plain Field Article

Abyssal nodule plains let HECTON-8 make resources feel geological instead of decorative. A manganese nodule is not a glowing collectible. It is a slow archive: a small body of mineral growth wrapped around a seed, built by chemistry in water that stayed stable long enough to write itself down. When the player finds a plain, the scene should feel both rich and reluctant.

This matters for tone. The game is about a Marauder alone in a deep ocean, not a miner cleaning a checklist. A nodule can be taken, but the surrounding silt tells a larger story. Current lines, dust shadows, empty harvest rings, crawler scars and stained crust all describe what happened before the player arrived. A careful player reads the plain and leaves with resource, route and evidence. A careless player leaves with a box of lumps and a blind white cloud.

For worldbuilding, the nodule plain also shows why Deep Reach failed morally before it failed mechanically. The company saw a patient seabed and translated it into cheap extraction. It priced every lump and discounted the damage done to the sediment record, the acoustic map and the workers who had to operate in the whiteout. The abyss did not fight back like a monster. It simply stopped being readable.
