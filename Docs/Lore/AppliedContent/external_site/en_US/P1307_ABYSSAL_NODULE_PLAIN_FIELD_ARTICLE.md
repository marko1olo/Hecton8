# Abyssal Nodule Plain Field Article

A manganese nodule is a slow archive. It grows in layers around a seed of grit, bone dust, metal flake or vent fallout, in water that stayed chemically stable long enough for the layers to record it. On the HECTON-8 abyssal plain the growths run about fourteen to the square metre at 3,640 metres, embedded in cold clay.

The layers that make a nodule worth lifting are the layers that make it evidence. A clean nodule dates a stable current. A stained one indicates industrial leakage, brine migration or a failed machine upslope. The clay around it holds current lines, dust shadows, empty harvest rings, crawler scars and broken crust.

Deep Reach survey filings from 2146 classify the plains as low-risk recovery and enter the sediment as recoverable loss. That filing prices the clay. It carries no line for the acoustic map written into the clay, which is what draglines destroyed. Extraction converted soft sediment into whiteout inside ten minutes, buried the survey markers, and returned pings off suspended cloud instead of ground. Squares that read as profitable on the ledger became blind water.

Survey cards recovered from square H-19 record four empty harvest rings. The crawler track beside them runs straight until it disappears under silt the crawler raised itself.
