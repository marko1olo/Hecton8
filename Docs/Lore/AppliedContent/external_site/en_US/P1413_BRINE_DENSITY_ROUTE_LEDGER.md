# Brine Density Route Ledger

The Brine Stairs sit between route and trap. They look architectural from a distance: shelves, pale lips, white ledges stacked into the dark. Up close they are moving chemistry. Dense brine lies under normal seawater like a second ocean poured into the cracks. Its boundary can shine. Its crust can wrinkle. Its surface can answer sonar with the confidence of stone while moving under the suit like oil.

That is why the old route boards do not say 'descend here'. They list habits. Ping from the side. Trim twice. Watch temperature falloff. Check for cable scars. Wait for the silt that rises from the boundary, then watch where it refuses to mix. A good crossing is not a line on a chart; it is a behavior that repeats after the ocean has had a chance to lie.

The ledger has human teeth in it. Several evacuation pods were reported missing from floor positions that never existed. Their final pings came from beneath the third density curtain, where a pilot saw a pale shelf, corrected downward and spent the last ballast margin trying to climb out of water heavier than the capsule. Deep Reach filed the losses as navigation error. The route board kept the cable scrapes.

In play, the same rule turns lore into action. The player can ride a brine layer to mask noise, dip a cutter into cold density water, recover preserved tags, or cross a canyon without exposing a light cone. The cost is attention: bad ping angle, stale ballast, damaged seals or panic descent can turn a route into a pit. HECTON-8 does not need a monster to punish arrogance. Sometimes the ocean only has to look flat.
