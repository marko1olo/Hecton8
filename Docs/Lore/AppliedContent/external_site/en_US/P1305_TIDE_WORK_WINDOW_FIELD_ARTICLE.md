# Tide Work Window Field Article

Route offices in Sector 44 did not post weather. They posted windows.

A recovered window board is a printed grid overwritten in chalk, one row per window, three columns that have to agree: slack water, pump drawdown, and carrier shadow. Tide phase on this moon moves hatch strain, silt load, acoustic range, brine-layer drift, suit ballast and pump duty together, so the board treats them as one entry. Row W-114 records twelve minutes of slack, no drawdown, a clear shadow. W-116 records a blackout and nothing else.

The vocabulary is efficient and consequential. Slack window, pump window, lift window, relay window, blackout window. A physical margin recorded as a scheduled slot is what allows a worker who did not return inside one to be filed as late execution rather than as a person left in a failing section. On recovered boards a name moves from cleared to delayed to held in the same chalk.

Windows also run in reverse, which is why salvage crews read them. Storm closes visibility and opens deniability. Slack makes a heavy door honest. A relay shadow keeps a lifted packet out of a legal queue for the length of a window.

Boards recovered from Sector 44 stop in 2147. What closed the last window is still argued at the Keelmark Loss Desk and in the Barnard Yards. The boards record only that it closed.
