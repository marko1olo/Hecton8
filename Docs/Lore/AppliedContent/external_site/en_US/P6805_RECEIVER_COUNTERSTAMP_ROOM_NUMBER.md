# Receiver Counterstamp Room Number

A receiver counterstamp room number is the first mark that makes a blank quarantine berth stop being blank. It looks small: a room code, a receiver strip, a medical owner seal, a timestamp, a body custody class. In HECTON-8 terms it is enormous. It means somebody above the ocean can no longer say the door has no owner.

That does not make the door kind. A room number is a handle, not a pardon. It lets Black Keel aim a body line at a named place, but it also decides who owns the body after the lift, who controls the evidence in the suit, who can delay testimony, and who gets billed when the pressure transition goes wrong.

## From Blank To Owned

The blank berth receipt says the route has heard the Marauder but cannot name a receiver room. A counterstamp changes that state. It writes a room into the field and ties the room to an institution: public quarantine, corporate recovery, medical custody, claim-pool emergency hold, or a mixed receiver with one clean label and several ugly clauses underneath.

The stamp must be read line by line. `Room named` is not the same as `body accepted`. `Body accepted` is not the same as `testimony protected`. `Medical owner present` is not the same as `Deep Reach excluded`. The route loves words that sound close enough to mercy to make tired hands trust them.

## Sample Room, Body Room

The most common trap is the sample room. A receiver may stamp a quarantine room for a sealed canister, pressure glass, suit residue or proof core and leave the living body in a different legal class. On the console both lines can sit beside each other: same carrier pass, same receiver name, different custody rule.

That difference matters. A sample can be boxed, delayed and archived. A living body speaks. A living body carries memory, debt, contamination, injury and proof in the same skin. If the counterstamp names only sample custody, the body line is still exposed even if the berth field no longer looks empty.

## The Good Stamp Is Still Sharp

A useful counterstamp names a room, a medical owner, a body custody class and a receiver liability line. It should make lift allocation possible because Black Keel can point to a door and an owner. It should also make refusal more expensive: if the route ignores the body after that, the refusal becomes visible to the next ledger.

But the stamp is still sharp. A public ledger receiver protects proof by making it harder to erase, but it can expose coordinates. A neutral quarantine room keeps a body alive while suspending speech. A corporate receiver can move fastest and turn the survivor into lawful custody before anyone outside the chain sees the face behind the line.

## Reading The Counterstamp

Treat the room number as evidence, not comfort. Compare its timestamp against the body ledger and the blank berth receipt. Check whether the room class says `body`, `sample`, `witness`, or `mixed exposure`. Check the owner strip. Check whether the lift allocation switch changed because the room accepted the body or because the proof core became valuable enough to drag the body with it.

The counterstamp is progress because it breaks the cleanest delay. It is dangerous because it replaces emptiness with ownership. In Aegir space, the first room number does not answer whether the Marauder is saved. It answers the harder question: who gets to close the door.
