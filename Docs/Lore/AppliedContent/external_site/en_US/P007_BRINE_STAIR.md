# Brine Stair

Brine stairs are density geography. Hypersaline water shed from HECTON-8's evaporite beds does not mix upward; it pools in shelves, curtains and slow bottom rivers with a sharp interface at the top of each layer. Survey convention numbers the interfaces rather than the layers, because the interface is what equipment notices.

Three effects are documented consistently. Sound refracts at the boundary, so a return can arrive from a depth the target does not occupy. Buoyancy changes across the interface faster than a ballast trim can answer, which pins craft against the underside of a layer they cannot see. And because the layers do not mix, they preserve: recovered brine stairs have yielded route markers, pressure caskets, drone hulls, and chemical traces in stratigraphic order, decades after deposition. Aegir Reclamation Pool survey notes list the third effect under recoverable value and the first two under transit hazard, on the same page.
