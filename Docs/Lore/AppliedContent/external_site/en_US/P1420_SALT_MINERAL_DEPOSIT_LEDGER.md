# Salt Mineral Deposit Memory Ledger

The colony tried to keep surfaces clean because clean surfaces looked controlled. Salt refused that lie. It came back after wiping, returned under gasket lips, gathered in screw pockets, and drew pale borders where water had stood long enough to become a record.

A mineral deposit has grammar. Bloom along a lower seam says migration. Downward calcium fingers say drainage. A ring around a bolt says the metal breathed under pressure. Powder trapped under newer oil says the water was there first. Crust broken only on one side says a panel flexed, not that somebody kicked it.

This is the kind of evidence a salvage crew learns to respect. Salt can show which corridor floods first, which hatch should not be trusted, which cave pocket hides brine, and which official “single breach” was really a long conversation between pressure, metal and neglect.
