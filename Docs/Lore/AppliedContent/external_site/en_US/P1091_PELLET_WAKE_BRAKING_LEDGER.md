# Pellet Wake Braking Ledger

HECTON-8 is isolated because travel is heavy. The Aegir route began with pellet-beam scouts: small autonomous probes pushed from Luyten Junction and expected to die usefully. They carried maps, star fixes and legal witness packets. A lost probe was a cost line. A lost colony ship was a generation of debt.

The Atlas-class freight era moved real mass. A carrier crossed with factory cores, cryo bays, drilling systems, route receivers, sealed contracts and the people cheap enough to sign. Its fusion-electric stacks spent years building velocity. Nothing in that architecture resembled a miracle. Every correction burned inventory. Every shield plate was a wager. Every kilogram arrived with a name attached to a ledger.

The braking ledger is the cruel page. A near-light carrier must become slow again before it can deliver anything. Magsails open across impossible spans and pull against charged particles. Pellet lanes bite the edge of the sail. Final passes use atmosphere, storm envelope and gas-giant gravity as a brake. Miss the margin and the ship survives nowhere useful. Cut the margin too fine and the route receives a plume, not cargo.

This is why rescue inside HECTON-8 can feel obscene without breaking physics. Sol can answer after the decision has expired. Deep Reach can inherit a debt faster than it can move a hull. Black Keel can sit in local orbit and sell a recovery window because it is already part of the Aegir machine. The survivor is not outside civilization. They are inside the order in which civilization arrives: paperwork, signal, then mass.
