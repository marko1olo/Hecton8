# Shallow Predator Shadow Rule

Shallow Predator Shadow Rule is a lore and encounter rule, not a species profile. Its job is to protect the tone of the photic shelf: beautiful, legible, alive, and still able to kill you. The predator arrives through witnesses. Glass grazers leave food behind and vanish. Lantern drift bloom keeps glowing but loses its soft particulate chatter. Shell clamp reefs tighten on cables as if the whole route has taken a breath. Only then does a shadow move through the light. This order matters because it makes the ocean feel systemic instead of theatrical. The player is not punished by a monster that materializes from nowhere; the player is pressured by a set of signs they could have learned to read. Good implementation should layer audio, visual and ecological cues so the warning survives different displays, languages and accessibility needs: missing ticks, cut bloom-noise, broad silhouette, displaced wake, route animals moving out of phase. The direct attack is the least interesting part. The stronger moment is when a repair objective is still reachable, the water is still gorgeous, and every living instrument says the route has become expensive.

## Scanner

SHALLOW PREDATOR SHADOW // Missing grazer ticks, torn bloom-noise and a broad moving shade are first contact. Route pressure comes before combat contact.

## Terminal

CONTACT RULE: predator evidence should change route choice before forcing damage. Stage as absence, silence, silhouette and wake; direct attack only after the player ignores or misreads at least two cues.

## Audio

When the little mouths stop ticking, stop moving.

## Field Note

Do not give the predator a face first. Let the shelf go wrong: food remains, water brightens, small life leaves, then the shadow crosses the task.
