# Packet Notary Mask Edge

HECTON-8 uses packet notary marks to keep damaged records useful without giving every secret away at once. A notary mask can be legitimate. It may hide a name, a route coordinate, a payload serial or a quarantined table entry. The problem is not that a line is hidden. The problem is when the hidden line no longer belongs to the same layer as the damaged object.

The edge of the mask carries that answer. If blackout pigment sits under salt bloom and shares the same scratch bed as the carrier, the mask may have travelled with the source. If the pigment rides over salt, bridges a scratch, or clips a route stamp while leaving matching machine damage readable, the document was handled after the carrier took damage. The redaction becomes a late action with an owner.

This evidence belongs beside transcript damage bands, hydrophone log strips and recorder-bay cassettes. The band shows what the readable text admits is missing. The strip shows what the room recorded through water and metal. The cassette teeth show how the carrier moved. The notary mask edge shows who tried to make the published record look cleaner than the object.

Public copy can call the mark a lawful omission. In the field, a Marauder reads the edge first. Salt under pigment and pigment over salt do not tell the same story, and only one of those orders can belong to the room.
