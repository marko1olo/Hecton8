# Cable Jacket Repair Wrap Ledger

Good cable dressing is ugly in a useful way. It is layered with panic, skill and bad lighting. A worker did not wrap a line because the line looked important on a diagram. They wrapped it because something on the other end still mattered: a pump refusing to die, a door that had to cycle once more, a sensor warning that nobody wanted to hear aloud.

HECTON-8 cable wrap reads through pressure and handling. Salt inside a tape seam says the repair is old enough to breathe water. Salt outside a clean clamp says the clamp came later. Fiber exposed under a shallow scrape means recent movement. Deep flattened bands mean a tool or bracket held the cable under load. If the abrasion sits only on the lower side, the line dragged across the deck after being tied above it.

That gives the world a practical grammar. A repaired cable can be loot, but it can also be a route marker, a hazard warning, a proof of late operation or the only honest arrow in a corridor full of dead signage. A Marauder who reads cable wrap does not just find copper. They find intention.
