# Ran Long Claim Transit

Every Aegir route chart begins with a comfort and an insult. The comfort is that HECTON-8 is not beyond humanity. By 2190, Sol, Centauri, Barnard, Tau Ceti, Luyten and the Aegir claim offices share enough clocks, legal templates and packet infrastructure to move people, mass and blame between stars. The insult is the scale. There is no faster-than-light rescue switch hiding behind the map. A packet can cross the gap as light, relay and patient error-correction. A person crosses as cargo, heat, oxygen, paperwork, propellant and risk. The Ran Long Claim made Aegir look close because the first public charts were drawn for accountants. They measured claim continuity, not a human wait. A route could be certified while the recovery carrier was still months from a beam-lane handoff. A court could accept a death dispute before a body bag had left the moon. A maintenance drone could keep stamping local work orders because the next authority packet had not yet crawled through the ladder. Black Keel sits inside that system. It is a claim-tender and salvage carrier with debt lockers, custody seals, spare hands and a clock full of bad windows. It answers because answer time is cheap. It moves when mass, legal exposure and carrier geometry line up. That difference is the first hard lesson of Aegir salvage. The frontier is mature enough to own you, bill you and archive you. It is not dense enough to save you quickly. For a Marauder on HECTON-8, the Ran Long Claim is less a road home than a proof that home has a queue, and the queue already knows your tonnage.
