# Pressure Room Recorder Bays

The pressure room recorder bay is where HECTON-8 turns audio from atmosphere into evidence.

        It is not a sleek black-box unit and it is not a convenient horror recorder. It is a wall-mounted cradle from the old colony: ceramic cassette slot, hydrophone puck, ink wheel, latch glass, service seal, clock ladder and enough salt-stained metal to make every clean audio file look suspicious. The machine was built for rooms where a bad seal could kill a whole shift before the terminal finished asking for confirmation.

        That is why the recorder bay matters. It gives the player a physical source of truth. The recovered sound can be beautiful, frightening or almost unreadable, but the bay decides whether it belongs to the room. Are the cassette teeth worn in the same direction as the strip pull? Did the pressure ink wheel dry out before or after the lock impact? Does the cracked cover match the damage band on the transcript? Did somebody tear the service seal from outside, or did a trapped worker open it with shaking hands?

        Used well, this object lets lore arrive through inspection instead of lectures. A player finds the bay before understanding it. Later they learn to read the dull marks: wheel drag, missing seconds, fresh scratches over old salt, a recorder bay that was opened twice, a cassette swapped into a room that never made that sound. The story becomes stronger because the machine has limits. It cannot explain the colony. It can only prove that one room was lying less than the report.
