# Rubber Gasket Ring Pressure Seal

Rubber gasket rings are small enough to look like detail, which is why they work so well in HECTON-8. A ruined base can lose logos, paint and clean architecture, but a gasket still says what touched what. It shows where a hatch bit down, where water crept through, where a worker over-tightened a clamp, and where a later salvager pried a door that may still have been doing its job.

The key is compression memory. Pressure-rated rubber does not fail like simple decoration. It flattens, swells, tears, powders, polishes and holds salt in different places depending on time and load. A smooth inner lip can mean repeated legal cycling. A ragged outer bite can mean panic entry. Salt deep in the groove means long seepage. Salt only on the face can mean recent exposure. Those are not flavor marks. They are route facts.

In play, gasket rings should make the player slow down near hatches and windows. A black ring can be scrap for a repair, a warning that a dry pocket is still alive, a clue that someone opened the module after the public timeline, or the only thing keeping a pipe socket from becoming a jet. Taking it blindly is cheap survival. Reading it first is HECTON-8 survival.
