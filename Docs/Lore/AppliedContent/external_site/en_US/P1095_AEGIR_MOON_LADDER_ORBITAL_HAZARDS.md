# Aegir Moon Ladder

The Aegir moon ladder is the first hard correction to the player's instinct that rescue only has to beat the ocean. HECTON-8 sits under a busy giant, not under an empty sky. Ran supplies the light, Aegir supplies gravity, radiation, debris weather and transfer timing, and the moons turn the space above the water into a moving lock.

The ladder's names matter because each rung breaks a different kind of certainty. Skarn makes clean electronics intermittent. Vela scatters optical and carrier links. Claw preserves old route logic that machines still obey. Lumen proves packets moved without proving they were handled justly. Thorne carries quarantine suspicion. Anvil leaves cargo law in salvage hardware. Kestrel compresses windows by simply standing in the wrong place. HECTON supplies the water, pressure and storm plume. Mute proves that dead routes can still exist on paper.

Black Keel is therefore not a heroic mother ship. It is a claim-tender inside that geometry. To lift a body it needs signal custody, a clean payload hash, quarantine handshake, guidance confidence and a tonne-window that insurance will accept. A bright sky can be useless. A true signal can arrive too late. The ladder makes isolation physical, legal and astronomical at the same time.
