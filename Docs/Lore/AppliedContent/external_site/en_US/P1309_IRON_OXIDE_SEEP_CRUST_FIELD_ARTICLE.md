# Iron-Oxide Seep Crust Field Article

Iron-oxide seep crust helps HECTON-8 separate believable science from generic rusty ruin art. The red material is not a universal damage decal. It is a readable surface with causes: iron-rich water, microbes, oxygen gradients, old salts, industrial leaks and cleanup chemistry. The same palette can mean life, failure or fraud.

For gameplay, the key is geometry. Colony rust tends to fall from hardware, collect under seams and follow gravity or flow off a source. Native seep crust follows pores, cracks, bubble rings and chemical boundaries in clay. A player who learns that difference can find routes and evidence. A player who scrapes first loses both.

For story, the crust is useful because Deep Reach can hide inside ambiguity. A red patch can be filed as local biology when machinery failed, or as contamination when native evidence blocks a claim. That is not flavor text. It is the exact kind of small bureaucratic lie that makes HECTON-8 feel lived in and abandoned by people who knew what they were doing.
