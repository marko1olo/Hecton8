# Iron-Oxide Seep Crust Field Article

Iron-oxide seep crust is a microbial mineral skin: red-brown at the surface, black-green in the cracks, with a white mineral lip at the growing edge. It forms where iron-bearing water crosses a narrow oxygen boundary in cold clay, and it spreads along pore lines and bubble vents rather than down a seam.

That geometry is how it can be separated from colony corrosion, which bleeds out of hardware and follows gravity. The two can also sit on top of each other, and cleanup solvent leaves a third pattern resembling neither.

Deep Reach filings used the ambiguity in both directions. Corrosion report 44-19 records red crust beside damaged equipment as local biology. Clearance sheet 7-A records native crust across a planned survey line as contamination and authorises removal. Both were signed in 2146. The colour is identical in the attached photographs; only growth direction separates the two cases, and neither file recorded it.
