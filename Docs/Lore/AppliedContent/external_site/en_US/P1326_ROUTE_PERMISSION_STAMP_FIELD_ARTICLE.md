# Route Permission Stamps

The drowned colony did not move people by speeches. It moved them by stamps.

On HECTON-8 a route permission stamp could be a printed square on a shift board, a scarred metal tab beside a hatch, a suit-chip update, a grease-pencil authorization on a cart slate, or a seal pressed into a tool crib tray. It was small because pressure work hates paperwork that cannot survive gloves, water and fatigue. It was powerful because it turned a worker's name into permission: go here, fix this, witness that, return by this route.

After the Great Tide, those little permissions became harder evidence than most surviving logs. Logs can be summarized. Stamps are stubborn. A blank return box proves an unfinished movement. A denied route beside a triage entry proves harm moved through procedure. A BRINE stamp on a worker who should have held a pump room proves someone reassigned risk. A clean late sticker on a corroded hatch proves the record was touched after the room had already changed state.

The important part is that route stamps can also lie. Deep Reach could backfill them, cross them out, or let a stale route remain legally active because admitting closure would count bodies. The physical room tests the stamp. Does the latch wear match repeated authorized use? Does the waterline cut through the ink? Does the suit chip agree? Did a Marauder correction mark warn that the official category was wrong?

For players, route stamps make the colony readable without turning every worker into a monologue. They are procedural, bitter and human at the same time. Follow them and the drowned station becomes less like a maze and more like a workplace that kept assigning tasks after safety had already failed.
