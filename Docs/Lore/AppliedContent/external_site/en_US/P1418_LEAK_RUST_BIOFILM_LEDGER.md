# Leak Rust Biofilm Chronology Ledger

A good leak stain does not look dramatic at first. It looks like dirt. That is why it belongs in HECTON-8. The colony did not leave its truth in clean plaques. It left truth where water lingered, where bolts sweated, where seals breathed and where workers stopped having time to scrub.

The useful question is not "what color is the stain?" The useful question is "which layer is on top?" Rust beneath salt suggests an old wet seam that later dried or pressure-breathed. Salt beneath oil suggests a later machine leak across an already mineralized edge. Biofilm over paint means the surface stayed wet and fed. Clean paint inside a dirty streak can mean a late hand wipe, a removed label or a tool dragged through the slime.

For a Marauder, leak chronology can carry several jobs at once. It can make a path legible, warn about a weak seal, show that a room cycled after official shutdown, or mark a proof object worth preserving instead of grinding into salvage. The best version of this material is quiet: a wall that rewards careful reading with the fact that the ocean did not arrive in one honest wave.
