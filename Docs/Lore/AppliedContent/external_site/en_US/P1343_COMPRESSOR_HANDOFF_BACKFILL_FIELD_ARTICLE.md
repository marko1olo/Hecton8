# Compressor Handoff Backfill

The compressor is a bad place to hide a clean line. It has too many small failures: a relay that stops mid-tick, a counter with one half-stained digit, a valve lip salted only on the room side, a drain cup that catches brine before any desk knows.

That is why handoff rows matter. Under Deep Reach procedure a room support load could be passed from local life support into a wider account. The row was useful: it told route, maintenance and claim desks where the air cost had gone. It also gave them somewhere to keep writing after the room stopped doing the work.

Backfill needs no grand falsification. It needs one later line that smooths the break: compressor accepted, handoff pending, support load carried, route still active. Read beside the pump counter, those phrases lose their comfort. The machine stopped in pieces; the row arrived in one clean block.

QA card CH-7 records the join failing: counter stopped six cycles before the row, relay tick gapped, salt on the room side only. Where that join breaks before the claim hold, the active route was carried by paperwork rather than by pressure.
