# Compressor Handoff Backfill

The compressor is a bad place to hide a clean lie. It has too many little failures: a relay that stops mid-tick, a counter with one half-stained digit, a valve lip salted only on the room side, a drain cup that catches brine before the desk knows anything is wrong.

That is why handoff rows matter. In Deep Reach procedure, a room support load could be passed from local life support into a wider account. The row was useful. It let route, maintenance and claim desks know where the air cost had gone. It also gave them a place to keep writing after the room had stopped doing the work.

Backfill does not require a grand falsification. It only needs a later line that smooths the break: compressor accepted, handoff pending, support load carried, route still active. Read beside the pump counter, those phrases lose their comfort. The machine stopped in pieces. The row arrived in one clean block.

This record does not name the final desk owner. It leaves another hard join: pump, relay, valve, ledger. If that join breaks before the claim hold, the active route was carried by paperwork, not pressure.
