# Cable Forest Conductive Biota

The Cable Forest is where HECTON-8 stops separating biology from infrastructure. Dead relay trunks, torn insulation, brine seepage and patient colonial algae gave local life a scaffold rich in copper, ceramic dust and remembered current. Cable kelp entered that scaffold first as a stain, then as a thread, then as a braided wet conductor. Static thicket learned to stiffen when service pulses passed through the lines. Lantern grass grew near leaks and flashed in reply to pressure waves, not language, but the rhythm is close enough to make a tired diver look twice. For a Marauder, this ecology is a resource map with teeth. A grounded cut can produce conductive fiber, lantern enzyme and a small useful charge. A careless cut can turn a quiet trunk into a complaint: false maintenance pings, old evacuation tones, route fragments repeating through wet tissue, and drones arriving to repair a line that is no longer only metal. The forest is not evil and not a miracle. It is the moon using what the colony left behind, and the abandoned colony misreading life as damage or repair depending on which protocol wakes first.

## Scanner

CONDUCTIVE KELP // Living strands braided through dead relay cable. Stores weak charge, repeats false pings and feeds clean current if cut after grounding.

## Terminal

CABLE FOREST ECOLOGY // Static thicket, lantern grass and cable kelp colonize torn insulation. Treat wet tissue as live line: it reads vibration, bridges damaged routes and may be logged by repair drones as infrastructure.

## Audio

Marauder spool: if the kelp hums back, stop cutting. That is not a plant singing. That is the route remembering pressure.

## Field Note

Good wire does not twitch. If it twitches, ground it, tag it and decide whether you need power badly enough.
