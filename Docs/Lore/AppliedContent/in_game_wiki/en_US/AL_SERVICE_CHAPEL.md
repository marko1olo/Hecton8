# Alcove Nine Work Order, Colony Maintenance

The chalk on the wall of Alcove Nine is a more accurate valve map than the binder on its floor.

The oldest marks are hand outlines pressed flat to the concrete, one to a crew. Later outlines carry a valve number written across the palm. The newest are scratched into the coating, by somebody in gloves with no time, and they use a notation the drawings never had: two crosses for cross-tied to a live header.

Valve 4 carries two crosses. The binder shows Valve 4 isolated. A work order to remove unauthorised surface marking was closed as complete with the wall untouched.

One outline lies under sprayed sealant applied before the alcove entered service.

Assessment: read the wall at 2,060 metres first, and treat any valve carrying two marks as live whatever the drawing says.

## Scanner

Recorder left on a ledge among hand-marks. Confidence 74 percent. Hazard: wall chalk indicates live cross-tie at Valve 4. Limit: markings undated.
