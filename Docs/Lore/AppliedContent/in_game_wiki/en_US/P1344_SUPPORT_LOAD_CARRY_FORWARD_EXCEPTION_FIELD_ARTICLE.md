# Support Load Carry-Forward Exception

A support load carry-forward exception is the desk mark that keeps a failed room attached to a route after the machinery has stopped doing the work. It usually lives on a narrow strip: room code, load class, maintenance override, carry-forward tick, claim hold reference. On a clean day it is bookkeeping. After a life-support break, it becomes a hinge.

The strip is physical enough to read. Grease from the service bay can cross the first half of the row and stop before the override stamp. Ink can pool where damp paper took a thumb. Salt can sit under the folded lip. If the carry-forward tick is cleaner than the grease break, and the compressor handoff already disagrees with pump counter and relay tick, the exception did not move air. It moved responsibility.

This is not a confession line. Deep Reach did not need one. A route could remain active because support load was carried forward. A claim could stay open because the room was still marked under maintenance custody. A failed worker could remain billable or recoverable while the oxygen ledger, suit reserve and scrubber serial were already pointing the other way.

Field handling should treat the strip like a wet mechanical part. Photograph both sides before flattening it. Record grease breaks, salt under folds, stamp overlap and the exact carry-forward tick. Then read it beside compressor handoff backfill. If the exception arrives after the pump stops, the load left the room as liability, not as breath.

## Terminal

CARRY-FORWARD QA // Compare exception strip, maintenance override stamp, grease break, compressor handoff backfill, oxygen ledger cutoff, suit reserve delta and claim hold. Carry-forward is liability movement, not air support.
