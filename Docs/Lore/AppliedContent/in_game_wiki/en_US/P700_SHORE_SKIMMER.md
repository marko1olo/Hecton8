# Shore Skimmer

It will not go below eight metres, which makes its absence more useful than its presence.

The flock runs a continuous line along the surface, feeding on whatever the swell concentrates. Where that line stops dead and restarts forty metres on, the water in the gap has gone green and stripped. No skimmer will enter it, nor should a diver on intake.

Anything that flickers gets struck, and a lamp held near the surface will draw the whole line into your faceplate for two seconds. That is long enough to lose the horizon with a full sling on.

They break formation about forty seconds before a squall, earlier than the Class-IV barometer manages. The line broke on flat water the morning the Annex lost the plate.

Assessment: follow the line, and treat the gap where it ends as closed water.

Deep Reach wrote the flock into the form as an oxygen gauge and cancelled the buoy contract the same quarter. The flock is right more often than the last buoy was, so count where the line stops, not how many fish are in it.
