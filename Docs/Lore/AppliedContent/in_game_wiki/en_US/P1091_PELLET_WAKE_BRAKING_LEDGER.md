# Pellet Wake Braking Ledger

At first glance the ledger looks like scrap: a palm-length strip of burned ablator foil, a cracked route-school slate and a mass account older than the crash. Together they put interstellar transit in a Marauder's hand. Aegir was not opened by heroic ships punching through the dark. It was mapped by small pellet-beam scouts fired from Luyten Junction, cheap machines carrying witness packets and no expectation of return. The route became legal before it became safe.

Human mass arrived later. Atlas-class carriers did not jump. They were pushed, fed, shielded and billed across years. Fusion-electric pulse stacks climbed toward near-light transit while crews slept cold and creditors measured every kilogram as a future lien. Extra shielding, food, propellant, rescue berths, replacement tools: all of it had a mass price. On HECTON-8, mass price turns into blue debt before anyone sees a survivor.

The wake sample records the part public primers soften. Arrival is the hard burn in reverse. Magsails drag against charged dust and sparse plasma. Pellet streams chew the sail edge. Aerobrake passes use gas giants and storm envelopes as tools that can also kill the ship. Too much caution and the carrier misses the window. Too little and the cargo becomes a bright smear.

That is why Deep Reach can file a claim faster than it can save you. A packet crosses the route as information. A debt moves by contract. Black Keel can price a recovery window because it is already in Aegir local space. A rescue hull from the inner worlds must still pay velocity, braking, crew risk and insurance mass. Civilization reaches HECTON-8 first as a signature, then as a warning, and only much later as a machine.
