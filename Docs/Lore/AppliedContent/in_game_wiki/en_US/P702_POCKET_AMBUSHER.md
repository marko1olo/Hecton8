# Pocket Ambusher

It does not hunt. It rents a hole and waits for the corridor to deliver.

A pocket in use looks wrong before it looks dangerous. Every unoccupied lip in a flooded wing carries a soft collar of silt. An occupied one is wiped clean, because the animal has entered and left it a few hundred times. Read the lips, not the dark.

It strikes by pushing water out of the pocket first. On a Class-IV that arrives as a pressure tap on the chest plate roughly half a second before contact. Divers who trust the tap survive. Divers watching their lamp cone do not.

It prefers cut openings to natural ones. Nobody has explained the preference. Assessment: approach a clean lip from above and outside its throat, or route around the wing entirely.

## Scanner

Large-bodied ambush feeder occupying a cut opening. Confidence 71 percent. Hazard: strike range exceeds visible body length. Limit: cannot size the animal without a second angle.

Company logged it as access control because it kept people out of the east wing cheaper than a working door. It kept Hollis out too. Wipe test the lips before you commit a hand.
