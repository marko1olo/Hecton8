# Drowned Concrete Pressure Rubble Ledger

Drowned concrete is one of the easiest materials on HECTON-8 to underestimate. It looks like pale rock until the scanner catches the pattern: ceramic aggregate, gasket stains, bolt dust, rust shadows where reinforcement used to be, and shell sand packed into cracks after years underwater. Natural limestone breaks around bedding. Pressure concrete breaks around a job.

That distinction matters in play. A concrete fragment can point to a buried corridor, a crushed pump room, a hatch frame or a load path that still holds pressure on the other side. It can also be a trap. Concrete that failed slowly grows algae along the lower edge and collects shell grit in quiet cracks. Concrete that failed recently shows sharp pale faces, fresh mineral dust and cable scrapes. A Marauder reads those signs before cutting, because the wrong slab can be the last thing keeping a room from becoming ocean.

Deep Reach sold the colony as clean engineering. The rubble is less flattering. It remembers rushed pours, repair patches, flood lines, unauthorized cuts and the places where workers used industrial material as shelter. Good salvage takes the metal, yes, but good evidence keeps the shape. A wall fragment with rust shadow and gasket stain can say where a door was, what side held pressure, and whether someone opened it, reinforced it or abandoned it before the water arrived.

## Scanner

DROWNED CONCRETE // Pressure-rated colony aggregate, not natural limestone. Rebar is gone; rust shadow remains. Shell sand in fractures marks long flooding, while clean shear faces mark recent collapse or salvage cut.

## Terminal

STRUCTURAL READ / OLD COLONY SLAB // Do not mine as generic stone. Concrete rubble marks a built room, pipe chase or load path. White ceramic aggregate means compression wall. Orange rust shadow means former reinforcement. Green-black lower stain means the piece stood in pooled water before collapse.

## Audio

Natural rock breaks like weather. Colony concrete breaks like somebody paid for a shape and the ocean collected late.

## Field Note

If it has a straight edge underwater, ask who needed the line straight.
