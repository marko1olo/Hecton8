# Clay Silt Turbidity Slope Field Article

Crossing log CT-8, gray clay slope at 2,050 metres, 60 m traverse.

  COMPRESSION RIPPLES: intact except the last 8 m
  FRESH SLIDE SCAR AT LIP: 3 m wide, no ripple recovery
  RETURN PINGER: placed before the crossing, water still clear

Fine clay settles out of old turbidity flows, so the top layer records what moved across it. Dark damp streaks with no ripple recovery are recent. Compression ripples run downslope and point at whatever is buried below.

Thrust wash strips that layer and turns the water into a wall, which is why the pinger goes down while you can still see.

Deep Reach survey called unstable faces seasonal turbidity when a route needed approval and operator disturbance when a shift needed blaming. Same slope, two files.

## Scanner

CLAY-SILT TURBIDITY SLOPE // Fine gray sediment under compression ripples. Fresh slide scar at upper lip. Thruster wash will erase surface testimony and reduce route visibility.

## Terminal

SLOPE ACCESS MEMO // Cross low. No hard thrust. Place return pinger before disturbed sediment. If clay lifts as a sheet, stop cutting and wait for acoustic return.
