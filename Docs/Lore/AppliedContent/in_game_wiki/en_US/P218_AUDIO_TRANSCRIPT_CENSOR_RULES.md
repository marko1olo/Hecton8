# Audio Transcript Damage Bands

Transcript damage bands are the visible scars of damaged audio. They mark what the recorder lost, what a packet notary suppressed, what a bad translation could not resolve, and what a later operator may have cut out. The band is not decoration. It tells the player how much trust the transcript deserves.

A good damaged transcript stays readable where survival depends on it. It can hide a name, a coordinate, a payload serial, a debt clause or the last second of a scream. It cannot hide the door warning, the route reversal, the pressure change, the identity of the speaking role, or the action the player needs to understand the evidence. If damage turns a clue into noise, the clue is not ready.

HECTON-8 uses damage bands as a rule of honesty. A clean subtitle over ruined audio is suspicious. A blacked-out line beside an intact recorder asks who censored it. A repeated missing second across several logs can point to an automated scrubber. The player should learn to read the shape of absence: clipped words, timing jumps, confidence brackets, speaker drift, translation scars and notary marks.

This also protects accessibility. Diegetic damage must not punish players who need captions. The band can show uncertainty, but the warning remains legible. The colony may have failed in darkness; the interface should not.

## Scanner

TRANSCRIPT DAMAGE BAND // Recording has physical or procedural gaps. Redaction may hide names, payload IDs or timestamps. It must not hide the core warning, speaker role or route-critical action.

## Terminal

ACCESSIBILITY GATE // Damaged audio may be incomplete. If the remaining transcript cannot tell the player what happened, what changed, or what is unsafe, do not publish it as a clue.

## Audio

Static may eat the name. It does not get to eat the alarm.

## Field Note

Hide the name if you must. Leave the alarm where a living person can read it.
