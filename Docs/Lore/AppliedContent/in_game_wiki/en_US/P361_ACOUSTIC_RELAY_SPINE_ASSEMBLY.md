# Acoustic Relay Spine Assembly

The acoustic relay spine is the capsule's first proof that it exists. A voice channel is too fragile after impact, mast loss and storm clutter. The spine uses low-band pressure-code through water: ceramic ribs, cable skin, pinger floats and a clock stable enough to repeat position without sounding like random wreck noise.

This matters because orbit does not rescue every sound. Black Keel can hear pressure events from HECTON-8, but the carrier still filters them through claim custody, route geometry and receiver timing. A damaged tap without a route proof becomes background. A clean tap at the wrong clock becomes evidence loss. The assembly turns sound into a recoverable object: position, survival state, clock, checksum.

Salvage work on the spine is narrow and unforgiving. A Marauder can replace cable jacket, seat cracked ribs, ballast the pinger floats and align the packet cadence, but the ocean will keep adding multipath delay, brine distortion and hull knock. The repaired spine does not call for pity. It gives the receiver enough repeated facts to risk a recovery window.

## Scanner

BROKEN ACOUSTIC SPINE // Ceramic rib stack, split cable skin, two pinger floats. Low-band tap possible; orbital receiver will ignore it unless timing and route proof match.

## Terminal

ASCENT RELAY: restore low-band acoustic spine before recovery window. High-gain mast lost. Emergency buoy unreliable. Black Keel receiver accepts pressure-code only with route proof, clock match and custody checksum.

## Audio

If the Keel hears us, it hears ceramic tapping coordinates through bad water.

## Field Note

The first call out of the capsule is not a voice. It is ribs, cable jacket, ballast float and enough clock discipline to make water repeat the same fact twice.
