# Paint Chip, Scratch and Abrasion Ledger

Chipped paint on HECTON-8 is not surface noise. Deep Reach painted pressure modules in readable service colors: off-white hull skin, dark primer and graphite rails, orange safety accents where a gloved hand was expected to find a latch, lever or inspection lip. Decades underwater remove logos and soften labels, but the missing paint keeps a record.

The order matters. Salt inside a chip means the exposed edge breathed with the room for a long time. Clean dark primer on the rim means a recent tool or glove cut through older mineral crust. A curved arc around a latch is not random abrasion; it is repeated access under stress. Straight graphite scratches can be a cutter guide, a dragged equipment case or a panel used as a brace. Orange flakes near a service corner usually mark human action, not weather.

For a Marauder, paint loss is a route tool. It can point to the safe latch side of a pressure door, prove that someone opened a panel after the official shutdown, or separate normal maintenance from later salvage interference. Strip the paint too early and the evidence becomes scrap. Read it first, then decide whether the plate is loot, cover, entry or proof.

## Scanner

SURFACE WEAR // Off-white paint chips expose dark primer, graphite scratch clusters and orange safety flakes near a latch arc. Salt sits inside old chips; bright exposed edges show only on the pull side. This was handled after the first flood.

## Terminal

WEAR READ // Paint loss reads in layers. Salt under a chip means old exposure. Clean primer on an edge means recent tool contact. Curved latch arcs show repeated access; straight graphite scoring shows a guide cut or dragged case. Record the pattern before stripping material.

## Audio

Paint remembers hands before steel remembers force. Missing color tells you who still had time to choose a side.

## Field Note

Fresh dark primer under old salt means someone opened it after the room was already dead. Photograph before you pry.
