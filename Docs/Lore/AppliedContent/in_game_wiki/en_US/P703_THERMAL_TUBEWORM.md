# Thermal Tubeworm

It grows only where hot fluid leaves the floor, which is the one place a cheap oxygen cell reads upward.

Vent chemistry loads the cell. A Class-IV reserve gauge inside a field climbs four to nine percent, smoothly, while the water gets worse. Top off against that number and you surface short.

The colony gives one warning. A field that pulls into its tubes together has already registered a change in flow that reaches you two or three seconds later.

Yield: micronodes seated at the base of tubes over a metre long. Prying one out vents the tube onto your hand at 4,830 m, near 60 C.

Two nodes recovered from fields 1,100 m apart carry the same stamped part number. Assessment: inside a field, treat a rising oxygen number as a fault and confirm on a second cell.

## Scanner

Vent-associated tube colony, dense. Confidence 66 percent. Dissolved oxygen 21 percent and rising. Hazard: thermal contact. Limit: cell not rated for sulphide interference.

## Terminal

## Audio

## Field Note

Calibration drift is the desk's word for a gauge that climbs in vent water, and it means the warning got deleted instead of the manual getting fixed. Carry a second cell into every field and believe the lower one.
