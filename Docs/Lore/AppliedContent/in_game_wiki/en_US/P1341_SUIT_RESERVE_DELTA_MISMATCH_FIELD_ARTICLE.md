# Suit Reserve Delta Mismatch

A suit reserve delta is the difference between three records: what the suit counted at the wrist, what the room said it delivered and what the compressor row claimed to hand off. It is a dull number until it disagrees with the oxygen ledger. Then it becomes the record that stayed bolted to the body.

A working suit does not spend air in clean blocks. Panic, exertion, mask leaks, cold seals and brine in a hose all leave jagged reserve loss. A later desk entry tends to look smooth. If the wrist counter drops into the low-air dead band before the room ledger admits cutoff, the room was no longer carrying the worker. If the desk later restores reserve without a matching tank seal or refill event, the air exists only in paperwork.

The mismatch matters because each owner can tell a different lie. Life support can say the room was still pending. Route desk can say the worker had not returned. Claim desk can say the suit remained recoverable equipment. The suit itself only knows pressure, draw and valve state. That narrowness makes it hard to bribe.

Field handling is physical. Do not vent or top up the suit before recording the counter. Photograph the wrist display, tank valve salt, seal wire, hose coupler scratch and any brine in the fill throat. Compare those marks to the scrubber cutoff row and claim hold. A reserve delta can show where air ended and accounting continued.

## Field Note

If the wrist ran dry before the ledger closed, trust the suit record first.
