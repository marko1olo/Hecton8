# Pressure Failure Thresholds

Pressure Failure Thresholds make HECTON-8 salvage physical instead of abstract. A container is not safe because its name is green in an inventory list. It is safe because the shell, gasket, clamp, proof mark and route history still agree with the depth around it. The thresholds are also a player fairness rule. Before a failure becomes loss, the world should speak: a seal ticks, a gasket drifts, a clamp chatters, a hairline shines, a salt bloom grows, an acoustic return ghosts, or a dead-sample fog starts to cloud the glass. Each signal means the same thing in different tiers: the material has crossed from useful object into local hazard. That lets crafting, UI and environmental audio share the same warning language. The player is punished for ignoring pressure, not for failing to read hidden math.

## Scanner

A good sample becomes a hazard when it crosses the wrong pressure line. The ocean does not care that the label was close.

## Terminal

PRESSURE FAILURE / shallow-rated: seal tick and water creep / industrial-rated: gasket drift and clamp chatter / brine-rated: hairline fracture and salt bloom / abyssal-rated: acoustic ghosting, dead-sample fog / Atlas-rated: custody lockout before structural loss.

## Audio

Pressure does not care that the label was almost right.

## Field Note

Failure thresholds must be readable before punishment: tick, drift, hairline, bloom, dead-sample fog. If the player can only learn after loss, the world is cheating.
