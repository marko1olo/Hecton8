# Welded Seam and Rivet Row Ledger

Welded seams and rivet rows are where the colony's clean geometry starts confessing. Factory hull lines repeat with patience: spacing, bevel, gasket, inspection strip. Field repairs do not. They stutter, swell, overlap old paint and leave heat scars where no launch diagram expected heat.

The order tells the story. A rivet row under a later weld means the original fastening was no longer trusted. Salt hanging below the bead means the joint breathed water more than once. A gasket channel half-buried by metal means someone chose strength over serviceability. Torn plate edges beside a clean bead usually mark a hurry: enough time to seal, not enough time to make the hull honest.

For a Marauder, a repaired seam can be better than a map. It points toward a room that leaked, a bulkhead that flexed, a crew who had tools but no replacement part, or a salvage crew that reopened the wound after everyone else stopped calling it a repair.

## Scanner

HULL JOINT // Lumpy underwater weld bead, oxidized rivet row, torn repair plate edge, salt run beneath seam. Gasket line is partly buried under later metalwork. This was not a factory joint.

## Terminal

JOINT READ // Weld over gasket means repair after seal compression. Rust below rivets means repeated wet breathing, not one flood. Bright scrape on bead crest means a tool tested the seam after pressure cycling.

## Audio

A weld is a signature made by heat. A rivet row is the part that cannot lie about rhythm.

## Field Note

If a seam looks too new, ask what old failure it is trying to cover.
