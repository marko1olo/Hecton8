# Cable Jacket Repair Wrap Ledger

Cables in HECTON-8 are not background noodles. A pressure cable is a promise that two places still mattered to each other: power, signal, pump control, sensor feed, hatch actuation, emergency light. When the jacket fails, the repair wrap becomes a short history of who refused to let that connection die.

The order of marks matters. Gray tape bands with salt under the lip are old repairs that breathed with the sea. Tight clamp dents with clean rubber are late interventions. Diagonal fiber showing under a scratch means the outer jacket was cut or dragged recently. Abrasion on one side means the line worked under tension, not in storage. A cable rubbed low while tied high tells you the room moved after the repair was made.

A Marauder should treat wrapped cable as route evidence. It can lead toward a working pump, a hidden terminal, a powered hatch, a trap that still has current, or a dead room someone tried to keep alive. Cutting every cable for scrap is fast. Reading which cable was repaired last is how a Marauder finds what the colony still cared about.

## Scanner

REPAIR WRAP // Black rubber cable jacket, gray tape bands, diagonal fiber underlayer, salt in seams, clamp-pressure marks. Abrasion is strongest on one side: the line was dragged while powered, not stored.

## Terminal

CABLE READ // Count repair bands before cutting. Wide tape with salt under the lip is old. Tight clamp dents without salt are late. Exposed fiber under a clean scratch means recent abrasion. A cable tied high and rubbed low carried load while the room moved.

## Audio

A dead cable is easy. A repaired cable means somebody still needed the other end.

## Field Note

Never cut the newest wrap first. It usually points to the thing someone saved last.
