# Scrubber Cartridge Serial Mismatch

A scrubber cartridge serial mismatch starts with the small plate fixed to the cartridge body, not the sleeve someone can replace at a desk. In a drowned service bay, that plate can sit under a lip of salt, a latch scar or a stain inside the fill throat. If the plate disagrees with the oxygen ledger or the refill sleeve, read the body first.

The cartridge sits between air and paperwork. It scrubs the room load; the ledger counts its service window; the slot latch shows which body sat in the frame; the refill sleeve says what stores claimed to issue. Those records should move together. A brine line under the latch can be older than a clean sleeve, and that order changes the reading.

Deep Reach did not need to fake chemistry to make trouble. A later sleeve could make a bay look restocked, keep a claim desk note open or push a cutoff row away from the room that failed. The serial plate cannot name the person who touched it. It can narrow the window where someone handled the cartridge after the air account stopped behaving like support.

Field handling is slow because the evidence is low and wet. Do not pull the cartridge before recording the plate, latch salt, sleeve seam, throat brine and ledger row. Bag the sleeve separately. Mark which side of the latch held salt. A clean wrapper does not repair a bad room. A serial mismatch shows where replacement language crossed physical support.

## Scanner

SCRUBBER SERIAL MISMATCH // Cartridge plate does not match the oxygen ledger row. Salt under the latch predates the clean refill sleeve.

## Terminal

SCRUBBER SERIAL QA // Compare plate serial, slot latch salt, refill sleeve, cartridge throat brine, oxygen cutoff row, suit reserve delta and claim restock note. Clean packaging is not a live-air record.

## Audio

Sleeve is clean. Latch salt is old. That cartridge was logged after the room went bad.

## Field Note

Read the plate, not the wrapper. Salt under the latch beats a clean sleeve.
