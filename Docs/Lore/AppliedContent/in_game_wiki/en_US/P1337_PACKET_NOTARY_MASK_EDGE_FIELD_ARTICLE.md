# Packet Notary Mask Edge

A packet notary mask is supposed to mark withheld transcript data without pretending the recorder forgot it. The honest mask has a reason: a name under legal hold, a coordinate under quarantine, a payload serial under sealed table rules. It sits in the document layer and leaves the carrier damage alone. The edge matters because the edge is where procedure touches material.

On a hydrophone strip or recorder cassette, a late mask has bad habits. Its pigment rides over salt dust instead of sitting under it. It bridges a shallow scratch without dipping into the scratch bed. It covers the printed route stamp but misses the scrape that should have forced the same blackout. Under magnification, the carrier says one thing and the notary layer says another: the room damaged the strip first; the office hid the line later.

Deep Reach can call a mask "authorized redaction" and still be lying about time. Authorization does not decide sequence. Sequence lives in pigment edge, mineral order, clock ladder brackets and the first place a tool dragged across the carrier. If the mask edge is older than the salt, the transcript was protected before exposure. If it is cleaner than the salt and floats over the cassette scrape, the transcript was cleaned after recovery.

Field handling is strict. Photograph the edge before wiping. Do not scrape pigment to read the covered line unless the carrier has already been copied. Compare the mask to the transcript damage band and route permission stamp. A readable stamp beside a late mask can turn a clean rescue transcript into a processed claim document.
