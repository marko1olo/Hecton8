# Ran / Aegir Name Authority Ledger

HECTON-8 uses several names because several authorities are trying to own the same water. Ran is the star: an astronomical anchor and the name used when route physics matters. Aegir is the gas giant: the bright parent in the sky, the gravity well that makes the moons expensive, and the shorthand used by claim offices that prefer to discuss a whole system instead of one ruined moon. RAN-B:H8 is the catalog key: cold, insurable, sortable and legal enough for ledgers. HECTON-8 is the field name the player actually bleeds inside.

The difference matters. A route plate saying Ran is talking about light, distance and transit. A Deep Reach memo saying Aegir is often talking about ownership, orbital assets and claim continuity. A Black Keel receipt saying RAN-B:H8 is pricing a specific moon record. A Marauder note saying HECTON-8 is talking about rooms, bodies, pressure doors and the ocean that does not care what the lawyers call it.

Good evidence preserves the name it found. Normalizing too early can erase motive. If a quarantine order says Aegir asset, it may be hiding a moon-scale death inside system-scale language. If a tender quote says RAN-B:H8, it has moved from story to billable mass. If a worker scratched HECTON on a panel, the scratched name is not slang. It is the version of the place that still had people in it.
