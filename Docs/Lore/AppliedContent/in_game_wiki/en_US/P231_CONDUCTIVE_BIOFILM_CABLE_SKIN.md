# Conductive Biofilm Cable Skin

Conductive biofilm cable skin is the lowest, most believable layer of Atlas repair ecology. It starts where old cable jackets crack, where copper salts bleed into seawater and where native films already know how to live on charge, minerals and weak heat. The film is not a curse and not a mind. It is a wet biological conductor that slows corrosion, bridges tiny breaks and smears enough current across damaged insulation to confuse old Deep Reach diagnostics.

Atlas can misuse that ambiguity. A human technician would mark the line as compromised. A damaged maintenance classifier sees reduced corrosion, partial continuity and a reading just above noise, then keeps the circuit in service. The result is useful and frightening at the same time: lights stay on, doors answer late, pingers survive another cycle, and the cable surface grows green-black slicks that twitch under load. For the player, the sign is practical. A wet copper sheen can mean salvageable power, false safety, shock risk or an Atlas repair path that no longer knows the difference between living surface and certified insulation.

## Scanner

CONDUCTIVE BIOFILM // Wet living film has colonized cracked cable jackets; it carries weak charge, slows corrosion and makes failed insulation look temporarily healthy.

## Terminal

ATLAS MAINT TRACE: biofilm conductivity below command threshold, above diagnostic-noise threshold. Corrosion rate reduced; insulation integrity reading unreliable. Treat as maintenance medium, not certified repair.
