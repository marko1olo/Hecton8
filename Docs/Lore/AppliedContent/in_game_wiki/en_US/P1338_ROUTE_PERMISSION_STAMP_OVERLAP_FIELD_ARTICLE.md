# Route Permission Stamp Overlap

A route permission stamp was not decoration. It was the colony's permission to move: worker, hatch, task family, access class, time window and expected return mark. When the stamp appears on a transcript carrier, it shows that the record passed through a route desk before somebody tried to make it look private.

The overlap is the useful part. A clean stamp can still be boring. A stamp partly buried under a packet notary mask is different. Ink from the route desk sits in one layer, salt from the room sits in another, and blackout pigment cuts across both. If the stamp is below the mask but above hatch salt, the permission was logged after exposure and before redaction. The transcript was not simply damaged in place. It was handled, routed, stamped and then selectively covered.

Return marks matter. A normal route stamp closes with a return tick, a cancellation slash or a recovery exception. A missing return mark beside a late mask means the paperwork stopped being a movement record and became a claim record. Deep Reach could still quote the stamp as authorization, but the overlap shows what kind of authorization: not permission to enter the room, permission to process the evidence after it left the room.

Field handling is plain. Do not cut through the stamp. Photograph mask edge and stamp edge in the same frame. Compare the overlap to hatch paint, suit tag, triage ledger and the transcript damage band. The question is not only who had access. The question is who had the carrier on a desk long enough to cover the line.
