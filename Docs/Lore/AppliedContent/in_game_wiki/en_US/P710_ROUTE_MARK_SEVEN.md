# Route Mark 7, Corrected Twice

Route Mark 7 is a beam face with three bearings on it and two of them struck out. 214/1,190 struck, initialled TA. 196/1,240 struck, initialled TA. 188/1,255 standing, in a harder pencil.

The hand that wrote all three never erases. A strike-through keeps the wrong number readable, which is how a route gets audited later, and it is also how you watch a route walk downhill: sixty-five metres of correction in two passes.

188 is followable. From this beam it runs to the Sector 44 crown, where hoist coverage stops at 1,880 metres.

Assessment: copy 188 and both struck figures together. The struck ones were correct when they were written, and the beam edge past the mark is cut without a rail.

## Scanner

Painted route mark, grease pencil over paint, two strike-throughs. Confidence 61 percent. Hazard: beam edge cut and unrailed. Limit: instrument returns the last figure only.

## Terminal

RETURN ACTION QUEUE / ROUTE ADVISORY 44-R-118. MARK 7, SECTOR 44 APPROACH, REVISED TWICE IN ONE SHIFT SERIES. CLASS: ELECTIVE ROUTE, CREW SELECTED. INSPECTION: NOT SCHEDULED. LIABILITY: RESTS WITH HOLDER.

## Field Note

Two corrections in one shift series is procedure working, not procedure failing. What it costs you is that the third figure is the only one anybody carried down.
