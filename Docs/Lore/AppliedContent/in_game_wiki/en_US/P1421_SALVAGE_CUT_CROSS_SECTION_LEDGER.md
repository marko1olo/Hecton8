# Salvage Cut Cross-Section Ledger

A salvage cut is an argument left in material. Nobody slices pressure hull for beauty. They cut because a route was blocked, a body was behind the wall, a cable had to be stolen, or a compartment still had value after the maps stopped agreeing with the metal.

The layers matter. Paint tells the original face. Dark metal core tells load path. Ceramic insulation tells whether the cutter heat was controlled. Rubber gasket slices show whether the seal was cut or torn. Cable bundles reveal whether the crew preserved signal, harvested copper or severed a system on purpose. Salt inside the cross-section means the opening stayed wet before anyone sealed it.

For a Marauder, the question is not only “can this be looted?” It is “who cut this, when, and what were they trying not to lose?” A clean official rescue cut and a hungry Black Keel cut do not leave the same edge. One makes a door. The other makes an alibi.

## Scanner

CUT EDGE // Painted hull skin, dark metal core, ceramic insulation layer, rubber gasket slice, cable bundle, burned cutter line, salt on torn fracture. The cut is not random: heat entered from the outer face, then stopped twice.

## Terminal

CROSS-SECTION READ // Smooth heat bead means controlled cutter. Ragged ceramic means panic or tool chatter. Salt inside the metal core means the edge sat open before recovery. Clean cable ends mean post-flood cut; green-black cable paste means pre-cut leak.

## Audio

A cut edge tells you who arrived after the failure, and what they were willing to destroy.

## Field Note

Look at the ceramic dust before the metal. Panic always shakes the soft layer first.
