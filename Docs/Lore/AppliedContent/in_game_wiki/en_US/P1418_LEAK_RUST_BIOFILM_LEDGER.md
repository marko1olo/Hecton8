# Leak Rust Biofilm Chronology Ledger

Leak stains in HECTON-8 are not decorative grime. They are a layered clock. Rust, salt, biofilm, oil and gasket runoff do not appear at the same speed or for the same reason. Read together, they turn a wall from background surface into failure record.

Rust below a bolt row means water stayed there long enough for metal to answer. Green-brown biofilm means a slow feed: warm equipment, nutrient trace, human waste line, coolant residue or a seep through organic packing. Pale salt bloom means water migrated and dried or breathed through pressure cycles. A black smear under a gasket means the seal carried dirty water before the panel finally moved. If oil crosses over salt, the machine leaked after the brine dried. If salt crosses over oil, the water came later.

That chronology matters to a Marauder. A room with one violent scrape may be dangerous but simple. A room with layered leaks was alive for a while after it began failing. Someone kept pumping, wiping, cycling locks or routing power through a place already losing the argument. Those layers can point to hidden service routes, late repairs, bodies moved after evacuation, or evidence that an official accident report compressed weeks into one sentence.

## Scanner

LEAK TRACE // Rust below bolt row, green-brown biofilm smear, pale salt bloom at gasket lip. Oil-water stain crosses the mineral edge: the leak cycled after the first flood, not during one impact.

## Terminal

LEAK CHRONOLOGY // Read layers in order. Rust forms where metal stayed wet and oxygen still arrived. Biofilm follows slow nutrient flow. Salt bloom marks evaporation or pressure breathing. A dark gasket runoff below clean paint means the seal failed before the panel tore.
