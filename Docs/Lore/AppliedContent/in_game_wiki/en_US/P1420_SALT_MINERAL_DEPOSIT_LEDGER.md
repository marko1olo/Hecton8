# Salt Mineral Deposit Memory Ledger

Salt and mineral deposits are the quiet handwriting of HECTON-8. A clean crack can lie. A polished panel can lie. A mineral edge is harder to fake, because it records motion, drying, pressure breathing and chemistry in separate layers.

White bloom on a seam means water migrated and left its load behind. Calcium drips below a bolt mean slow drainage under gravity, usually after a room stopped being scrubbed. Green-gray rings around a stain mean a repeated wet edge, not one splash. Powder in a gasket groove means the seal moved air or brine through many cycles. If new black runoff crosses the salt, the machine leaked after the mineral crust was already old.

For a Marauder, deposits are not dirt. They are water memory. They can mark a hidden seep, a weak hatch, a cave route that dries during pressure swings, or a supposedly sealed compartment that kept breathing after shutdown. The best deposits do not shout. They wait for someone to notice that the wall has been counting tides without a clock.

## Scanner

MINERAL DEPOSIT // White salt bloom on seam lip, calcium drip below old bolt, green-gray ring around a pressure stain. Not foam. Not snow. The water moved through here, stopped, breathed, and left its minerals behind.

## Terminal

SALT READING // Dry salt on top of rust means old wet failure. Salt under fresh black runoff means the seal leaked later. Calcium fingers that grow downward mark gravity during low-pressure drainage; rings around a bolt mark pressure cycling.

## Field Note

Do not clean the salt before you read it. The first swipe erases the tide count.
