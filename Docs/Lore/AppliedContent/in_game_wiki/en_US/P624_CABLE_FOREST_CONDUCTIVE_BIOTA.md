# Cable Forest Conductive Biota

A recovered cable-forest sample shows why salvage crews ground their tools before harvesting conductive kelp. The growth forms where old relay jackets, mineral brine and light-starved mats overlap. It can patch weak wiring, lure charge into a dead cell or make a relay answer for one more burst, but it also throws signal ghosts and may wake repair drones that still count the living bridge as part of the route.

## Scanner

CONDUCTIVE KELP // Living strands braided through dead relay cable. Stores weak charge, repeats false pings and feeds clean current if cut after grounding.

## Terminal

CABLE FOREST ECOLOGY // Static thicket, lantern grass and cable kelp colonize torn insulation. Treat wet tissue as live line: it reads vibration, bridges damaged routes and may be logged by repair drones as infrastructure.

## Audio

Marauder spool: if the kelp hums back, stop cutting. That is not a plant singing. That is the route remembering pressure.

## Field Note

Good wire does not twitch. If it twitches, ground it, tag it and decide whether you need power badly enough.
