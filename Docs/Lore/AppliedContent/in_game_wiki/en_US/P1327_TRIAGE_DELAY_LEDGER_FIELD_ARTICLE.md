# Triage Delay Ledgers

Triage delay ledgers were not diaries from the medical bay. They were queue machines with blood on the edges. A normal row named the worker, injury class, pressure exposure, oxygen debt, equipment state, movement authority, witness hash and next transfer window. That made the record useful under pressure. It also made cruelty easy to hide.

The most important field is delay. Delay does not mean waiting in a chair. On HECTON-8 it could mean a sealed hatch, a route permission denied by a supervisor, a stretcher without a charged oxygen cylinder, a suit chip that no longer matched its owner, or a medical lock held because opening it would admit the casualty count was higher than the report. The ledger called these cases stabilized, deferred, amber hold or no movement authority. The room often says something harsher.

Read a triage ledger through the room around it. Cot straps cut in panic can contradict a calm transfer note. A clean late stamp over salt bloom can prove the row was corrected after flooding. Repeated oxygen debt marks beside an empty rack are not treatment notes. They are a supply failure. A missing witness hash beside a route hold crossout means nobody with authority wanted to be the person who confirmed the delay.

Deep Reach liked triage because medicine sounds merciful even when it becomes logistics. Marauders should treat the ledger as a crime scene. Do not pull one page and call it evidence. Preserve the page with the hatch tag, the oxygen rack, the pressure tape and the floor marks. The human story is not that someone was hurt. The story is that the colony had a form ready for leaving them where they fell.

## Scanner

## Terminal
