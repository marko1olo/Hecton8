# Salvage-Cut Cross-Section Field Article

Cut-edge card SC-3, panel lifted from the west wall of Module 4 at 1,120 metres.

  LAYERS OUTWARD: paint, graphite load plate, sprayed ceramic, gasket rubber, cable bundle
  BURN LINE: runs over salt
  GASKET SLICE: glossy, compressed

Read the order, not the hole. Burn over salt puts the cut after the flood. Salt inside the ceramic under clean paint means water crossed the wall before anyone opened it. A compressed gasket slice means the module still held pressure when the cutter went in.

The cable bundle was severed with no isolation tag; on the far side that run may still carry power, oxygen or data.

Black Keel prices this panel on mass and alloy class. Photograph the burn direction first: custody on a post-flood cut converts to salvage, and the salt line is the only thing that dates it.

## Scanner

SALVAGE CUT EDGE // Painted hull skin, dark pressure metal, ceramic insulation, rubber gasket slice, cable-bundle cross-section, burned cutter line and salt-stained fracture edge. Cut sequence is readable.

## Terminal

BLACK KEEL HANDLING // Do not price a cut panel by outer paint. Record exposed core, insulation waterline, cable state and burn direction. A clean edge can still be stolen evidence.

## Field Note

Cut edges keep time better than clocks. Salt first, burn second means somebody came back after the room drowned.
