# Route Permission Stamps

Route permission stamps were the colony's small language of movement. They appeared on shift boards, hatch tags, suit chips, vehicle slates, tool cribs and sealed route cards. A good stamp did not tell a story. It tied one worker to one place, one task and one expected return mark.

That is why they matter after the flood. A locker name can prove a person existed. A route stamp can prove where the system sent them. PUMP, SEAL, RELAY, BRINE, FORGE, TRIAGE, BOTANY and WITNESS were not flavor labels. They decided who could cross a lock, enter a service crawl, touch a pump spine, ride a brine cart, sign a relay packet or stand near a medical door. When the stamp is missing, late, crossed out or cleaner than the metal around it, the room starts arguing with the record.

Read the stamp with the object. A stamped hatch that never opened is a denial. A hatch opened after its stamp expired is a late order or an unauthorized rescue. A return box left blank beside scraped floor paint means someone went out and no one with authority came back to close the loop. A fresh administrative sticker over old salt means the story was repaired later. Deep Reach loved stamps because stamps turn people into process. A Marauder should love them for the opposite reason: process leaves fingerprints.

Do not peel a route tag because it is easier to carry than the whole panel. The panel gives pressure state, waterline, tool marks and door direction. The tag alone becomes collectible noise. The tag with its room still knows who was sent, who was held, and who was converted into a clean loss category.

## Scanner

ROUTE STAMP // Worker name, job family and access class still readable. Return mark absent. Hatch paint shows opening after the stamp expired. Preserve the tag before cutting the frame.

## Terminal

ROUTE DESK RULE // A valid stamp must bind worker, route, task and return state. Compare against hatch damage, shift board, suit tag and triage ledger before accepting the route as true.

## Audio

A diary says what someone wanted remembered. A route stamp says who was allowed through a door before the ocean took the door away.

## Field Note

A stamp is not mercy. It is where the system put a body.
