# Cold Veil Eel

It hunts by being mistaken for insulation, and at 1,900 metres the water is full of loose insulation.

A torn damping blanket hangs slack and drifts with the flow. A veil eel holds one straight edge against it. That is the whole difference, and it reads at lamp range if you watch edges instead of material.

It needs the cold, so it works Scrubber Bay 7, which still dumps a plume every forty minutes on a 2147 cycle. The eel comes off the wall four seconds after the priming knock carries through the deck. Contact does not cut. It pulls heat faster than the suit loop replaces it, and a cold seal is a stiff seal.

It sits out every sixth cycle.

Assessment: work the bay between plumes, and treat any sheet with a fixed edge as an animal.

## Scanner

Cold-water sheet morphology, one margin held rigid. Confidence 55 percent. Hazard: thermal loss on contact. Limit: cannot separate specimen from damping blanket beyond nine metres.

## Terminal

SPECIMEN 44-E-503. CLASS: TEMPERATURE INSTRUMENT, UNSCHEDULED. SITE: SCRUBBER BAY 7 DISCHARGE, 1,900 M. TAG 7742-C: REPORTING. BAY CYCLE: MAINTAINED. STAFFING: NOT REQUIRED SINCE 2147.

## Field Note

They call it an instrument because the maintenance routing grew a tag into its leading edge, and the discharge loop still logs what that tag sends. Watch the priming knock, not the water.
