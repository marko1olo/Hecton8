# Iliya Varnek Tide Margin

Iliya Varnek is the 2147 Deep Reach risk signature tied to the Great Tide margin. He is not written as a cartoon executive with a lever. The memo is worse because it is ordinary: a production schedule, an averaged model, a confidence line, a worker exposure note and a recommendation to keep moving.

The key phrase is averaged model confidence. On average, the route looked survivable. In the tail, where Aegir tide stacking, ice-shelf rebound, pressure traffic and Atlas compensation errors met, the margin narrowed until a living route became an accounting tolerance. The people in those pressure rooms did not live on average. They lived inside the worst hour.

Varnek's signature gives the player one layer of responsibility. It does not prove he knew the exact disaster that would follow. It proves Deep Reach had a readable risk path and chose the cheaper interpretation. The crime is not omniscience. The crime is accepting uncertainty because the bodies who would carry it were not in the room where the margin was priced.

## Scanner

RISK MEMO // I. Varnek downgraded the Great Tide margin after the model passed on average. Tail risk was left inside worker pressure rooms.

## Terminal

VARNEK, I. // Aegir Operations Risk // Recommendation: maintain production schedule. Extreme tide stack remains low-frequency operational variance pending Atlas compensation review. Worker route exposure acceptable under averaged model confidence.

## Audio

He did not order a flood. He priced it low enough to happen.

## Field Note

Do not read this as a murder order. Read it as a budget line where uncertainty became cheap enough to spend.
