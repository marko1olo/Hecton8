# Pressure Suit Repair Patch Ledger

Pressure suit patches are human handwriting on a life-support object. Factory panels speak in clean curves and certification marks. Field patches speak in crooked thread, wrong-colored textile, salt under tape and the blunt honesty of somebody fixing a leak before the next pressure cycle.

Patch order matters. Seam tape under cross-stitch means the first repair failed or was not trusted. Salt trapped beneath thread means the suit was already exposed when patched. Abrasion over a patch means the wearer returned to work. Clean tape over old damage means inspection after use. Two fabric colors mean cannibalized material, often from a dead suit or a locker nobody expected to reopen.

For a Marauder, a patch is not costume detail. It can identify an emergency route, a worker's last task, a triage decision, a stolen kit, or a suit grade that never belonged to the person wearing it. In HECTON-8, survival often looks like ugly stitching that held for one more dive.

## Scanner

SUIT PATCH // Dark neoprene-like shell, stitched repair island, reinforced seam tape, salt scuff, off-white technical textile strip. Patch grade uneven: field repair, not factory service.

## Terminal

PATCH READ // Cross-stitch over seam tape means emergency closure. Salt under thread means patch applied after exposure. Clean tape over abraded fabric means a later inspection. Two patch colors mean cannibalized suit material.
