# Ribbed Flexible Hose

Hose trace card RH-4, ribbed pressure line, service drawer to pump cradle, 6 m run.

  RIDGE TOPS: dry salt, abrasion on one side
  TROUGHS: still wet
  CLAMP BANDS: two flats, one clamp over old salt
  PAINT FLECK: cyan at the drawer end

Salt on the ridges is dry cycling. Wet troughs mean the line carried pressure after the room flooded. Abrasion on one side means it was dragged, not installed.

A clamp over old salt is a late repair; salt over the clamp edge means the clamp was there first. Cyan identifies a coolant line once the label is gone.

Trace both ends before cutting. A dead-looking hose can be the only thing still feeding air, coolant or a powered junction.

## Scanner

RIBBED FLEXIBLE HOSE // Pressure-rated rubber line. Salt on ridge tops, wet troughs and flattened clamp bands suggest recent flow. Trace both ends before cutting; a dead-looking hose can still be the only thing feeding air, coolant or proof.

## Terminal

Bypass note: hoses routed after the evacuation order are not tidy. If the ribs are polished on one side, someone dragged the line under load. If the troughs stay wet, the line carried pressure after the room flooded.

## Audio

You can hear the room through the hose if you put your glove on it. Air sounds sharp. Coolant sounds tired. A cut line sounds like debt arriving.

## Field Note

Ridges tell you what dried. Troughs tell you what kept moving. Clamp flats tell you who got desperate.
