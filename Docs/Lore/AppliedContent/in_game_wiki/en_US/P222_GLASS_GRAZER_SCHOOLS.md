# Glass Grazer Schools

Glass grazers are the first shallow animals that make beauty useful without making the shelf safe. Their transparent shells and hard mouth-ridges scrape mineral film from pressure glass, loose photic mats and old cable jackets. A feeding school leaves pale lanes that show recent current, fresh growth and the safest line around a fouled seam. Watch the edge of the school, not the center: they avoid warm sealant, sour brine leaks and surfaces that carry Atlas-compatible signal drift. If the ticking stops while food remains, something larger has changed the water. Treat the cleared lane as evidence, not as permission to sprint.

## Audio

Hear the ticking on the shell? That is lunch, map and warning in the same sound.

## Field Note

Do not chase the school. Read what it exposes: fresh film, old scrape, warm-sealant avoidance, and the empty lane the animals refuse to cross.
