# Damp Insulation Blanket Heat Memory Ledger

A damp insulation blanket is not soft background. In HECTON-8 it is a wall that kept a secret badly. Heat, breath, condensation and pressure do not cross fabric evenly. They darken seams, salt stitch lines, flatten quilt pockets and expose which side of a module stayed alive longest.

The sequence matters. Moisture darkening inside a seam means slow condensation while the compartment still had warm air. Salt dust on top of that line means the space later dried or pressure-breathed. A tear with dry salt inside means the breach opened after the blanket was already old. A clean patch over rotten quilting means somebody repaired the room after admitting it would never be clean again.

For a Marauder, insulation is shelter evidence. It can show a surviving air pocket, a hidden service void, a room kept warm by a dying machine, or a corridor where workers slept because official bunks were already unreachable. The blanket is humble material, but it remembers comfort as a technical emergency.

## Scanner

INSULATION BLANKET // Quilted gray-beige technical fabric, compressed seam, dark moisture line, salt dust in stitch channel, small tear exposing darker inner layer. This wall once held heat or air longer than the room around it.

## Terminal

INSULATION READ // Dark seam first, salt second: slow condensation before dry pressure cycling. Salt under torn fabric: breach opened after the blanket dried. Warm-side staining on the wrong face means a panel was reversed during late repair.

## Field Note

If the blanket is damp above shoulder height, ask what was still warm enough to breathe there.
