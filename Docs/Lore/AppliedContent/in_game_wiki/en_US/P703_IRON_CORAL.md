# Iron Coral

It only grows on metal. A stand in open silt maps what is buried under it.

Colour reads the substrate: rust-red over structural steel, grey-black over machine alloy, dull blue where the plate below carries trace XO.

The skeleton is harder than a Class-IV palm plate. Two shifts hauling across a stand will open a glove seam, which will not read as a leak until you flex the hand.

Crust over a crack runs three times thicker than crust on sound plate 200 mm away. Nothing in the metal explains it. Where it bridges a weld, the seam cannot be inspected again.

Yield is the crust itself, richest where thickest. Plate cut with crust on it bills at gross, and a 4.8 tonne window does not itemise, so chip it at the wreck, not on the Keel.

## Scanner

Mineralised colonial growth on ferrous substrate. Metal return strong. Confidence 77 percent. Hazard: abrasive to glove seams. Limit: crust and substrate return as one mass.

## Terminal

ASSAY LOT 44-N-118. CLASS: RECOVERABLE MINERAL, GRADE PENDING. SUBSTRATE: STRUCTURAL STEEL, ORIGIN NOT ESTABLISHED. SEAMS 12 TO 19: OVERGROWN, INSPECTION CLOSED. MASS CREDITED AT GROSS. DESK: KEELMARK LOSS DESK.

## Field Note

Mass credited at gross means the crust rides on your window and the desk keeps the difference. If a break comes up blue, that is not a grade, that is blue debt, and you log it before you touch it.
