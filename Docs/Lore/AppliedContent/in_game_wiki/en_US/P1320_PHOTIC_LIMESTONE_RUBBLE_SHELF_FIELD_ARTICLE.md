# Photic Limestone Rubble Shelf

The photic limestone rubble shelf is the first place many crews mistake visibility for safety. In shallow HECTON-8 water, calcium plates reflect Aegir-filtered light and turn the sea floor pale enough to read from a distance. That is useful, but incomplete. Shell grit collects where small currents still work. Olive algae stains protected pores where the surface has stayed stable long enough for life to hold. Bright white fractures mark fresh breakage, old quarry scuffs or recent salvage. A shelf that looks smooth from above may hide undercut edges and loose rubble pockets. Walk it like a ledger, not a beach.

## Scanner

PHOTIC LIMESTONE SHELF // Pale calcium plates, shell grit and broken colonial-rubble fragments. Bright water makes it look simple. The cracks are the map: current, quarry cuts, collapse edges and safe boot holds all collect different sediment.

## Terminal

Survey note: do not route by brightness alone. Shell grit in a crack means recent wash. Olive stain in pores means stable shelter. Clean white fracture means a fresh break or a scavenger cut.

## Audio

The first shelf fools everyone. You see light and think safe. Then the rubble moves under your boots and the ocean tells you it has been counting longer than you have.

## Field Note

Bright stone is not safety. It is just evidence you can see before it punishes you.
