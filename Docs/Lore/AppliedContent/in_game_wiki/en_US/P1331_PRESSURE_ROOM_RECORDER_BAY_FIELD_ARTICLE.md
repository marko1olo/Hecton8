# Pressure Room Recorder Bays

A pressure room recorder bay is the metal cradle that made hydrophone log strips possible. It is usually bolted to the inside wall of a pump room, lock vestibule, triage pocket or service chamber, close enough to feel the room through its frame and dumb enough to keep working after the network stopped being honest.

        The colony did not install these bays because anyone loved old machinery. They were installed because pressure rooms kill fast and digital logs can be edited faster. A recorder bay used a ceramic cassette, a hydrophone puck, a pressure ink wheel and a clock ladder cut into its own feed. If the room flexed, the bay heard it. If a hatch bit the frame, the teeth climbed the tape. If someone cleaned the audio later, the cassette still carried wheel drag, salt bloom, latch scars and the small uneven places where the machine hesitated under load.

        A good bay tells the player what kind of lie they are holding. A missing cassette tooth means the strip was pulled in a hurry. A cracked glass lip with no matching pressure spike means someone broke the bay after the event. A dry ink wheel in a wet room points to a swapped cartridge. A service seal torn from the wrong side can turn a harmless recovered voice into proof that someone stood there after lockdown. None of this needs exposition. The object should look like it has spent years waiting for someone patient enough to read the boring parts.

        Recorder bays also explain why HECTON-8 audio should not feel like magical surveillance. The bay has a place, a body and limits. It hears through metal and water. It misses soft words. It over-records pumps. It lies when the room is flooded past its pickup. It becomes useful only when compared to route permission stamps, transcript damage bands, hydrophone strips, door state and the scars left by marauder correction marks. The bay is not a camera. It is a stubborn machine still holding pressure after everyone who trusted the archive was gone.

## Scanner

PRESSURE ROOM RECORDER BAY // Wall-mounted acoustic recorder cradle. Empty cassette teeth, cracked bay glass, pressure ink wheel and dead-clock ladder remain readable. Match any strip against door damage and pump cadence.

## Terminal

MAINTENANCE NOTE // A recorder bay is evidence only when the bay seal, cassette tooth wear, ink wheel, time ladder and room state agree. Do not publish a cleaned audio pull without the bay photograph.

## Audio

The bay did not remember people. It remembered what the room survived.

## Field Note

Photograph the bay before you play the strip. Clean sound is cheap. A dirty cradle is harder to forge.
