# Shallow Seagrass Root-Mat Substrate

Shallow root mats grow where light, carbonate sand, mild current and old nutrient runoff meet. They resemble seagrass from a distance, but the useful part is below the blades: dark root fibers stitching shell grit, coral analogue dust and pale limestone crumbs into a springy skin. That skin can save a diver by holding loose sediment in place, or betray a diver by hiding soft silt under a stable-looking surface. Root knots trap route pingers, tool cord, broken shell clamps and old pipe edges. A clean pale gap in the mat may be grazer work, recent foot traffic or a current lane. Probe before trusting it.
