# Recorder Latch Glass Fracture

The recorder latch glass is not a window. It is a small pressure-rated witness cover over the bay latch, installed so a tired technician could confirm the latch was seated without opening the recorder. It was cheap, replaceable and usually ignored. That is why it survived as evidence.

        Glass remembers force differently from metal. A door shock leaves a tight star with short legs and no scrape along the latch tongue. A hand tool leaves a crescent bite at the outside edge before the glass runs. A worker opening the bay from inside a failing room leaves glove polish, salt-wet bloom and tiny chips on the hinge side. A late scavenger leaves cleaner hooks through old mineral dust. None of this tells the whole story, but it tells which side touched the bay.

        Good latch glass should be read beside the cassette teeth and the pressure ink wheel. Teeth show strip pull. The wheel shows wet or dry recording. The glass shows access. If the official audio says the recorder was recovered sealed, but the glass has a pry hook over old salt, somebody opened it after the room changed. If the glass has a pressure star and the cassette teeth still match the room, the bay may have been shut when the lock hit. The difference matters: one is an accident recorded by a machine, the other is a machine made to support a lie.

        For the player, the rule is simple: scan before salvage. Break the glass too early and the bay becomes parts. Read it first and it can decide whether a clean clip belongs to this room, whether a route stamp was forged after lockdown, or whether a dead worker actually reached the latch before the water did.

## Scanner

LATCH GLASS FRACTURE // Clear pressure glass over a recorder bay latch. Crack origin, inner salt, hinge-side chips and pry arcs show whether the bay opened under pressure, after flooding or by tool.

## Terminal

MAINTENANCE NOTE // Read latch glass before lifting the cassette. Interior bloom means the room breathed wet. A clean outer hook means late pry. A pressure star without latch travel means impact, not access.
