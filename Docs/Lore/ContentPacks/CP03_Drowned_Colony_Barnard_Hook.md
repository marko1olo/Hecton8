# CP03 - Drowned Colony And Barnard Hook

Status: source draft.
Packet Family: human texture / personal motive / colony evidence.
Canon Owner: Player_Motive_Arc, HECTON8_Depth_Bands, Gameable_World_Packets P04/P11/P18.
Runtime Layer: Narrative.
Primary Surfaces: terminal, PDA, object labels, personal log, ending dossier.

## Player Experience

The colony should not be introduced as "lore."

It should be introduced as spaces built for ordinary work: tool lockers, food heaters, wet boots, cheap wall paint, pressure bunks, child height marks, missing shift boards, broken mess tables, moldy checklists, and an emergency manifest that stopped being updated mid-procedure.

Then the player finds a Barnard mark.

Not a huge reveal. A tool stamp. A pressure wrench style. A work chant in a transcript. A name from a yard contract. Something that says: people from the player's world were here.

## Core Objects

Worker Locker:
Personal items, tool tags, meal tins, patched suit layers.

Pressure Bunk:
Small sleeping chamber. Good for environmental storytelling and hidden notes.

Shift Board:
Names, routes, missed check-ins, evacuation priority columns.

Mess Heater:
A mundane object that makes the space feel inhabited.

Barnard Tool Mark:
Stamp, design pattern, worker slang, or maintenance plate tied to Barnard Yards/frontier salvage culture.

Evacuation Manifest:
Key evidence. People became routing problems.

## Beat Chain

1. Player enters first drowned worker space.
2. Finds ordinary life objects before horror objects.
3. Reads a small terminal or wall note.
4. Finds a Barnard mark.
5. PDA/personal log updates tone.
6. Later, names recur in deeper manifests.
7. Endings can account for whether the player recovered, ignored, sold, or exposed these names.

## Seeded Variants

Barnard Connection:
Toolmaker, old crew, salvage school, debt broker, mentor, rival yard, union mark, employer mark, revoked work contact. No family-revenge or missing-relative hook.

Colony Space:
Kitchen, locker hall, pressure bunk row, tram stop, tool crib, nursery corner, hydroponic break room.

Manifest State:
Readable names, corrupted names, only employee numbers, missing children, Deep Reach overwritten columns.

Emotional Timing:
Early subtle mark, midgame stronger name, late-game direct professional link through procedure, debt, mentor, rival crew, employer, or revoked work access.

You can ignore a dead corporation. Harder to ignore a wrench from your own yard.

PDA Codex Body:
The first Barnard mark on HECTON-8 is small enough to miss. That is why it matters. Not a flag, not a confession, not a dramatic message. A tool stamp. A supply habit. A maintenance shorthand used by people who learned work the same hard way the player did.

HECTON-8 stops being a remote contract when the same marks begin appearing beside names, evacuation routes, and failed authorizations. The colony was not just Deep Reach property. It was made of imported labor from the same frontier economy that still feeds Marauder work in 2190.

## Gameplay Use

This pack turns professional interest into personal investment:

- discover human colony texture;
- tie player origin to HECTON-8;
- create names for later evidence;
- let endings track whether the player carries evidence or only cargo.
