## Black Keel Ownership

### Option A - Neutral Claim-Pool Vessel

Black Keel belongs to an Aegir-system claim pool: a legal/insurance cooperative that leases automated carriers to independent salvage operators.

Pros:
- Best preserves player independence.
- Makes Marauder economy feel normal.
- Allows the carrier to be cold, contractual, and not personally evil.
- Good for replay: different contracts can use the same infrastructure.

Cons:
- Deep Reach pressure becomes indirect unless they own the debt, insurance, or route priority.
- Needs a strong reason why the pool accepted a dangerous HECTON-8 job.

Best use:
Default baseline. Deep Reach manipulates the pool through old claim priority, insurance locks, and hidden payload clauses.

### Option B - Deep Reach Shell Company Vessel

Black Keel is formally owned by a clean shell operator, but Deep Reach controls the payload terms, route certificates, and recovery priority.

Pros:
- Strong paranoia from the start.
- Makes false endings and betrayal easier.
- Explains why the player was selected and why rescue is not clean.

Cons:
- Risks making the player feel stupid if the setup is too obviously a trap.
- Reduces independent Marauder flavor if overused.

Best use:
Use as hidden truth, not public start state.

### Option C - Marauder Cooperative Vessel

Black Keel is owned by a rough salvage cooperative, with the player as one debt member among many.

Pros:
- Strong field culture.
- Lets the carrier feel familiar but unforgiving.
- Supports future NPC names, caches, and old route notes.

Cons:
- Weakens isolation if the cooperative feels too friendly.
- Harder to explain why no one helps unless all comms/orbits/debt locks are tight.

Best use:
Good if the player needs a stronger social origin.

### Option D - Impounded / Repossessed Vessel

Black Keel was once Marauder or independent, but is now under legal repossession, insurance custody, or route court control.

Pros:
- Combines independence, debt, and corporate/legal pressure.
- Makes the carrier emotionally ugly: familiar hardware under hostile ownership.
- Fits "professional interest becomes personal."

Cons:
- More complex to explain cleanly in first hour.

Best use:
Strong hybrid candidate.

Current recommendation:
Hybrid A + B + D. Publicly Black Keel is a claim-pool automated tender. In practice it is debt-impounded and Deep Reach has hidden priority hooks in the recovery contract.

## Aegir Moons

Working approach:
Use both catalog labels and field names.

- Public / navigation: Aegir-I, Aegir-II, Aegir-III.
- Marauder / crew slang: short physical names based on danger, color, orbit, or resource.
- Deep Reach: asset codes and claim blocks.

Design rule:
Every named moon needs one hard physical role:

- orbit class;
- geology;
- resource or hazard;
- route function;
- why it matters to Black Keel / HECTON-8.

Current bias:
Keep HECTON-8 as Aegir-VIII publicly, with a harsher field nickname still undecided.

## Blue Debt Behavior

### Option A - Stable If Kept Pressurized

Blue debt is valuable only when stored in pressure-rated containers. Depressurization ruins quality.

Pros:
- Strong salvage engineering loop.
- Clean hard-sci-fi constraint.
- Makes extraction physically tense.

Cons:
- Less horror unless paired with Atlas signaling or biological contamination.

### Option B - Atlas-Signal Active

Blue debt carries or reflects a pressure-harmonic signal Atlas uses for repair/classification.

Pros:
- Connects resource to predators, drones, and Atlas attention.
- Makes greed mechanically dangerous.
- Good for false endings: sample extraction has consequences.

Cons:
- Must avoid magic artifact feeling.

### Option C - Bio-Chemically Contaminating

Blue debt samples can carry catalytic biofilm, enzymes, spores, or repair resin.

Pros:
- Ties resource to flora/fauna and Atlas-altered biology.
- Explains quarantine language.

Cons:
- Risks zombie/virus cliche if written lazily.

Current recommendation:
Hybrid A + B, with restrained C. Blue debt must be kept pressurized, and good samples carry a weak Atlas-compatible pressure signal. Contamination is chemical/industrial/ecological, not horror-virus.

## False Ending Forms

### Option A - Real Credits: Salvage Success / Truth Failure

Player extracts a valuable sample, Black Keel recovers it, contract pays, credits roll. The truth remains buried or sold.

Pros:
- Bitter and clean.
- Good Stalker-like false ending.
- Strong critique of player greed.

Cons:
- Can feel like a hard stop if player invested in the world.

### Option B - Partial Extraction, Same Seed Return

Player reaches orbital recovery, sees consequence, then can return to the same HECTON-8 seed with changed contract pressure.

Pros:
- Keeps long campaign alive.
- Lets early escape be real but not final.

Cons:
- Needs careful UI/save framing so it does not feel like a fake ending.

### Option C - Corporate Capture Ending

Black Keel or Deep Reach proxy recovers the player and sample. Player becomes witness, asset, or liability.

Pros:
- Strong if Deep Reach hooks are hidden.
- Makes ownership choice matter.

Cons:
- Can remove agency if not clearly earned by player choices.

### Option D - Coward Exit / Bad Dossier

Player repairs enough to leave, but without truth or enough payload. The dossier records them as alive but useless, haunted, or blacklisted.

Pros:
- Supports "you can leave, but you did not finish."
- Good for replay meta.

Cons:
- Risk of moralizing the player too directly.

Current recommendation:
Use at least two false-ending families:

- Material Ending: pays, credits, truth failure.
- Returnable Partial Exit: same seed can continue, but contract/world state worsens.

## Personal Motive

### Option A - Found Names

Player begins professional. It becomes personal because recovered names and bodies expose what Deep Reach erased.

Pros:
- Best for exploration-first structure.
- Lets player bond through evidence, not backstory dump.
- Works with any origin.

Cons:
- Slower emotional ignition.

### Option B - Barnard Yards Link

Someone from Barnard Yards, old salvage culture, or the player's debt chain was tied to HECTON-8.

Pros:
- Deepens the ex-Deep-Reach protagonist with a salvage-culture origin instead of family melodrama.
- Deepens human-space lore.

Cons:
- Needs precise naming to avoid melodrama.

### Option C - Contract Was A Trap

The player was selected because their identity, debt, or credentials make them useful to Deep Reach/Black Keel.

Pros:
- Strong thriller pressure.
- Makes carrier ownership matter.

Cons:
- Can overpower exploration if revealed too early.

### Option D - Former Deep Reach Past

Player was directly connected to Deep Reach or inherited old guilt.

Pros:
- Immediate personal stakes.

Cons:
- More conventional; risks shrinking the world around one protagonist.

Current recommendation:
Superseded by current lock: the player is former Deep Reach field-systems / evacuation-infrastructure labor and now a Barnard/frontier Marauder. Keep A + B + late C, but reinterpret them through that identity: professional interest first, found names and recognized procedures make it personal, Barnard Yards explains the salvage body language, and late contract-trap evidence reveals the player was selected as useful disposable labor.
