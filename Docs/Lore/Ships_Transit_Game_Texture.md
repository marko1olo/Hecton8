# Ships And Transit - Game Texture

Status: working world texture.
Purpose: define how ships and interstellar travel feel in player-facing material.

## Core Rule

Ships are infrastructure, not freedom.

In HECTON-8, a ship is usually a route contract, a pressure schedule, a debt object, a cargo authority, or a machine that can leave without you.

## No-FTL Consequence

There is no instant Sol command, no real-time Earth rescue, no magic courier.

For an Epsilon Eridani-like distance around 10.5 light years:

- 0.05c means about 210 years before overhead;
- 0.10c means about 105 years before overhead;
- 0.15c means about 70 years before overhead;
- 0.20c means about 52.5 years before overhead.

Braking, launch infrastructure, shielding, cargo mass, politics, and staging make real operations uglier.

This does not mean nobody travels. It means most movement is planned, inherited, financed, and staged through older domains and route infrastructure.

## Aegir Build-Up Timeline Shape

First:
Needleprobes and fast survey craft arrive with thin data.

Second:
Seed craft arrive and build anchors, depots, reactors, survey grids, and early mistakes.

Third:
Heavy cargo and system carriers build local infrastructure around Aegir.

Fourth:
Human settlement arrives under contract, custody, torpor, debt, or corporate employment.

Fifth:
HECTON-8 dies in 2147, but machines, route law, claims, and remote records keep moving.

By 2190:
The system is specialist-known, commercially dirty, and physically isolated enough for a Marauder to be trapped.

## Ship Classes As Player Texture

Needleprobe:
Player sees it as old survey data, weirdly precise maps, dead beacon formats, and ancient first-look images.

Seed Ship:
Player sees buried factory bones, old autonomous hardware, Atlas-compatible architecture, and infrastructure older than the colony rooms.

Mass Carrier:
Player sees cargo records, route costs, sealed containers, and why moving heavy things between stars is a civilization-level decision.

Sleeper Transport:
Player sees old human transfer manifests, medical custody records, torpor tags, and people treated as scheduled cargo.

System Carrier:
Player sees the Black Keel. Local, useful, automated, distant, contract-bound, and not emotionally loyal.

Bathy-Drop Capsule:
Player sees wreckage, survival shell, descent heat shield, broken ascent package, and the reason the game begins in the ocean.

Transfer Tug:
Player sees small orbital/local craft in logs or wreck fragments. Good for Aegir route history and old colony supply chains.

Maintenance Drone Tender:
Player sees it indirectly through old repair drone systems and automated service nests.

## The Black Keel

The Black Keel should feel like:

- a rented machine;
- a creditor;
- a weather-dependent voice;
- a cargo authority;
- a possible rescue platform;
- a partial Deep Reach leak;
- a thing that can help without caring.

It should not feel like:

- the player's cool personal ship;
- a warm crew hub;
- a dead background prop;
- a villain monologue device.

## What The Player Does With Transit

The player can:

- repair uplinks;
- time orbital windows;
- choose which packet to send;
- hide or report samples;
- rebuild an ascent path;
- recover route telemetry;
- unlock carrier leverage;
- trigger false exit or final payload outcomes.

The player cannot:

- casually call Earth;
- summon a rescue ship at will;
- fly the Black Keel directly from the ocean;
- bypass pressure/orbital/weather constraints with one upgrade.

## Source Text Hooks

Scanner - Bathy-Drop:
Disposable descent package. Designed to arrive, not return.

Scanner - Old Transfer Lug:
Local transfer fitting. Aegir traffic hardware, not interstellar grade.

Carrier Line:
Recovery requires window, verified ascent package, and claim clearance.

Marauder Line:
Ships do not rescue you. Routes do, if you paid the right people before you got wet.

Terminal - Sleeper Manifest:
Human transfer batch received under employment custody. Medical wake authority deferred to local operator.

## Gameplay Rule

Transit must create pressure and possibility at the same time.

If the player believes escape is impossible, the carrier is just background. If escape is easy, HECTON-8 loses its teeth. The correct state is worse: escape is possible, but every path asks what the player is willing to leave behind.
