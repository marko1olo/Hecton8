# COLONY LIFE BEFORE THE DISASTER: SECTOR 44

> **Source:** Personal log recovered from Sector 44 Living Quarters, Unit 8-B.  
> **Speaker:** Maintenance Tech V. Aris.  
> **Reviewer note:** This document provides context on the psychological and physical deterioration of the Deep Reach workforce prior to the catastrophic pressure failure.

---

## 1. The Constant Weight

It is one thing to know the ocean is out there. It is another to feel it pressing against the titanium, every single second. Sector 44 was built on the edge of the abyssal trench. The groaning of the titanium struts is constant. It sounds like a dying whale trapped inside the walls.

The corporate orientation video said the modular bulkheads could withstand a direct impact from a tectonic shift. But when you are lying in your bunk at 0400 hours and you hear the rivets pinging against the hull pressure, you realize the math doesn't care about your heart rate. The ocean wants to crush us. It is just waiting for someone to forget to cycle an airlock.

## 2. Synthetic Rations and Vitamin D

We haven't seen the sun in fourteen months. The Atlas-6 AI regulates the UV-spectrum lighting in the hydroponics bay to simulate daylight, but the bulbs are failing and supply ships from Earth are three solar cycles behind schedule.

We survive on synthesized kelp protein blocks and recycled water. The water always tastes faintly of copper and ozone. Keelmark executives claim the nutritional paste meets all FDA requirements, but half the crew is suffering from scurvy and bone-density loss. You try swinging a plasma cutter for twelve hours a day when your teeth are loose.

## 3. The "Deep Sickness"

The company doctors call it "Pressure-Induced Delirium." We call it the Deep Sickness. 

It starts with the acoustics. Because sound travels four times faster in water, the acoustic footprint of the colony creates a feedback loop of low-level vibration. After six months, people start hearing voices in the hum of the ventilation shafts. They swear the water outside the reinforced glass is whispering their payroll ID. 

Last week, a driller named Chen tried to open an exterior airlock without his helmet. He said he just wanted to feel the rain. The automated safety overrides stopped him, but corporate docked his pay for "unauthorized use of heavy machinery." 

## 4. The Xenon Quotas

None of this would matter if the quotas weren't killing us. Deep Reach doesn't care about the research labs or the geological surveys. They only care about the Xenon-Omega substrate. 

We are mining a highly volatile isotope from the edge of an active thermal vent. The heat is unbearable. The automated drones keep melting, so they send us out in the Class-IV suits to manually extract the cores. If we miss the weekly extraction quota by even a kilogram, Atlas-6 dials back the oxygen scrubbers in the living quarters by 5% to "conserve power." 

We are suffocating by degrees, and the spreadsheets call it efficiency.
