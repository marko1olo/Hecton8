# THE AEGIR MOON SYSTEM AND ORBITAL HAZARDS

> **Source:** Aegir Navigation Authority, Astronomical Logbook.
> **Speaker:** Automated Celestial Registry.
> **Reviewer Note:** A breakdown of the orbital mechanics and environmental hazards of the Aegir system, defining the parameters of our isolation.

---

## 1. The Moon Ladder and Gravitational Resonance

The gas giant Aegir dominates the sky of HECTON-8, anchoring a complex system of nine major moons and dozens of smaller moonlets. HECTON-8 occupies the eighth position in this orbital sequence, locked in a stable resonance that drives its intense geothermal activity and severe tidal surges.

The moons—Skarn, Vela, Claw, Lumen, Thorne, Anvil, Kestrel, HECTON, and Mute—constitute the planetary environment. Each body exerts a distinct physical influence on operations below. Skarn, the innermost moon, experiences severe magnetospheric heating, generating intense radiation that sweeps across the inner orbits. Vela continuously sheds ice crystals and fine silicate dust, creating orbital debris clouds that dirty the optical sensors of communication relays. Thorne and Anvil, cold and dense, remain as relics of abandoned corporate mining surveys, their gravity wells serving as navigation checkpoints.

## 2. Eclipse and Route-Shadow Windows

During orbital alignment, the inner moons pass directly between Aegir and HECTON-8, creating temporary signal and light blockages. Known as route-shadow windows, these occurrences obstruct all high-frequency communication between ground facilities and the *Black Keel* in high orbit.

Outside these specific orbital events, the sky remains bright and readable. The route-shadows are predictable orbital transitions, not permanent environmental darkness. Marauders must program their descent and salvage plans around these windows; arriving at a salvage site during a shadow window results in a complete telemetry blackout, disabling recovery systems and leaving the diving team stranded.

## 3. Electromagnetic Surges and Particle Scattering

The radiation sweeps from the inner moons create intense electromagnetic interference. Skarn's passage triggers charged-particle surges that overload unshielded surface antennas.

Vela's silicate dust scatters carrier signals, dirtying high-frequency channels. These periods of signal decay are called shutter events. They interrupt the automated handshakes between HECTON-8's surface stations and orbital relays, causing severe data packet loss. During a shutter event, emergency transponders fail, and guidance systems suffer from severe lag.

## 4. Relay Interruption and Handshake Constraints

Deep Reach operations rely on automated packet relays that transit between HECTON-8 and Luyten Junction. Because no instantaneous communication exists, data packets must be physically carried by automated cargo runners.

The *Black Keel* acts as the primary in-system depot, collecting telemetry and cargo before entering the transfer orbit. If a Marauder fails to authenticate their payload hash before the *Black Keel* exits the transfer window, the cargo is legally classified as abandoned. The delay between Sol Core financial databases and Aegir operations means that a Marauder's debt ledger updates weeks after a transaction, forcing crews to operate under stale financial data and constant contract pressure.
