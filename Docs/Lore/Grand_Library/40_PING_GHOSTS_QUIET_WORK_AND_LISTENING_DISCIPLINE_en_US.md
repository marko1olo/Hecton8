# PING GHOSTS, QUIET WORK AND LISTENING DISCIPLINE

> **Source:** Deep Reach acoustic navigation primer, Black Keel receiver-recovery policy, Marauder suit-audio field notes.  
> **Speaker:** Navigation acoustics instructor, partially overwritten by salvage practice.  
> **Reviewer Note:** HECTON-8 is not silent. It is full of things that have learned to make the player decide whether sound is evidence, bait or memory.

---

## 1. Sound Travels Differently Under Debt

In air, a shout belongs to the person who made it. Under HECTON-8's water, a sound becomes public before it becomes useful.

Pressure carries noise through structure, silt, cable, bone and suit frame. A dropped tool can speak through three rooms. A pump start can announce itself to a creature that has no concept of pumps but understands rhythm. A receiver ping can return from a wall, a wreck plate, a living shell or a route that collapsed after the old map was printed. Sound is not a straight line here. It is a negotiation with density.

Deep Reach built acoustic systems because light fails quickly and radio becomes local rumor. Pings, chirps, sweeps, coded knocks, tether hum and receiver handshakes formed a second map over the colony. That map was never clean. It was useful, which is a different virtue.

Marauders inherit the mess. They do not ask only "did it answer?" They ask what else heard the question.

## 2. Ping Ghosts

A ping ghost is an answer from a thing that is no longer what the system thinks it is.

Old receivers keep identifiers after their mounts twist, flood or shear away. A navigation node can answer from under silt where no route remains. A dead drone can repeat a handshake because its charging cradle still believes in it. A collapsed corridor can throw back the shape of a passage if enough metal is folded into the right lie. Some ghosts are harmless. Some are expensive. A few are hungry by association.

Deep Reach called these acoustic artifacts. Black Keel calls them verification risks. Marauders call them ghosts because the word makes people careful without pretending the phenomenon is mystical. A ghost is not a spirit. It is stale certainty with a return time.

The player learns that an answer is not proof of presence. It is proof that something answered.

## 3. Quiet Work

Quiet work is not stealth for romance. It is respect for unknown listeners.

Workers learn which sounds belong to tools and which sounds belong to mistakes. A careful cutter has a pitch. A rushed cutter has teeth. A suit fan cycling normally becomes part of the body, but a fan fighting salt has a wet impatience to it. Metal under stress sings before it fails. Living fauna often stops making the sound the player had gotten used to hearing.

Marauders edit their work around this. They pad clamps. They tape loose hooks. They avoid unnecessary speech in narrow wrecks. They kill confirmation chirps when the route ahead has too many listening surfaces. They leave some clicks alive because silence can make a nervous person invent worse signals.

Quiet work does not mean no sound. It means every sound should have a reason.

## 4. Listening As Evidence

Good listening has legal value.

A recording can prove a pump was running before entry, that a door locked after a pressure dip, that fauna occupied a route before a crew disturbed it, that a distress beacon was alive when Black Keel claimed it was dead. Sound records do not replace eyes, but eyes miss timing. Sound is often the only witness to sequence.

This makes listening dangerous. A crew that records too much may record its own trespass. A player who preserves a receiver log may preserve proof against themselves. A muted alarm can save focus and destroy a claim. A private suit edit can look like tampering if the wrong inspection officer wants it to.

The ocean does not care about chain of custody. Buyers do. Courts do. Black Keel does when it is convenient, which is often enough to matter.

## 5. What The Player Learns

The player learns that sound is a tool with witnesses attached.

A ping can map a void, wake a route, startle fauna, prove a signal, expose the player or waste breath. A silent approach can preserve a claim or miss the one warning the room was still able to give. A recovered receiver can become navigation, evidence, bait, blackmail or comfort.

The best Marauder is not the quietest. The best Marauder knows which noises belong to survival and which noises only belong to ego. They know when to send a question into the dark and when the dark has already answered enough.

On HECTON-8, listening is not passive. It is one of the ways the player changes the world without touching it.
