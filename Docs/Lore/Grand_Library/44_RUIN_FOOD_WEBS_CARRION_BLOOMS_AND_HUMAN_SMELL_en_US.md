# RUIN FOOD WEBS, CARRION BLOOMS AND HUMAN SMELL

> **Source:** Atlas biology fragments, Marauder route notes, Deep Reach leak-ecology margins and Black Keel contamination briefings on organic trace transfer.  
> **Scope:** How abandoned human rooms become feeding sites, how carrion blooms move through infrastructure, and why a sealed suit still changes the water.  
> **Field use:** Read before entering wet ruins where the route looks quiet, recently cleared or too clean.

---

## 1. A Room Starts Feeding

A human room does not die all at once. It becomes uneven.

Power fails by wall, not all at once. One panel stays warm. Another goes cold. A pump stalls, restarts and pushes old water through a dry service throat. A pressure door opens by a handspan and gives smell a straight path. Paint flakes from a corner where salt got under the primer. Grease loosens. Cloth fiber swells. Dead microbes lift from a drain grille. Battery heat keeps one brace warmer than the silt around it.

HECTON-8 life follows those differences. It does not need a tragic room, a corpse in view or a human explanation. It needs warmth, organics, current, shelter, charge, soft mineral, metal taste or a path to more of the same.

The first arrivals are small. Filter mats spread over slow leaks. Pale grazers work mineral film from pipe seams. Krill collect where pump vibration shakes food loose. Shell-backed cleaners live in screw recesses and gasket lips. They can make a dead room look maintained because they remove the soft evidence before they touch the hard one.

By the time a Marauder reaches the hatch, the room may already have a feeding order.

## 2. What Infrastructure Feeds

Ruins feed more than predators.

A warm battery wall feeds grazers. A cracked algae tray feeds a bloom. A cycling pump feeds krill by breaking film loose. A leaking medical recycler feeds white mats that later feed shell cleaners. A cable pass-through feeds iron kelp where current touches heat. A half-open locker feeds whatever can scrape salt from cloth and skin from the inside of a glove.

Large hunters arrive later. They do not begin the system; they collect from it. A trench stalker does not need to understand a door alarm. It only needs to learn that alarms are followed by movement, movement is followed by blood, and blood is followed by smaller animals that forget caution. A leviathan-class contact does not hunt a corridor because it knows the word corridor. It waits at the mouth of a repeated current that smells like trapped meat and hot copper.

Deep Reach filed this under contamination management. Atlas marked parts of it as maintenance ecology. Marauder notes use the shorter rule: if a place smells fed, do not assume you are first.

## 3. Carrion Blooms

Carrion blooms are not only made of bodies.

A bloom can start from dead crew, but also from nutrient paste, a ruptured algae tank, a spool of suit liner, freezer paste warmed for three hours, a medical recycler that cycled with the wrong valve open, or a tool pouch left wet long enough to grow a skin. The water does not sort the source into moral categories. It sorts by what can be eaten, carried, filtered, hidden or used as cover.

Most blooms move in stages. First comes clouding: water faintly milked near a seam or drain. Then sorting: small mouths, filter fans, white whiskers, soft film and moving specks that vanish when a lamp hits. Then work: grazed edges, polished screws, shell trails, bite pits, cable jackets cleaned down to the rubber. Then route use: larger animals using the bloom as a blind, pantry, mating station or current marker.

The loud stage is not always the dangerous one. A quiet bloom can mean the small life has already been eaten. A polished floor can mean cleaners have removed the warning trace. A warm-coin smell can mean blood is old enough to spread and fresh enough to matter. A doorway with no growth can be worse than one furred with growth because something may be keeping it clear.

Absence is a biological mark. Treat it like one.

## 4. Human Smell

Human smell is not one smell.

To HECTON-8 it is soap residue, synthetic cloth, battery heat, skin salt, stress sweat, adhesive, tool lubricant, plasticizer, sterilant, blood iron, heat-baked insulation, stale breath and the sharp chemistry of fear escaping through a bad cuff seal. A diver trusts the suit because it holds pressure. The water reads trace.

This is why some crews stop using fresh cleaning wipes before descent. The wipes pass Black Keel inspection and make the diver loud to the route. Other crews rub gloves in old silt before entering a feeding room. Some carry sacrificial protein tabs, not to tame predators, but to throw a smaller argument into the current before their own body becomes the larger one.

None of those habits are official procedure. Most are written in the margins of dead workers' route notes. Black Keel sells odor control as a consumable. Deep Reach called it trace discipline. Marauders call it not ringing the dinner bell.

## 5. When Clean Means Fed

A clean ruin is not automatically a safe ruin.

Clean screws can mean shell-backed cleaners. Clear cable jackets can mean grazers stripped the soft growth. Empty krill pockets can mean a predator passed through recently. A doorway with perfect visibility can mean the current is strong, or that a larger animal uses the opening often enough to keep the walls scraped.

Good route readers compare the wrong things. A dusty wall beside a polished latch. A cold floor below a warm hinge. New shell on an old blood path. No filter mats in a room with an active leak. A sealed cabinet with salt inside the handle cup. Those mismatches tell time better than a dead timestamp in an Atlas file.

The safest-looking chamber in a ruin is often the one that has already been cleaned by mouths.

## 6. Salvage Handling

Biology can ruin evidence before anyone lies about it.

Film grows inside a sample bag fast enough to hide the sediment the bag was opened for. A recorder polished by grazers loses the blood that proved who held it, and scavenger traffic moves small tagged items between rooms, so a find location is only as good as the hour it was logged. Under Keelmark form KM-CC-12 any of that reclassifies the load as unrecoverable, contaminated or disputed, and a reclassified load does not pay.

Good crews bag evidence before cleaning it. They photograph bite pits, shell growth, film edges and missing small life. They record smell, water temperature, pinger response, current direction and whether the object was found in water, silt, gut, bloom, shell growth or Atlas-tagged tissue. Those details look small until a clerk decides whether a dead worker was a person, a contamination source or a payable load category.

Do not wipe the trace record off the object before the claim system has a chance to misread it.

## 7. Field Rule

Before opening a wet ruin, ask what has been feeding there.

Then ask what fed on that, what stayed away, what changed temperature, what opened a current, what stopped making noise, and what your suit will add when the seal flexes.

If the answer is still worth the claim, enter like the room is already occupied.
