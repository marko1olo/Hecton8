# RUIN FOOD WEBS, CARRION BLOOMS AND HUMAN SMELL

> **Source:** Atlas biology fragments, Marauder route notes, Black Keel contamination briefings on organic trace transfer.  
> **Speaker:** Field ecologist assigned to salvage risk, with margin comments from workers who learned the same lessons by losing bait, blood, tools and friends.  
> **Reviewer Note:** Ruins in HECTON-8 should never feel biologically empty. A dead human space becomes a feeding event, a shelter, a chemical scar and a behavioral signal.

---

## 1. A Ruin Is A Feeding Event

A human room does not die all at once.

First the power goes uneven. Warm panels cool. Cold panels warm. Pumps cough, stall, restart and push old water through places that were dry for years. The room changes pressure. It vents smell. It sheds flakes of paint, grease, skin, cloth fiber, dead microbes, cooked insulation and metal dust. Something outside notices.

The first animals do not arrive because the room is dramatic. They arrive because gradients changed. Heat moved. Chemistry loosened. A gasket failed and gave the water a line of organics. A food printer spoiled behind a locked door. A body was removed badly. A battery bank warmed one wall longer than the next. HECTON-8 life does not need a story to investigate. It needs a difference.

This is why abandoned bases are not empty levels. They are delayed meals. Every hatch, duct and cable pass-through becomes a question asked by the ocean: is there warmth, salt, protein, current, shelter, metal, charge, light, rot or a path to more of the same?

By the time a Marauder arrives, the answer may already have teeth.

## 2. What Human Infrastructure Feeds

Human infrastructure feeds more than predators.

Filter mats feed on slow leaks. Pale grazers work mineral slime from pipe seams. Blind krill pack into service shafts where vibration breaks microbial films loose. Iron kelp anchors to warm bracing near current leaks and grinds softly enough that a tired diver may mistake it for machinery. Tiny shell-backed cleaners live in screw recesses, gasket lips and cracked ceramic. They make dead rooms look maintained from a distance, because they remove the wrong evidence first.

The large hunters come later. They do not begin the system; they cash it in. A trench stalker does not need to understand a door alarm. It only needs to learn that alarms are followed by movement, movement is followed by blood, and blood is followed by smaller animals that forget caution. A leviathan does not hunt a corridor because it knows what a corridor is. It hunts the mouth of a repeated current that smells like trapped meat and hot copper.

Deep Reach tried to write this as contamination management. Atlas wrote it as maintenance ecology. Workers wrote it in shorter words: if a place smells fed, do not assume you are first.

## 3. Carrion Blooms

Carrion blooms are not only about bodies.

A bloom can start from dead crew, yes. It can also start from a crate of nutrient paste, a ruptured algae tank, a spool of suit liner, a freezer that warmed for three hours, a medical recycler that cycled once with the wrong valve open, or a tool pouch left in a wet corner long enough to grow a skin. The ocean does not care which part was human, corporate, animal or manufactured. It cares what can be eaten and what can carry eating forward.

The bloom has stages. First comes clouding: water that looks faintly milked or dusty near a seam. Then comes sorting: small mouths, filter fans, white whiskers, soft films and moving specks that vanish when light hits. Then comes confidence: grazed edges, polished screws, shell trails, bite pits, cables cleared down to jacket material. Then comes ownership: larger animals using the bloom as a route, a blind, a mating place or a pantry.

The dangerous stage is not always the loud one. A quiet bloom can mean the small life has already been eaten. A polished floor can mean cleaners have been through. A smell like warm coins can mean blood is old but not forgotten. A doorway with no growth at all can be worse than a doorway with too much growth, because something is keeping it clear.

Marauders learn to read absence as carefully as abundance.

## 4. Human Smell

Human smell is not one smell.

To HECTON-8 it is soap residue, synthetic cloth, battery heat, skin salt, stress sweat, adhesive, tool lubricant, plasticizer, sterilant, blood iron, cooked insulation, stale breath and the sharp chemistry of fear leaking through bad seals. A diver thinks they are sealed because the suit holds pressure. The ocean disagrees. It reads trace.

This is why some crews stop using fresh cleaning wipes before a descent. The wipes make the diver safe for Black Keel inspection and loud to the water. Others rub gloves in old silt before entering a feeding room. Some crews carry sacrificial protein tabs, not to feed predators like pets, but to throw a smaller argument into the current before their own body becomes the larger one. None of these habits are officially recommended. Most are written in the margins of dead people’s route notes.

Black Keel sells odor control as a consumable. Deep Reach called it trace discipline. Marauders call it not ringing the dinner bell.

## 5. What The Player Learns

The player learns that ecology is not background.

Ruin life tells time. Fresh films say a leak is recent. Old shell trails say a route stayed wet. Cleaned bones say small animals had access before large ones. A corridor that suddenly lacks krill may be empty because it is safe, or empty because something made it safe for itself. A quiet machine room may be powered down, or it may be a listening blind.

Good play means asking what the ruin has been feeding, what has been feeding on it and what the player is about to add. Opening a hatch can start a bloom. Cutting a cable can release heat. Bleeding into a glove can change a route. Removing a filter mat can expose a current path that brings something larger. Leaving bait can save a life or teach the wrong animal a new door.

HECTON-8 does not separate salvage from biology. A recovered object, a broken seal, a dead worker and a warm wall all enter the same ledger of appetite. The ocean keeps accounts in mouths, films, trails and silence.
