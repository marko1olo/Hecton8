# QUARANTINE LOCKERS, SAMPLE CHAINS AND DIRTY GEAR

> **Source:** Black Keel intake rules, Quarantine Review Gate forms, Keelmark Mutual rental rider 9, and deck-3 maintenance log extracts. Working file for lot 44-Q-1188.  
> **Scope:** How recovered matter becomes evidence, debt, infection risk, auction value and locked property after it crosses the intake seam.  
> **Field use:** Read before opening a recovered container, wiping a tool, breaking a sample seal, or arguing with the gate over a locker holding your only cutter.

---

## 1. The Intake Seam

Black Keel receives wet objects with unknown biographies. The place that happens is a painted seam on the deck, 60 mm wide, repainted at every refit.

Below the seam an object is salvage. Above it the same object is mass, water, odour, liability and proof, and it acquires a number.

```text
INTAKE TALLY 2190-07-14 / DECK 3 / QUARANTINE REVIEW GATE
LOT......: 44-Q-1188
BEARER...: contractor, lien 4.8 t-window, solvency current
ITEMS....: 1 cutter head, wet; 1 memory core, wet; 1 pump housing with
           surface film; 3 sealed tubes; 1 glove, left, torn at the cuff
GROSS....: 31.4 kg
HOLD.....: pending, all items, 72 h minimum
FEE......: accruing from 09:41
```

Seventy-two hours is the minimum on the form. The form has no field for a maximum.

Salt hides in screw heads. Brine sweats out of insulation about an hour after the object warms. Biofilm sits under handles where a glove kept it wet, and pressure glass holds grit in every chipped edge. A sealed tube carries place, handoff, pressure history, route, witness, and whatever the person who closed it hoped nobody would ask about.

Deep Reach treated contamination as a category. Black Keel treats it as leverage. That surface film on item 3 can be logged as a biohazard, as a stabilising surface system with a named buyer, or as an auction premium, and which one it becomes depends on which desk reaches the lot first. Nothing about the object changes when it crosses the seam. The ruling changes, and the ruling is the part that gets stamped.

A crew member who climbs out alive has completed the first of three returns. Gear returns. Samples return. The claim returns in a state that can be priced. Clean aboard this ship is a decision with a signature under it, made by whoever holds the locker key.

## 2. What Comes Aboard

Every recovered object brings part of the moon with it, and the sheet has a column for that.

The cracked helmet in 44-Q-1188 came up with scratch direction running aft to fore across the visor, a salt band 14 mm above the seal ring, and hatch-dog grease on the crown. Those three facts together put the wearer through a partly seated hatch head-first, which contradicts the route the bearer declared at the seam. Heavier items carry more: mud from the floor that held them, metal dust from the cut, a heat history sitting in the surface film, an odour that outlasts the receipt by weeks. Sealed bags are the worst of the three, because the water inside one has touched the find, the glove, the knife, the deck plate, and the hands of whoever was breathing hard while they closed it.

That cargo pays. Dirt proves route. Stain proves contact. A living film can hold a dead seal shut long enough to matter, and a smear on a glove has closed cases that a claim tag opened wrongly. The same cargo gets into suit racks, sours food printers, loads air filters, seeds a sample freezer at minus 40, and turns one crew's shortcut into another crew's lung problem eight weeks later.

Experienced crews do not ask what a find is worth first. They ask what it does at 18 degrees and one atmosphere, because that is where the deck keeps it.

## 3. The Locker And Its Meter

A quarantine locker converts uncertainty into billable time.

The good ones are pressure-rated, drain-backed and sensor-lined: volatile sniffer, humidity trend, heat curve against a reference, a movement channel sensitive to 0.2 mm, and a surface-chemistry match against risk tables written in 2103. The cheap ones are boxes with confident labels. Eleven of the first kind are aboard and thirty-one of the second, and the hold notice printed for them is identical.

```text
HOLD NOTICE / LOCKER D-14 / LOT 44-Q-1188
BASIS.........: surface film, unclassified, item 3
CLASS PENDING.: trace / viable / harmonic (assay queued)
MINIMUM HOLD..: 72 h
FEE...........: 1.10 t-window equivalent per 24 h, from 09:41
RELEASE BY....: Quarantine Review Gate, two signatures
NOTE..........: items 1-7 share the locker. Release is by lot, not item.
```

`Release is by lot, not item` is the line crews argue with and never win. One unclassified film on one pump housing holds the cutter head, the memory core, all three tubes and the glove for as long as the assay queue takes, and the meter runs against the whole lot.

The logic is ugly and it is not stupid. A closed tender is a small volume of air with sixty people breathing it. Bad returns spread into filters, freezers, bunk vents, skin breaks and lungs, and a viable contaminant aboard becomes a second organism learning the plumbing at the ship's expense.

Crews mark the lockers the way prisoners mark walls. The inner face of D-14 carries fourteen scratched dates, a running fee total, the words `sensor 2 lies cold`, and a name with a line through it.

## 4. Sample Chains

A sample is worth what its chain survives. The chain is form QRG-4, two sides of one card.

```text
CHAIN OF CUSTODY QRG-4 / SAMPLE 44-S-1188-C
RECOVERED AT....: Sector 44, factory level 3, ledge east of pump house 6
DEPTH...........: 1,240 m        PRESSURE AT SEAL: 12.4 MPa
SEAL TIME.......: 03:11:40       SEAL TEMP.......: 4 C
TOOL............: scraper 44-T-77, certified residue-free 2190-07-02
GLOVE LOT.......: G-4419, 2 of pair, pair number recorded
WATER ADMITTED..: 1.5 mL est., ambient, from the ledge
WITNESS.........: second crew member, badge on file
CLAIM MARK NEAR.: yes, tag scratched through, not lifted
TRANSFER 1......: bearer to intake, 09:41, seam camera 3
TRANSFER 2......: intake to locker D-14, 09:58, two signatures
HOLD CLASS......: pending assay
```

Every field on that card is a place to lose a case. Buyers attack `WATER ADMITTED` first, because 1.5 mL taken at 12.4 MPa and 1.5 mL taken on the deck look identical in the tube and different in the pressure trace. `GLOVE LOT` is there because a glove from the wrong pair moves a sample into another crew's route. `SEAL TEMP` at 4 C is what lets an assay argue a film was dormant when it went in and active when it came out.

Weak chains are short. `Found in Sector 44` is a complete entry under the old Deep Reach schema and answers nothing: which ledge, before or after the pump cycle, above or below the brine layer at 1,480 m, clean tool or borrowed glove.

A preserved scrape puts another crew in the room first. Where a seal reads contaminated, a buyer's complaint dies before it is filed. One missing humidity tick is enough to show that a container was opened while somebody else held custody. That is how a 40 mL vial prices above a 300 kg machine: the machine is mass, and the vial can stand up at a hearing.

The smallest items are the dangerous ones to carry. Light enough to steal, precise enough to convict the carrier.

## 5. Dirty Gear As Debt

Rented gear comes back under conditions, and the conditions are priced per item.

```text
RENTAL RETURN SCHEDULE / KEELMARK MUTUAL RIDER 9
SUIT, Class-IV, returned outside clean spec.....: 0.40 t-window
CUTTER, residue uncertified.....................: 0.25 t-window
SAMPLE BAG, seal broken.........................: 0.15 t-window + hold
GLOVE, single, contaminated.....................: 0.05 t-window
DECONTAMINATION LABOUR..........................: 0.02 t-window per hour
BEARER DECEASED.................................: balance transfers to lien
```

The last line is the only one that has ever been challenged in arbitration. Marek Ibarra's desk won that hearing in 2151 on the wording of the rider, and the wording has not been touched since.

The schedule produces ugly behaviour on the deck. Crews wipe tools before handing them across. They hide a stain that would prove a route, because a route fee runs cheaper than a contamination fee. Clean gloves trade hand to hand at about 0.02 t-window, which is the decontamination rate and no accident. Most crews carry two pouches, one for objects that smell like a hearing and one for objects that smell like money, and some dirt stays on the seafloor on purpose, because bringing it up hands the gate a new handle and rider 9 a new line.

Dirt is also a record. A scratch pattern identifies a hatch. Salt in a boot seam will name a brine layer to within about 60 m of depth, and rust dust in a cutter tooth dates the cut to after flooding, because dry-cut steel does not carry it. One smear on a glove has put a body in a room whose own door log denied the entry.

The same dirt that fines a worker under rider 9 is what keeps that worker from ending up as an `asset reassignment` with no coordinate attached.

## 6. Evidence That Keeps Working

Recovered material does not stop changing when it is bagged, and QRG-4 has a reverse side for exactly that.

Warm brine separates inside about six hours. A dried film goes active again on breath moisture. Sealed tubes lose pressure through the septum and stain the label from the inside, which reads on inspection like tampering. Mineral crust grows across a sample edge at roughly 0.1 mm a week and buries what used to look like first contact. A tool with a living smear conducts once in the locker at 0.4 volts and never again in the hearing room. One dead invertebrate in a pouch has outweighed the part beside it, because that species only travels toward a lit lamp, which put a working lamp in a corridor the operator had sworn was dark.

The reverse of the card is eleven timed ticks: bag temperature, seal time, pressure, humidity, odour note, drip, audible movement, colour shift, who observed, who signed, who declined to sign. The Keel prefers evidence that sits still and this moon does not supply any, so the chains that hold up in arbitration are the ones that record movement instead of denying it.

Six of the eleven ticks on lot 44-Q-1188's card are blank. The card was stamped `chain intact` anyway.

## 7. When The Locker Lies

Quarantine fails without ever opening, and every failure below is on the deck-3 maintenance log.

A fouled volatile sniffer calls the whole box clean; D-9's read clean for nine days on a fault a bench test found in four minutes. Clogged drains make a dry locker sweat, and the sweat logs against the sample as humidity rise. Cheap seals let ship air into a tube and erase the pressure story, the one field a buyer cannot reconstruct. The heater step in the assay wakes the film it was meant to expose. A clerk shifting one bag for shelf space can break two chains in the time it takes to write down the wrong locker number. Crews buy fast releases, then spend the hour they bought scrubbing mould out of a bunk vent.

False clean does more damage than delay. False dirty is not harmless either: it holds a route proof until the buyer's window closes, keeps a repair tool locked while a habitat leaks, and lets fee accumulation transfer ownership of a find to the Keel without anybody having to decide to take it. A locker is a witness with drains, sensors, habits and an owner.

A quarantine label is a claim somebody else is making about your object. Sometimes the label is right. Sometimes it is the most expensive contaminant in the box.

## 8. Field Rule

Bag the object below the seam, while the water in the bag is still the water from the room.

Write the chain before the locker takes the lot. After that the card gets filled in by people who were not there.

At the hearing the question is which dirt puts you in the room, and which dirt hands the room to somebody else.

Lot 44-Q-1188 released on day 19 of a 72-hour minimum hold. The film assayed `trace`, the lowest class on the sheet, of no interest to any buyer on the manifest. The accumulated fee was 20.9 t-window equivalent against a 4.8 t-window lien. The cutter head came back with a Quarantine Review Gate certificate, a clean stamp, and one tooth ground flat by the decontamination cycle.
