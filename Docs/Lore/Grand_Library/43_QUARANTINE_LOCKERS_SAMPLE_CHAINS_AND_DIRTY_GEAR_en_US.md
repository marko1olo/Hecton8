# QUARANTINE LOCKERS, SAMPLE CHAINS AND DIRTY GEAR

> **Source:** Black Keel intake rules, Deep Reach contamination law, Marauder deck notes on gear that should not have come back aboard.  
> **Speaker:** Claim intake officer, corrected by contractors who have lost tools to quarantine more often than to predators.  
> **Reviewer Note:** Quarantine in HECTON-8 lore should feel physical. It is not paperwork floating above the game. It is a locked box, a timer, a bill, a smell and sometimes the only reason the ship does not become the next ruin.

---

## 1. Nothing Comes Back Clean

Black Keel does not receive treasure. It receives problems with mass.

Every object lifted from HECTON-8 brings part of the ocean with it. Salt hides in screw heads. Biofilm sits under handles. Brine sweats from insulation after the object warms. Pressure glass keeps microscopic grit in its chipped edge. A sealed sample carries more than material; it carries place, time, route, contact, custody and whatever the person who sealed it hoped the next person would not ask.

Deep Reach treated contamination as a category. Black Keel treats it as leverage. A dirty cutter can become a quarantine fee. A wet memory core can become evidence hold. A living film on a pump can become biohazard, stabilizing surface system or auction premium depending on which office sees profit first. The object does not change. The label does.

Marauders learn that returning alive is only the first return. Gear must return. Samples must return. Claims must return in a state someone can price. A person who climbs out of the ocean with the wrong dirt on the wrong glove may be safer than they were below, but they are not yet free.

On Black Keel, clean is not a condition. Clean is a ruling.

## 2. The Quarantine Locker

A quarantine locker is where the ship turns uncertainty into time.

The good lockers are pressure-rated, drain-backed, sensor-lined and mean. They sniff volatiles, test humidity rise, watch heat curves, listen for tiny movement and compare surface chemistry against old risk tables. The bad lockers are cheaper boxes with confident labels. Black Keel owns both kinds and charges as if every box is the good kind.

Crews hate the lockers because the lockers do not care about need. A repair seal can sit behind glass while a habitat leaks below. A personal knife can be held because it touched an unpriced film. A sample that would prove a claim can wait while an automated arm decides whether the container is sweating or merely ashamed. A suit glove can fail quarantine and trap an entire tool kit behind it.

The ship's logic is brutal but not stupid. A bad return can spread into air filters, food printers, suit racks, sample freezers and crew lungs. A living contaminant aboard a closed salvage tender is not an inconvenience. It is a second ocean learning the ship.

Marauders mark lockers the way prisoners mark walls: dates, odors, warnings, prices, which sensor lies, which hinge sticks, which technician has mercy and which clerk sells mercy back as a service.

## 3. Sample Chains

A sample is only valuable if its story survives.

Deep Reach loved sealed evidence tubes because they made the world look orderly. Tube, tag, timestamp, location, operator, witness, transfer. Black Keel kept the structure and removed the comfort. A sample chain is not there to protect truth. It is there to decide which truth can be billed.

A good sample chain says where the thing was found, who touched it, how it was sealed, what water entered the container, what pressure it survived, what sensor watched the handoff and which claim mark was near it. A bad chain says "found in Sector 44" and hopes nobody asks which part of the sector, before or after the pump cycle, above or below the brine layer, clean tool or borrowed glove.

Players can use sample chains as weapons. A preserved scrape can prove another crew entered first. A contaminated seal can ruin a buyer's complaint. A missing humidity tick can show that a container was opened after custody. A perfect chain can make a tiny vial worth more than a heavy machine because the vial can survive court.

This is why some of the smallest items on HECTON-8 are the most dangerous to carry.

## 4. Dirty Gear As Debt

Dirty gear changes what a person owes.

Black Keel rents tools with return conditions. A suit comes back clean, or the worker pays. A cutter comes back with certified residue, or the worker pays. A sample bag comes back unbroken, or the worker pays. If the worker dies, the debt does not become tragic. It becomes transferable.

The system creates ugly habits. Crews wipe tools before helping each other. They hide stains that might prove a route because a route fee is cheaper than a contamination fee. They trade clean gloves like currency. They keep one sacrificial pouch for things that smell like court and one trusted pouch for things that smell like money. They know which dirt can be sold, which dirt must be hidden and which dirt should be left on the seafloor because bringing it back gives Black Keel a new handle.

Dirty gear is also memory. A scratch pattern on a helmet can tell which hatch was used. Salt in a boot seam can identify a brine layer. Rust dust in a cutter tooth can prove a door was cut after flooding. A smear on a glove can connect a player to a room they never claimed to enter.

The same dirt that gets a worker fined can keep them from being erased.

## 5. What The Player Learns

The player learns that extraction is not the end of contact.

A thing brought back from HECTON-8 keeps acting. It warms, leaks, grows, stinks, conducts, proves, spoils and costs. A locker can protect the ship or hold a needed tool hostage. A sample can save a claim or incriminate the hand that sealed it. A clean return can be honest, faked, purchased or impossible.

Good play means deciding what deserves to come aboard, what should stay sealed below, what must be copied before quarantine and what dirt is worth the fee. Sometimes the right move is to bring back the dirty thing because its story is stronger than its risk. Sometimes the right move is to leave value behind because Black Keel would own the moment it crossed the intake line.

Quarantine is where the ocean stops being water and becomes administration with teeth. It teaches the player that every recovered object has a second dive after ascent: the dive through custody, price and proof.
