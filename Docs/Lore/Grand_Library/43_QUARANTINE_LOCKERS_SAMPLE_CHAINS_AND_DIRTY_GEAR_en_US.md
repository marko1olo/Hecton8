# QUARANTINE LOCKERS, SAMPLE CHAINS AND DIRTY GEAR

> **Source:** Black Keel intake rules, Deep Reach contamination law, Marauder deck notes on gear that should not have come back aboard.  
> **Scope:** How recovered matter becomes evidence, debt, infection risk, auction value and locked property after it crosses the intake line.  
> **Field use:** Read before opening a recovered container, wiping a tool, breaking a sample seal or arguing with quarantine over a locker that has your only cutter inside.

---

## 1. The Intake Line

Black Keel does not receive treasure. It receives wet objects with unknown biographies.

The intake line is a painted seam on the deck, but everyone treats it like a border crossing. Below that line, an object is salvage. Above it, the same object is mass, smell, water, liability and proof. Salt hides in screw heads. Brine sweats from insulation after the object warms. Biofilm sits under handles. Pressure glass holds grit in chipped edges. A sample tube carries not only material, but place, handoff, pressure history, route, witness and whatever the person who sealed it hoped nobody would ask.

Deep Reach treated contamination as a category. Black Keel treats it as leverage. A dirty cutter can become a fee. A wet memory core can become an evidence hold. A living film on a pump can become biohazard, stabilizing surface system or auction premium, depending on which office sees profit first. The object does not change when it crosses the line. The ruling does.

A crew member who climbs out of the ocean alive has completed only the first return. Gear must return. Samples must return. Claims must return in a state that can be priced. On Black Keel, clean is not a condition. Clean is a decision stamped by someone who owns the locker key.

## 2. What Comes Aboard

Every recovered thing brings part of HECTON-8 with it.

A cracked helmet brings scratch direction, salt band, old breath and whatever rubbed against the rim during ascent. A generator brings mud from the floor that held it, metal dust from the cut, heat history in its film and a smell that may outlast the receipt. A sealed bag brings water that touched the find, the glove, the knife, the deck and the inside of the person who panicked while sealing it.

That cargo can be useful. Dirt can prove route. Stain can prove contact. A living film can keep a dead seal from opening. A smear on a glove can say which room was entered when a claim mark says otherwise. The same cargo can also crawl into suit racks, sour food printers, foul air filters, seed a sample freezer or turn one crew's shortcut into another crew's lung problem.

This is why experienced crews do not ask whether a find is valuable first. They ask what it will do after it warms up.

## 3. The Quarantine Locker

A quarantine locker is where the ship turns uncertainty into time.

Good lockers are pressure-rated, drain-backed, sensor-lined and mean. They sniff volatiles, watch humidity rise, compare heat curves, listen for tiny movement and match surface chemistry against old risk tables. Bad lockers are cheaper boxes with confident labels. Black Keel owns both kinds and charges as if every box is the good kind.

Crews hate the lockers because the lockers do not care about need. A repair seal can sit behind glass while a habitat below leaks. A personal knife can be held because it touched an unpriced film. A proof sample can wait while an automated arm decides whether the container is sweating or only cooling unevenly. A suit glove can fail quarantine and trap the whole tool kit behind it.

The logic is brutal, not stupid. A closed tender is a small world. A bad return can spread into filters, sleepers, freezers, lockers, skin breaks and breath. One living contaminant aboard a salvage ship is not a nuisance. It is a second ocean trying to learn the plumbing.

Marauders mark lockers the way prisoners mark walls: dates, odors, fees, lies, which hinge sticks, which sensor overreacts, which technician has mercy and which clerk sells mercy as a service.

## 4. Sample Chains

A sample is only valuable if its story survives.

Deep Reach liked sealed tubes because they made disorder look orderly: tube, tag, timestamp, location, operator, witness, transfer. Black Keel kept the structure and removed the comfort. A sample chain is not built to protect truth. It is built to decide which truth can be billed.

A strong chain records where the thing was found, who touched it, what tool scraped it, what water entered the container, what pressure it crossed, which sensor watched the handoff and which claim mark was nearby. A weak chain says `found in Sector 44` and hopes nobody asks which ledge, before or after the pump cycle, above or below the brine layer, with a clean tool or a borrowed glove.

A preserved scrape can prove another crew entered first. A contaminated seal can ruin a buyer's complaint. A missing humidity tick can show a container was opened after custody. A perfect chain can make a small vial worth more than a heavy machine because the vial can survive court.

The smallest items on HECTON-8 are often the most dangerous to carry. They are light enough to steal and precise enough to condemn.

## 5. Dirty Gear As Debt

Dirty gear changes what a person owes.

Black Keel rents tools with return conditions. A suit comes back clean, or the worker pays. A cutter comes back with certified residue, or the worker pays. A sample bag comes back unbroken, or the worker pays. If the worker dies, the debt does not become tragic in the ledger. It becomes transferable.

The system creates ugly habits. Crews wipe tools before helping each other. They hide stains that might prove a route because a route fee is cheaper than a contamination fee. They trade clean gloves like currency. They keep one sacrificial pouch for things that smell like court and one trusted pouch for things that smell like money. They learn which dirt can be sold, which dirt must be hidden and which dirt should stay on the seafloor because bringing it back gives Black Keel a new handle.

Dirty gear is also memory. A scratch pattern on a helmet can identify a hatch. Salt in a boot seam can name a brine layer. Rust dust in a cutter tooth can prove a door was cut after flooding. A smear on a glove can connect a body to a room it never admitted entering.

The same dirt that fines a worker can keep that worker from being erased.

## 6. Evidence That Keeps Working

Recovered evidence does not freeze when it is bagged.

Warm brine separates. Film dries, then wakes when breathed on. A sealed tube shifts pressure and stains its own label from inside. A mineral crust grows across a sample edge and changes what looks like first contact. A tool with a living smear can conduct once in the locker and never again in the hearing room. A dead insect inside a pouch can become more useful than the part beside it because the insect proves which light was on.

Black Keel likes evidence that sits still. HECTON-8 rarely provides it. The best chains admit change instead of pretending nothing moved: bag temperature, seal time, pressure tick, humidity rise, odor, drip, sound, color shift, who noticed and who had reason not to notice.

If the evidence keeps working, the chain must keep speaking.

## 7. When The Locker Lies

Quarantine can fail without opening.

A sensor can foul and call everything clean. A drain can clog and make a dry box sweat. A cheap seal can leak ship air into a sample and erase the pressure story. A heater test can wake the thing it was meant to expose. A clerk can move one bag to save space and destroy two chains at once. A crew can bribe a fast release and spend the saved hour cleaning mold from a bunk vent.

False clean is worse than delay. False dirty is not harmless either. It can hold a route proof until the buyer leaves, make a repair tool unavailable during a leak, or give Black Keel enough excuse to own the find by fee accumulation. A locker is not truth. It is a witness with power, drains, sensors, habits and owners.

Treat a quarantine label as a claim someone else is making about your object. Sometimes the label is right. Sometimes it is the most expensive contaminant in the box.

## 8. Field Rule

Before the intake line, bag the object you have.

Before the locker, record the story you need.

Before the hearing, know which dirt proves you were there and which dirt lets someone else own the proof.

Nothing comes back clean. The question is who gets paid to call it dirty.
