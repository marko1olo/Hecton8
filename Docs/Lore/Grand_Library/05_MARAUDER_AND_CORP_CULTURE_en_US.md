## 1. Deep Reach: Structure of the Enemy
The *Deep Reach Extraterrestrial Development Combine* is not a cardboard villain in a black cape. It is a bureaucratic machine that kills people with spreadsheets. Their dirty work on Aegir is divided through a system of shell companies and departments so that no one bears personal responsibility for the corpses:

*   **Aegir Continuity Holdings:** The frontier shell company holding the paperwork for the planet HECTON-8.
*   **Atlas Continuity Office:** The legal department defending the phrasing in the Atlas-6 AI code, guaranteeing the continuity of isotope extraction.
*   **Keelmark Loss Desk:** The insurance department. Their job is to turn the names of dead workers into "load categories and financial risks."
*   **Recovery Compliance Office:** They are the ones who hire us, the marauders, in 2190. They coordinate the orbital tender *Black Keel*, demand proof of xenon presence, and ensure contractor silence.

The horror of Deep Reach isn't that they have a secret throne room. The horror is that every document they produce is technically flawless and legally justified, even while people are suffocating behind a locked door.

## 2. Marauder Culture
We are not romantic space pirates from adventure novels. The word "Marauders" was originally a corporate insult for independent crews who cracked open abandoned modules before insurance inspectors arrived. The crews kept the word because it frightened the right people.

A marauder is a salvager, a contract-breaker, a bankrupt pilot, or a rogue welder who has learned: "a rescue clause in a corporate contract is just another way to own your corpse."

## 3. The Three Main Ledgers
The economy of marauder crews is built on three concepts: **Air, Debt, and The Dead**.

*   **Air:** Counted in minutes, partial pressure, CO2 scrubber heat, and the ugly silence that follows a broken valve.
*   **Debt:** Counted in tool wear, battery cycles, favors, and names crossed off Deep Reach's insurance lists. A marauder's starting lien is 4.8 tonnes (orbital lift mass) equivalent, which must be worked off before you are allowed up on the Black Keel.
*   **The Dead:** A missing worker is not a trophy until someone proves whether they were abandoned, reassigned, "dissolved into biomass accounting," or simply locked behind a door the corporation still calls "secure." Marauders do not romanticize this work. They inventory it.

## 4. The Language of Survival
Inside a flooded complex, rank belongs to competence. A cutter slices a door only after the scout marks the return route. A technician can overrule a pilot when the gauges lie. A green marauder earns trust not through heroism, but by reporting minor failures before they become disasters: frost blooming around a hatch, a voice echoing on a dead channel, a school of fish compressing against the current.

The corpos write: "temporary atmospheric variance." A marauder writes: "CO2 eats judgment first."
The corpos write: "asset containment priority." A marauder writes: "they saved the xenon tank and vented the living quarters."

A marauder's honor is not clean morality. It is a repeatable, fail-safe procedure under lethal pressure. We steal from the corporation and collect debts from the dead. That is the only boundary keeping marauders from turning into corpos with cheaper suits.
