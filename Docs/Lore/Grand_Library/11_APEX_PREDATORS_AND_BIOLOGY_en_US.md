# APEX PREDATORS AND ABYSSAL BIOLOGY

> **Source:** Black Keel biological risk guide, Deep Reach hydro-acoustic survey H8-44, Atlas biology fragments, and Marauder route notes recovered from Cable Reef lockers.  
> **Scope:** How large HECTON-8 predators fit the food web, what human work changes, and why a salvage route can become bait before a diver sees teeth.  
> **Reader note:** This is not a trophy file or a kill manual. If a large contact has become a clean target, the route has already failed.

---

## 1. Food Before Teeth

HECTON-8 does not make predators from darkness alone.

The first rule is food. Ran light feeds the photic shelf. Vent heat feeds mineral films and tube colonies. Brine faults sort dead matter into slow rivers. Wrecks and drowned workrooms add another layer: grease, battery heat, cooked insulation, skin salt, freezer paste, dead algae trays, warm copper, torn suit fiber and pressure chemistry spilled through bad seals.

Small life arrives first. Filter mats open on slow leaks. Pale grazers polish screws clean. Krill pack into service shafts where pump vibration breaks microbial film loose. Shell-backed cleaners live in gasket lips and cracked ceramic. From a distance those rooms can look maintained. Up close they are being eaten in the wrong order.

Large hunters arrive when the smaller system has already told them where to wait. A route predator is usually cashing in a food event that started before the Marauder entered the water. The mistake is treating the animal as the beginning of the danger. The danger began when a room started feeding.

## 2. What Human Work Feeds

Human machinery changes the food web every time it fails.

A cycling pump throws warm water into a cold seam. A bad battery wall holds heat long enough to collect grazers. A pressure door opens and gives scent a straight line. A cutter frees metal dust, blood iron, insulation sourness and tool lubricant in the same cloud. A pinger announces a return path to machines, crew and anything that has learned that pings precede motion.

Deep Reach called these events contamination. Atlas marked them as maintenance opportunities. Marauders use shorter language: a fed room has company.

That company is not always visible. It may be a missing krill cloud. It may be a polished floor where cleaners have stripped every soft trace. It may be iron kelp grinding against a live cable and hiding fin movement behind the noise. It may be a bright clearing made by grazers, useful for seeing the route and dangerous because something else has already learned to watch the edge.

## 3. Route Predators

Marauder notes use names like `trench stalker` because a field name is faster than a taxonomy dispute.

The reliable behavior is not a neat species card. These hunters use vibration, pressure change, bioelectric noise, chemical trails and repeated machinery rhythm. In the Cable Reef they ride pump hum and cable chatter. In the Brine Canyons they use density layers as cover; a diver can be close to an animal and still get a false range because the brine answers first. In the Machine Fields they move through gantry shadows, old service throats and oxygen pockets that also look like safe stops.

They do not need to understand a suit. They learn patterns. Lamp flare means prey scatter. Cutter heat means injured material and moving hands. A pinger means a body plans to return the same way. A full-powered suit can sound like a small machine with meat inside it.

The practical question is not "can this thing be killed?" The practical question is what the route taught it before it saw the diver.

## 4. Armor, Tools And Bad Confidence

Pressure armour stops tools it was never shaped against.

Plates, cartilage, mineralized shell and thick pressure tissue are survival hardware. They are not invitations to duel. A harpoon can glance. A cutter can burn enough to make a predator release, then fill the water with heat, metal taste and vibration. A charge that saves one doorway can advertise the next three. The tool that feels like a weapon is often a louder version of the bait.

Black Keel training does not tell Marauders to win fights. It tells them to keep a door, a pinger line, a pressure shadow, a quiet window and a dirty exit. Most living crews survive by interrupting contact, not by proving dominance. Break the cue. Break line of scent. Kill the light for five seconds if the animal is using it. Stop cutting. Let the machine noise cover withdrawal. Leave the room before the food event becomes a schedule.

Confidence kills faster than fear here. Fear checks the gauge. Confidence opens the same cabinet twice.

## 5. Leviathan-Class Contacts

`Leviathan-class` is a caution label, not a confirmed family tree.

The best records begin before any body is visible: sub-40 Hz pressure tremor, prey blackout, shell fields folding shut, hull stress alarms without impact, passive carrier in the chest, and sonar displays that lose the small world first. A close body may be less useful to record than the way every other living thing stopped making choices.

Deep Reach tried to file many of these contacts as seismic displacement. Some were probably geologic. Some were instrument error under pressure. Some were animals large enough that the water behaved like weather around them. The records worth keeping are the ones with physical agreement: bent gantry, crushed filter growth, route lights occluded in order, hydrophone overload, then silence in the prey band.

If a standard sonar screen draws a clean icon for a leviathan-class contact, the decision distance is already gone. The right response was earlier: lower sound, leave the repeated current, do not cross the mouth of a feeding route, and never treat a prey blackout as good luck.

## 6. Biology As Salvage Risk

Biology moves evidence.

A body tag travels in a cleaner's gut and leaves the animal two rooms from the worker it names. Shell sealant closes over a wall stamp before the room needs it read, and a carrion bloom makes a sealed route read occupied. Tagged animals carry Atlas sensors into passages no drone fits. Keelmark form KM-CC-12 ends a payout on three words: unrecoverable, contaminated, disputed. A predator dragging a payload into a bloom satisfies all three before any office decides.

This is why biological risk is also legal risk. A ruined room does not only threaten the diver. It threatens chain of custody. It changes what can be proved, what can be priced, what can be denied, and what Black Keel is willing to lift through an orbital window.

Good salvage crews bag evidence before cleaning it. They photograph bite pits. They record smell, temperature, small-life absence and pinger response. They mark whether a sample was found in water, silt, gut, bloom, shell growth or Atlas-tagged tissue. Those details sound small until a clerk decides whether a dead worker was a person, a contamination source or a payable load category.

## 7. Field Rule

Do not enter a room by asking what hunts here.

Ask what fed here. Ask what changed temperature. Ask what opened a current. Ask what stopped making noise. Ask what the suit is leaking into the water and what the route has already taught the animals.

Then decide whether the salvage target is still worth becoming part of the same food event.
