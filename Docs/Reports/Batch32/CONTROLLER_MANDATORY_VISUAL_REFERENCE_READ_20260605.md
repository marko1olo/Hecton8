## Visual Bar

First-20 route moment affected: product-face surface approach, shallow exploration, cockpit/visor readability, and early underwater route credibility. This removes ambiguity about the visual target for water, sky, terrain, coastline, flora, and UI work.

Surface target:

- The explicit hero bar is the `BEST ILLUST` image: bright island/coastline, readable whitewater, layered cliffs, dense alien vegetation, large gas giant, layered cloud mass, and base/vehicle scale cues.
- Surface is not a noir hiding zone. Surface water, sky, gas giant, coastline, and vegetation must be bright, readable, and premium.
- Previous-development cliff/sky/gas-giant references prove the intended direction existed, but they are not enough alone without richer vegetation, shoreline composition, foam, clouds, and material detail.

Shallows target:

- Clear cyan/teal water, visible surface caustics, sand shelves, arches, coral/flora clusters, fish/fauna scale, and strong silhouettes.
- Empty sand planes, flat terrain ramps, weak coral density, muddy green water, and hidden horizon lines fail the bar.

Middle-water target:

- Vehicle/cockpit/base scale cues must sit inside dense reef or canyon composition, not empty fog.
- Color remains legible: cyan water body, coral accents, readable foreground/background separation, particles only as atmosphere, not cover for missing art.

Deep target:

- Darkness is allowed only in depth, caves, storms, interiors, eclipse windows, and pressure events.
- Deep scenes still require bioluminescent cyan/magenta/yellow anchors, readable silhouettes, populated foregrounds, and navigation clarity.

UI/cockpit target:

- Diegetic UI must read as physical instrumentation or visor glass over the world, not a flat screen-space overlay.
- The middle-deep cockpit reference supports dense but readable HUD data: O2, power, health, depth, temperature, and threat/status framing without blocking route visibility.

## Rejection Criteria

- Surface/shallows hidden by darkness, fog, bloom, vignette, or crushed blacks.
- Water that lacks readable color depth, foam/whitewater where waves hit coast, caustic response in shallows, and material separation.
- Gas giant or sky reduced to a dull backdrop without scale, cloud layering, or horizon composition.
- Terrain that looks like bare MapMagic noise without erosion, cliff strata, wet rock response, scatter, and traversal silhouettes.
- Flora/coral that is sparse, disconnected, floating, cardboard-like, hard-popping, or LOD0-only beyond 20 m.
- Deep water that is only black/blue fog without bioluminescent anchors, silhouettes, and foreground dressing.
- UI that uses screen-space overlay style for gameplay interaction, unreadable small text, or opaque panels that hide route decisions.

## Scalability Consequences

- Low/compact: use authored composition, baked AO, texture arrays/channel packing, dithered foliage, impostors/HLOD, cheap fog/LUT haze, fake caustics, and restrained post. Visual floor still requires bright surface, readable water, coherent sky, and recognizable flora/coral silhouettes.
- Middle: keep the low-tier composition and buy stronger caustics, denser near-field dressing, longer LOD residency, better wet rock/foam response, and cleaner cockpit/visor readability.
- High: spend saved frame time on richer shoreline foam, layered sky/gas-giant materials, denser reef/flora pages, higher detail normals, better reflection/probe coverage, and stronger biome silhouettes.
- Ultra: visual overkill lane keeps gameplay truth unchanged but extends density, draw distance, material layering, volumetric/biolum cues, and cockpit glass/panel fidelity.
