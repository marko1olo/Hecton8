## Prime Law

Celestial systems are macro pressure clocks, not astronomy simulation.

HECTON-8 uses moons, tides, surface storms, seismic pulses, and light cycles to create route timing, visibility changes, predator behavior pressure, and environmental evidence. It rejects orbital realism that costs frame time without changing decisions, decorative sky cycles with no underwater consequence, and celestial claims that imply unsupported simulation.

## Surface And Celestial Visual Boundary

The sky, Aegir, moons, cloud decks, and surface relay light are spectacle and navigation grammar, not a dark/noir default. Surface celestial presentation must be bright enough to read silhouettes, weather, route timing, and scale.

Eclipse is a temporary route, communication, and lighting occlusion scalar. It may dim, color-shift, interrupt signals, and stage urgency, but it must not become a permanent black skybox or a standing dark-surface art direction.

Celestial visuals must use authored project textures, proven asset sources, or controlled offline generation with reviewable results. Muddy bands, random sine stripes, low-resolution blobs, and crayon-like moon textures are rejected. `GlobalQualityWeight` scales cloud depth, reflection, atmosphere, texture resolution, and secondary shafts; it does not scale the surface into ugliness.

Aegir visual lock: the current blue/purple/methane-rich direction is allowed when texture quality, cloud bands, atmospheric softness, scale, and route context are premium and believable. Do not reject it merely for being unusual. Reject it only when it looks muddy, low-resolution, procedurally scribbled, or disconnected from lighting/water route context.

Storm design remains open. Storms may create temporary danger, dimming, signal trouble, wave state, route timing, and spectacle, but normal surface art must not become permanently dark or ugly because storm/noir language exists.

## Truth Ownership

Celestial owns macro cycle phase, tide schedules, day/night relay inputs, seismic timing seeds, and cycle events. It does not own weather fields, water physics, AI cognition, rendering implementation, or mission state.

`atmosphere.md`, `water.md`, `world.md`, `ai.md`, `rendering.md`, and `audio.md` consume celestial summaries. They must not recompute celestial truth independently.

## Cycle Model

Required:

- deterministic phase representation;
- simple triangle/sine/LUT approximations before physical orbital math;
- fixed low cadence for macro updates;
- event summaries for tide/seismic/light changes;
- stable save/load phase;
- no per-frame Kepler or high-cost astronomy.

Celestial cycles should be felt through water level, turbidity, predator activity, surface relay light, station timing, and sonar/audio mood, not through decorative skybox trivia.

## Gameplay Use

Valid uses:

- tide opens/closes route windows;
- seismic pulse changes cave risk or silt;
- night/day relay alters distant visibility;
- moon phase shifts biolum/ecology behavior;
- surface storm affects depth turbidity and acoustic noise;
- archive/log timestamps align with macro cycles.

If the player cannot perceive or use the cycle, it is background lore only.

## GlobalQualityWeight Scaling

`GlobalQualityWeight` may scale celestial visual richness, diagnostic overlays, sky/surface relay detail, light shafts, biome response presentation, and optional cycle UI detail. It must not change phase truth, save identity, route authority, tide timing, or macro event ownership.

Compact keeps deterministic phase, readable route timing, simple lighting/fog relay, and low-cadence events. High tiers add richer surface/celestial presentation and environmental response.

## Production Packet

Any celestial, tide, day-night, moon, orbit, seismic, or macro-cycle implementation must declare:

- phase model and units;
- update cadence and owner phase;
- consumer list: water, atmosphere, AI, biome, lighting, audio, UI, or narrative;
- deterministic seed or calendar source;
- save/load behavior if phase persists;
- event window rules and hysteresis;
- Compact readability route when celestial visuals are invisible or low-cost;
- High/Ultra visual route for sky, surface relay, tides, shafts, or instrument display;
- profiler/GC proof when runtime macro-cycle code changes.

Macro time cannot be an invisible global variable. If it drives a system, the owner and proof route must be named.

The packet must also state how the player notices the cycle when sky visibility is absent: instrument readout, tide behavior, acoustic noise, lighting relay, AI pressure, biome response, or mission evidence. A cycle with no player-readable consequence stays disabled.

## Proof Artifacts

Celestial work must provide:

- phase model;
- cadence;
- event list;
- consumer owner list;
- save/load phase proof if persistent;
- compact visibility or route impact capture;
- profiler proof if runtime cycle work changed;
- explicit premium-approximation rationale if physical astronomy is requested.

## Rejection Gates

Reject:

- expensive orbital simulation with no player decision;
- decorative sky cycle with no underwater effect;
- multiple systems owning phase separately;
- route timing without save/load phase proof;
- celestial visuals implying gameplay not implemented;
- per-frame macro-cycle math.

## Acceptance Sentence

Celestial systems are accepted only when macro phase is deterministic, low-cadence, saved where needed, consumed by named systems, perceptible to the player, and implemented through cheap fakes before expensive simulation.
