# HECTON-8 Cinematics, Cutscenes, And Directed Moments Bible

Status: AUTHORING LAW / STATIC DOC / RUNTIME PROOF NOT IMPLIED
Evidence class: STATIC_DOC
Scope: cutscenes, directed gameplay moments, camera blocking, trailer-like in-game shots, black-box replay, scripted sequences, capture truth, and cinematic proof gates.

## First-20 Route Hook

- First-20 moment: boot-to-world handoff, first exit, first hazard, death/faint, and first repaired route change may use directed moments only to reveal real playable truth.
- Route blocker removed: prevents opening-route scenes and captures from implying unbuilt mechanics, hiding missing assets, or removing control without creating a new survival decision.
- Proof class: STATIC_DOC only; route acceptance still requires truth labels, owner state proof, compact capture, control-loss timing, and public-claim review for external footage.

## Prime Law

Cinematics must intensify playable truth. They must not replace it.

HECTON-8 directed moments should feel like pressure, machinery, memory, and threat temporarily taking control of attention. They must not become fake trailers inside the game, long noninteractive lore walls, or spectacle that promises systems the player cannot touch.

## Truth Ownership

Cinematics owns blocking, timing, shot order, camera handoff, authored animation timing, black-screen/cut transitions, and capture labels. It does not own mission truth, damage truth, AI truth, player inventory, survival state, or world facts.

Directed moments consume owner state. If a shot implies a breach, creature, route, death, salvage, or system failure, that state must exist in the owning system or the moment is explicitly framed as memory, simulation, or black-box replay.

## Directed Moment Rules

Required:

- player-facing purpose;
- state owner list;
- entry condition;
- exit condition;
- skip/interrupt rule where applicable;
- camera comfort note;
- UI/audio/haptic coordination;
- truth label: gameplay, staged in-engine, replay, memory, dream, debug, or marketing capture.

No cinematic should remove control longer than the decision value it creates.

## Cutscene Design

Good HECTON-8 cutscenes:

- reveal evidence;
- show machine failure;
- mark irreversible route change;
- stage first encounter with partial truth;
- teach a tool through pressure;
- replay black-box data;
- transition between safety and threat.

Bad cutscenes:

- explain lore before player need;
- show monsters as glamour shots;
- hide low-quality assets with darkness;
- use camera shake as panic substitute;
- imply gameplay systems not implemented.

## GlobalQualityWeight Scaling

`GlobalQualityWeight` may scale capture lighting, secondary animation, VFX layering, camera material effects, crowd/creature background density, and optional replay diagnostics. It must not change event truth, mission state, save identity, or player consequence.

Compact keeps clear blocking, strong silhouettes, audio evidence, and short control loss. High tiers add richer lighting/VFX/material/camera polish after gameplay truth remains intact.

## Production Packet

Any cinematic, cutscene, trailer capture, or directed moment must declare:

- purpose: tutorial, transition, evidence reveal, death, arrival, disaster, extraction, or trailer-only capture;
- truth label: gameplay, scripted in-engine, debug capture, mockup, or non-public internal;
- state owner list for doors, AI, player control, camera, audio, UI, and save lock;
- entry, exit, skip, fail, and interrupt rules;
- maximum control-loss duration;
- black-box fields recorded before and after the moment;
- Compact capture proof if it can occur in gameplay;
- public-claim review through `textes.md` if any capture is used externally.

A beautiful shot that lies about playability, performance, or current build state is rejected.

## Proof Artifacts

Cinematic work must provide:

- sequence purpose and truth label;
- owner state list;
- entry/exit/interrupt rules;
- compact capture;
- normal/high capture if visual claim;
- shot list;
- player control loss duration;
- public/trailer claim review via `textes.md` if used outside internal build.

## Rejection Gates

Reject:

- cinematic implying unbuilt mechanics;
- long lore delivery without player need;
- control removal with no new decision;
- camera hiding weak assets;
- capture label omitted;
- public trailer shot without proof state;
- cutscene state mutating gameplay truth outside owners.

## Acceptance Sentence

Cinematics are accepted only when they reveal or intensify real system truth, preserve honest state ownership, keep control loss justified, label capture truth, and provide compact-readable proof.
