## Unity 6000 Evidence

Unity exposes the asynchronous upload pipeline through QualitySettings controls for buffer size, time slice, and persistent buffer behavior.

Official references:

## Law

[RULE] Async texture/mesh upload settings are part of the Scalability Matrix, not ad hoc project settings.

[REQ] Texture-streaming and upload-budget changes that affect player-visible surfaces inherit the Visual Reference Parity Gate. Streaming proof must include repeated shot-list comparison against mandatory references plus best-known internal baseline/current rejection matrix, not only hitch or memory numbers.

[RULE] Set upload budget during bootstrap after hardware tier resolution:
- Low / MX350: 64 MB upload buffer, 1 ms upload time slice.
- Mid: 128 MB upload buffer, 2 ms upload time slice.
- High / Ultra: 256 MB upload buffer, 4 ms upload time slice.

[RULE] `QualitySettings.asyncUploadPersistentBuffer` stays enabled during gameplay so Unity does not repeatedly allocate/release the upload ring under stream pressure.

[FORBID] Changing async upload buffer/time-slice every frame.

[FORBID] Treating a larger upload buffer as free performance. On 2 GB VRAM / 8 GB RAM machines, larger upload buffers compete with texture residency and OS pressure.

[FORBID] Accepting lower texture residency, blurry terrain/water/sky/materials, or delayed hero texture load as optimization success when captures fall below April/previously-in-development baseline. Declare `VISUAL_ROUTE_INVALID` or `PENDING VERIFICATION` until owner stack and streaming proof recover the visual floor.

## DOD

- Bootstrap writes buffer size, time slice, and persistent-buffer state once per tier decision.
- Texture import and Addressables groups do not bypass the global upload budget.
- PlayMode capture includes upload spikes, texture memory, and reserved memory.
- Player capture on MX350 proves no frame exceeds the streaming spike budget before marking VERIFIED.

## Scalability

Low: 64 MB / 1 ms. Prefer smaller streamed mips and visible fade-in over upload spikes.

Middle: 128 MB / 2 ms. Allows moderate streaming while protecting frame cadence.

High: 256 MB / 4 ms. Buys faster hero-area texture residency after the renderer has spare time.

Ultra: 256 MB / 4 ms unless a Player capture proves more headroom.

## Hardware Impact

Expected gain: lower texture upload hitches and fewer upload-buffer resize events on i3/MX350.
