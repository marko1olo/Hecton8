## 0. Prime Law

[RULE] AUP is the only simulation-scale spatial authority.
[RULE] AUP means `int64x3` sector/grid plus `float3` local offset. `Transform.position` is presentation only.
[RULE] Every rebase creates a 300-frame Sync-Fence. No system may claim post-shift correctness before the fence resolves.
[RULE] Millimeter quantization is mandatory at AUP commit boundaries. Float drift is not allowed to become save, physics, navigation, or render truth.

[FORBID] Reconstructing AUP truth from camera-relative `Vector3` after a rebase.
[FORBID] Saving or hashing world-space `Transform.position` as authority.
[FORBID] Interpolating gameplay authority across pre-shift and post-shift coordinates.
[FORBID] Accepting a stale shift generation id in physics, rendering, navigation, save, or telemetry.

## 1. AUP Commit Contract

Every AUP write must carry:

- `Sector` as signed 64-bit grid coordinates
- `LocalMillimeters` or equivalent millimeter-quantized local offset
- `ShiftFrameID`
- `SourceSystemID`
- `FiniteFlags`
- optional presentation-only camera-relative cache

[RULE] Quantize local offsets before persistence, telemetry hashing, native packet emission, and deterministic comparison.
[RULE] Quantization error budget is tiered and explicit.
[RULE] AUP conversion code must reject non-finite values before write and use a safe fallback.

Tier budgets:

| Tier | Commit Grid | Max Local Error | Probe Cadence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low/MX350 | 10 mm | 20 mm | 30 frames |
| Middle | 5 mm | 10 mm | 15 frames |
| High | 2 mm | 5 mm | 10 frames |
| Ultra | 1 mm | 2 mm | every frame |

Low tier is allowed to look smooth through presentation interpolation. Low tier is not allowed to corrupt authority.

## 2. 300-Frame Sync-Fence

On every origin shift:

1. Increment `ShiftFrameID`.
2. Write a Sync-Fence entry to the 300-frame Black Box ring.
3. Freeze gameplay-side physics integration for the shift boundary.
4. Rebuild CPU-side AUP-derived world caches.
5. Invalidate render matrices, navigation nodes, physics packet caches, and save-position snapshots carrying the old `ShiftFrameID`.
6. Resume simulation only after owner systems acknowledge the new shift id.
7. Keep fence telemetry alive for 300 frames.

Fence telemetry fields:

- `ShiftFrameID`
- `FenceFrameIndex`
- `CameraAupBefore`
- `CameraAupAfter`
- `ShiftVectorMillimeters`
- `PhysicsHash`
- `RenderHash`
- `NavigationHash`
- `SaveHash`
- `NonFiniteFlags`
- `MaxObservedErrorMillimeters`

[RULE] Frame 300 closes only if every registered probe is finite and under tier error budget.
[FORBID] Clearing the fence because the scene "looks fine".
[FORBID] Dropping the fence when the player changes scene. Dump or carry the unresolved state.

## 3. Drift Probe Law

[RULE] Every AUP-sensitive system owns a cheap drift probe.
[RULE] Probes compare authority AUP against the current presentation cache using squared distance or integer millimeter delta. No sqrt in probe hot paths.
[RULE] Probe output is a hash and max error, not a string log.

Required probes:

- physics body position
- render matrix position
- navigation node/sample position
- world streaming chunk key
- save snapshot position
- telemetry blackbox position

[FORBID] Probe allocation.
[FORBID] probe string formatting in hot paths.
[FORBID] relying on Unity inspector values as post-shift proof.

## 4. Failure Response

[RULE] Non-finite or over-budget AUP drift writes a telemetry error hash and safe fallback.
[RULE] Fatal AUP drift dumps follow root Black Box law: explicit batch/logging ID may use `Docs/AgentLogs/Dump_[ID].bin`; ordinary runtime uses a deterministic AUP/system-name/timestamp project artifact.
[RULE] The owning system disables the unsafe path and preserves gameplay safety before presentation polish.

Fallback priority:

1. last valid AUP for same entity and same shift id
2. owning sector origin plus safe local zero
3. quarantine entity and remove it from physics/render/navigation until repaired

No "unknown drift" reports are accepted.

## 5. Scalability

Low/MX350: probe every 30 frames, commit at 10 mm, and hide snap with presentation interpolation only.

Middle: probe every 15 frames, commit at 5 mm, and keep hash plus sample telemetry.

High: probe every 10 frames, commit at 2 mm, and retain render matrix samples.

Ultra: probe every frame, commit at 1 mm, and keep full 300-frame Sync-Fence debug payload.

Saved low-tier CPU time buys more stable visual transition and better audio/haptic rebase masking, not weaker authority.
